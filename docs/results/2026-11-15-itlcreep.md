## Verdict

**Not a server effect; the creep does not reproduce.** In 46 minutes of the soak2 traffic on the same engine binary, nothing server-side, and nothing client-side from either vantage point, climbed:

- **Server side flat.** The scheduler's own iteration interval (from `INTARSIA_CB_TRACE`, ms stamps) has p99 169 to 191 ms in every 4-minute bin, trend -0.1 +- 1.6 ms per 10 min. The engine's token-gap histogram shows 16.7 to 21.4 gaps above 150 ms per 1000 tokens in every bin; **all 10 gaps above 250 ms of the whole window fall in the first 2.5 minutes** (cold start), none after (soak2: 1 in 137,156). None above 500 ms. Memory (MemAvailable 43.6 to 45 GB after minute 10, device free memory within 0.5 GB), store (host tier full within the first 5 minutes, 4104 evictions by the end) and MTP acceptance (73.7 % in phase 1, 76.5 % in phase 2 by the engine's counters) behave as in soak2.
- **Box-local client flat.** The client on the box against localhost (the server's own SSE output) has a true per-token gap p99 of 176 to 187 ms in every bin; per replay trend -0.2 +- 0.2 ms (phase 1, 11 replays). TTFT sum per 48 requests: -0.42 +- 0.32 s per replay (soak2's Mac client: +0.39 +- 0.17).
- **Mac client, same minutes as the box client, equal.** Phase 2 ran worker 0's conversations from the Mac and worker 1's from the box at the same time on the same server: p99 of the gap 176 to 183 ms (Mac) against 177 to 186 ms (box) bin by bin; gaps above 150 ms 17.4 to 21.0 against 16.1 to 21.0 per 1000; no trend on either (Mac -0.2 +- 0.4 ms per replay). Mean TTFT 1.396 s (Mac) against 1.363 s (box), a constant +0.03 s.
- **The number that crept is a noisy estimator.** soak2's "client ITL p99" is localeval's `itl_ms_p99`: each chunk gap divided by a character-proportional token weight. The server sends one SSE chunk per token (0.994 chunks per completion token), so the plain gap is the exact per-token gap, and the weighted figure is not: per request it is **1.19 to 1.36 times the plain p99 at the median, up to 5.7 times, and above 1.5 times for 39 to 47 % of requests**, on the box over loopback as much as on the Mac. The replay-level median of that weighted figure wanders 180 to 372 ms on the box while the plain p99 sits at 176 to 187 ms. soak2's 237 to 333 ms "creep" is the same size as this noise.

What this does not settle: why the soak2 replays S70 to S78 happened to land high. I did not reproduce a Mac-side cause (the Mac path in this window showed 3 times as many near-zero gaps, 5.3 % against 1.7 %, i.e. chunk bunching, and pings with p99 67 ms, but no extra tail). The honest reading is: soak2's two drifts (client ITL p99 +96 ms between halves, client TTFT +0.39 s per replay) were measured by a client on a loaded Mac over a tunnel with an estimator whose own noise is of that size, and the server counters taken in the same soak do not support a server-side change. Details and the limits are in the sections below.

## Step 1: where the soak client ran

- `scripts/soak2/lib.sh` `replay2()` runs `cd /opt/code/deepeval && uv run python scripts/soak2/replay2.py --url http://spark-4e77:8191/v1 ...` from the machine that runs `fleetq run`, and `/opt/code/deepeval` does not exist on spark1. So the client ran on this Mac (endor), and only the server, the memory watcher and the 60 s sampler ran on spark1 (via `ssh`).
- The path from this Mac to the box: `spark-4e77` resolves to the Tailscale address 100.120.88.104 (`utun4`); `tailscale status` shows the peer as `direct 192.168.1.95:41641`, i.e. a WireGuard tunnel over the LAN with no relay. The Mac's default route is `en0`, the Wi-Fi hardware port (`networksetup -listallhardwareports`; macOS hides the SSID from `-getairportnetwork`, so "Wi-Fi" is read from the port name and the user's note of 40 to 45 MB/s). The Spark is on wired `enP7s7` at 1000 Mb/s.
- Measured at the start of this lane, idle: 8 pings Mac to spark1 gave min 3.8 ms, **avg 17.0 ms, max 50.9 ms, standard deviation 18.3 ms**, 0 % loss. A path with tens of milliseconds of jitter is on the same scale as the 150 to 450 ms ITL values at issue.

## Free check on the soak2 data (no new run)

`scripts/itlcreep/soak2_recheck.py` re-reads soak2's per-replay scrapes (`raw/soak2-recheck.md`, seeds S62 to S78 against the baseline scrape S61):

- The server's own submit-to-first-token sum per replay has a slope of +0.147 +- 0.123 s per replay; the client's is +0.389 +- 0.166. The difference (client minus server, i.e. what happens outside the engine: request upload, HTTP, tokenizer before submit, the path back) grows by **+0.242 +- 0.081 s per replay** (2.7 s at S62, 9.9 s at S78). The TTFT drift is therefore mostly outside the engine's scheduler.
- The server's mean token gap per replay *falls*: -0.064 +- 0.023 ms per replay (24.7 to 23.1 ms).
- The client's weighted ITL p99 median per replay correlates with the number of MTP spec steps (+0.73) and its p50 with the same (-0.82); S78, with the highest p99 (454 ms) and the lowest p50 (10.6 ms), has 465 spec steps against about 270 typical. More spec steps mean more time with one decoding sequence (batched MTP N=1 drafts only then), which delivers tokens in bursts; the client's weighted estimator reads bursts as large and small values at once. Time with fewer than two requests in flight and the client's dwell between a worker's requests (0.000 s) did not change, so the client was not slower to send the next turn.

## Method of the new run

One server (`serve --max-batch 2 --prefix-store on`, default 8 GiB host budget, MTP default on, `--mem-budget 40`, vLLM up beside it), binary built from the soak2 commit `ef31cff03`, `INTARSIA_CB_TRACE=1` the only addition (scheduler events with wall-clock ms stamps on stderr; nothing stored, no behaviour change). `scripts/itlcreep/window.sh` ran under fleetq #9381 (`--quiet gpu,cpu --mem 55`), a memory watch (kills at 10 GB; lowest MemAvailable 43.6 GB, never triggered) and the 30 s counter sampler:

- **Phase 1, 22 min:** the whole soak2 traffic (8 conversations x 6 turns, 8k to 33k tokens, K=2, half the conversations at T=1.0, fresh seeds 401 to 411) from a client on the box against `127.0.0.1`.
- **Phase 2, 20 min:** worker 0's conversations (seeds 501 to 510) from the Mac over the tunnel and worker 1's (seeds 601 to 610) from the box, at the same time. Each process loops its own seeds, so two streams are in flight at all times as in soak2, but turn t of the two workers is no longer in lockstep. The Mac and the box see the same server in the same minutes.
- The server was up 46 minutes in all (soak2: 38), so phase 2 covers the uptime range in which soak2's creep appeared (minutes 22 to 38).
- `scripts/itlcreep/replay3.py` is soak2's `replay2.py` plus the raw chunk arrival times (wall clock) and unweighted gap statistics per request (localeval's client wrapped, not copied); prompts, plan and timing are unchanged. Mac minus box clock offset: -0.167, -0.161, -0.222 s (ssh round trip 0.32 to 0.45 s), applied to the Mac marks.
- Quantities: **w99** localeval's weighted per-request ITL p99 (the soak2 number, median over requests); **g99** the plain gap between consecutive content chunks, p99 pooled over the replay or bin; **s99** the same after merging chunks closer than 3 ms; **g150** gaps above 150 ms per 1000 chunks; **srv** the scheduler's iteration interval, DecodePhase to the next DecodePhase while a sequence decodes (the server's side of the token gap: a prefill chunk sharing the iteration is included).

### The weighted estimator against the plain gap

| vantage | requests | chunks per token | w99 / g99 per request: median, p90, max | requests with w99 > 1.5 x g99 | chunk gaps < 3 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| box, phase 1 | 528 | 0.994 | 1.19, 4.20, 5.70 | 39 % | 1.0 % |
| box, phase 2 | 240 | 0.994 | 1.31, 4.44, 5.55 | 46 % | 1.7 % |
| Mac, phase 2 | 240 | 0.994 | 1.36, 4.27, 5.51 | 47 % | 5.3 % |

One SSE chunk per token, so a gap of 18 ms is a gap of one token; localeval divides each gap by `n_tokens x chars / total chars`, and a chunk carrying one character (a comma, a newline) after an ordinary gap reads several times too large. The worst request on the box (seed 604) has w99 99 ms against a plain p99 of 18 ms. This is a property of the estimator, not of the server or the network, and it is the dominant noise in the soak2 "creep".

### The Mac's path

- The Mac reaches `spark-4e77` over Tailscale (100.120.88.104 on `utun4`, `direct 192.168.1.95:41641`, no relay) via `en0`, the Wi-Fi port; the Spark is wired. Pings, 1 per second for the whole window: median 4.0 ms, p99 66.6 ms, max 273 ms, 4.9 % above 30 ms, 2 above 100 ms, 0 lost.
- The Mac's 1-minute load average during the window: min 1.3, median 15.1, max 51.0 (many agents run on this machine).
- Over that path the Mac saw the same gap tail as the box (above), but **chunk bunching** 3 times as common (5.3 % of gaps under 3 ms against 1.7 %; one replay, seed 510 in the last minute of phase 2, had 16.5 %, with no ping spike (max 12 ms that minute) and a low load). Bunching moves tokens within a pair of gaps and so changes w50 / w99 of the weighted estimator without changing the engine's rate. It is the one client-side effect I can measure, and it is small here.
- Client TTFT against the server's: phase 1 (box) 1.389 s against the server's 1.327 s; phase 2 (both clients) 1.379 s against 1.316 s; per replay, the Mac's mean TTFT is 0.033 s above the box's and does not grow. In soak2 the same difference grew from 2.7 s to 9.9 s per replay (sum over 48 requests) on the Mac. That growth did not recur.

## What this settles and what it does not

Settled (with the soak2 binary, 35B NVFP4 CUDA, this traffic, 46 min):
1. The soak2 client ran on the Mac over Tailscale / Wi-Fi (step 1).
2. The engine's time series (scheduler iteration interval, engine token-gap histogram, server TTFT, memory, store) is flat from minute 3 to minute 46 (the store fills in the first 5). The only gaps above 250 ms are in the first 2.5 minutes, in all 10 cases.
3. A client on the box sees a flat true per-token gap p99 of 177 to 187 ms; the Mac in the same minutes sees the same.
4. localeval's weighted per-request ITL p99 is not a gap percentile: it is inflated by up to 5.7 times, by 1.5 times or more for 4 in 10 requests, and its replay medians scatter by 180 to 370 ms on loopback, as much as soak2's "creep". Anything claimed from it needs the plain gap p99 beside it. Both numbers can be had from `replay3.py` (and the per-chunk marks are in the rows).

Not settled: what made soak2's S70 to S78 land high. Candidates that fit and that I did not (and here cannot) exclude: the estimator noise itself (the first-half to second-half difference of 96 ms is about 3 standard errors of a mean of nine replay medians when the replay median has a standard deviation of about 70 ms, a post-hoc look at one of two metrics), a bunching episode on the Mac path (S77 / S78's p50 of 16.3 and 10.6 ms against 17.9 normally is the signature, and one such episode appeared in this window at replay 510), and the Mac's own load. I did not see a server-side candidate; the ones in the brief were checked and none moved: store size (host tier full and evicting within the first 5 minutes, 4104 evictions, no change in any gap statistic), scheduler queue (iteration interval flat), MTP acceptance (soak2's per-replay acceptance, 73.6 to 82.7 %, has a slope of -0.01 +- 0.14 points per replay; this window 73.7 % then 76.5 %), CUDA graph cache (11 captures for 74,805 replays and 0 eager steps over the whole window, step time flat), fragmentation (device free memory flat within 0.5 GB after minute 10).

Recommendation for readers of soak2 and later soaks: report the plain chunk-gap p99 and the engine histogram, not `itl_ms_p99`, and run the client on the box (the box-local client costs nothing and removes the Mac and the tunnel). `scripts/itlcreep/replay3.py` does both.
