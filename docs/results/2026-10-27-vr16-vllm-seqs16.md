# vLLM `--max-num-seqs 16` vs IntarsiaML main, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4, one GB10 (lane vr16, 2026-10-27)

Lane vr16, 2026-10-27, intarsiaml. spark2 (spark-fa00, GB10), `nvidia/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4`. Ours: `origin/main` 8f93d2397 (includes the scc row-exact GEMVs, #208). Raw output, localeval run ids, both vLLM command lines: `2026-10-27-vr16-vllm-seqs16/raw/`; scripts: `scripts/vr16/`.

## Result

Why this run: the `spark2-vllm` service (role retro) runs `--max-num-seqs 2`, so its flat ~114 tok/s at 4 to 16 streams (scc doc, `2026-10-27-scc-cuda-35b-concurrency.md`) is a queue of two, not what vLLM does with room. Here the same image, model and flags run with only `--max-num-seqs 16`.

`localeval speed --sizes 1024 --gen 256 --reps 3 --concurrency 1,4,8,16 --thinking off`, 2 rounds, one fleetq window (#6369, `--takes-down retro --quiet gpu,cpu`). Aggregate tok/s is the median of the 2 rounds; TTFT p50 is the median of the rounds' burst medians; TTFT max is the median of the rounds' burst maxima (every stream of the burst is at most 16, so max is the slowest first token).

| streams | ours tok/s | vLLM seqs16 tok/s | ours / vLLM | ours TTFT p50 / max (s) | vLLM TTFT p50 / max (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 77.8 (77.5, 78.2) | 73.7 (73.7, 73.7) | **1.06x** | 0.19 / 0.19 | 0.19 / 0.19 |
| 4 | 163.9 (164.4, 163.5) | 174.2 (175.1, 173.3) | 0.94x | 0.50 / 0.78 | 0.63 / 0.63 |
| 8 | 212.3 (213.0, 211.6) | 249.6 (249.2, 250.1) | 0.85x | 0.88 / 1.60 | 1.11 / 1.11 |
| 16 | 259.2 (258.3, 260.1) | 340.1 (339.6, 340.7) | **0.76x** | 1.70 / 3.38 | 1.65 / 2.19 |

At 16 streams vLLM with room for 16 sequences is 1.31x ours (+81 tok/s). The earlier "ours 0.87x vLLM at 16" in the scoreboard used the old seqs16 record (295.6, below); the same-window figure is 0.76x. Ours leads only at one stream. vLLM's TTFT max equals its p50 at 4 and 8 streams (its streams get their first token together, probably a batched prefill, not checked); ours prefills the streams one after another per the scc profile, so the last stream waits longest, and ours is worse on max TTFT at 16.

Our numbers reproduce the scc doc (259.2 vs 258.1 at 16, 163.9 vs 159.9 at 4, 212.3 vs 208.6 at 8, 77.8 vs 78.0 at 1), so main still has the same serving path as the scc branch.

Quick reading of the gap, not measured here: the scc profile put 55.9 ms of a 16-row decode step into kernels (50.3 ms after the row-exact GEMVs), roughly 16 / 0.0503 = 318 tok/s of kernel time if the burst were all decode, against 259 served; vLLM reaches 340. The remaining distance is the serialized prefill of the burst (TTFT 3.4 s) plus step gaps. No profile was taken in this window.

## Record check

`/opt/code/model-benchmarks` record `20261023-125940-adhoc-vr-35b-vllm-seqs16-p2-conc` (`data/records/localeval.jsonl`, R22 in the scoreboard): yes, it already is a `--max-num-seqs 16` run, not the seqs-2 service. Its `engine.launch.max_num_seqs` is 16, the same image (vLLM 0.26.1rc1.dev602+g65b7662d3, `missioncntrl/vllm-openai:65b7662d`), spark2, `--max-model-len 131072`; it came from lane vr (`2026-10-23-vr-vllm-maxseqs16.md`, PR 118, fleetq #4561) with a `vr-vllm` container from the same launcher. Its aggregate tok/s: 69.6 / 161.4 / **295.6** at 1 / 4 / 16 streams (TTFT 0.18 / 0.61 / 1.62 s); the three repeats p1 to p3 span 292.7 to 295.6 at 16.

It differs from this run in the load: `--gen 128 --reps 1`, streams 1/4/16 only (no 8), and one round per record, against `--gen 256 --reps 3`, 1/4/8/16 and 2 rounds here. Same engine flags, so 340.1 (here) against 295.6 (then) at 16 streams is +15 %; the shorter 128-token generation puts a larger share of the burst in prefill, which most likely accounts for part of it, but that was not isolated. 4 streams agree (174.2 now, 161.4 then) within about 8 %; 1 stream is 73.7 now, 69.6 then.

## Caveats

- Order: both vLLM rounds ran back to back, then both rounds of ours (a second vLLM start would have cost about 5 min of the window). Not interleaved as in the scc windows; round-to-round spread is under 1 % for ours and vLLM at 16 streams, but a drift between the two halves is not excluded.
- 2 rounds, not 3, so medians are means of two.
- vLLM runs with the production KV pool (8 GiB, fp8) and `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.32`; a larger pool was not tried. At 16 streams of 1280 tokens the pool is not the limit.
- localeval sends one identical prompt to all streams (vLLM runs with prefix caching on), as in every scc number. Identical prompts also give identical MoE routing across streams; distinct prompts were not measured.
- Not done: other `--max-num-batched-tokens` or `--max-num-seqs 32` for vLLM; 4k/16k prompts; our own profile of the 16-stream gap.
