# m27 batch flip: why greedy batched ids of the Qwen3.8-27B on Metal depend on load, and the tail peel (2026-11-04)

Question: greedy batched decode of the 27B on Metal occasionally gave different ids for the same request on the same binary (`2026-11-03-m27-hs-rowtable.md` section 4). Harmless near-tie or a real defect?

**Answer.** Deterministic and composition-dependent, not a race and not stale state. A prefill pass with a body of 8 rows or fewer runs `matmul_mxfp4_gemv`, a longer one `matmul_mxfp4_nax`, and they give different bits. The chunk size is chosen by load (2048 when nothing is decoding at the request's first chunk, else 256), so a prompt whose length mod 256 is 2..9 gets different bits depending on what else runs. The size of the effect is **bf16 rounding amplified by the model, with neither path preferable**: no kernel is materially wrong (section 3). Branch `m27/metal-peel` sets `prefill_peel = 8` under `[tuning.metal]`, which removes the dependency. It changes solo numerics at exposed lengths, so it waits for a decision.

Raw data: `2026-11-04-m27-batch-flip/raw/`. Binaries: `intarsia.main` (`3c57d50a2`, same sha as the diagnosis binary at `19aa28788`, sha256 75fa583f...), `intarsia.peel` (this branch, c913e7e8...).

## 1. Cause

Real concurrent bursts at `--max-batch 2`, q4's queue position set by 0.15 s arrival gaps, outcome predicted before the run (#8322, `raw/window.txt`):

| q4 queue position | runs | chunk | chunks | ids |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 3 (idle engine) | 6 | 2048, decoding 0 | [772] | 6 of 6 give 557 at token 184 |
| 2, 4, 5 | 9 | 256, decoding 1 | [256, 256, 256, 4] | 9 of 9 equal the reference |

* Fixed schedules are bit-stable: 21 replay runs fall into exactly two classes by schedule, all top-20 logprob rows identical inside each (#8315).
* Row order, partner row and decode composition play no part: rows are bit-equal between solo decode and decode beside q0 or q3.
* Prefix reuse is ruled out: `cached` was 0 in every Admitted event of every trace.
* The 3 historical flip streams equal the C=2048 stream on all 200 ids. They have no trace, so that they got it through queue position 1 or 3 is an inference.
* Unforced bursts with the trace on showed no flip: 6 rounds (#8318) and 24 shuffled 4-request rounds (#8320), q4 at C=256 in all.

**Attribution.** Last pass of q4 set with `--busy-chunk 772 - t` (#8319, `raw/threshold.txt`): t = 4, 6, 9 differ from the one-pass run; t = 10, 17, 18, 33, 34 are bit-equal to it for all 200 rows. The boundary is body 8 / body 9 = `GEMV_MB`. With `INTARSIA_METAL_MXFP4_NAX=0` (all GEMV) solo and behind are bit-equal for q4, plen 260 and 1028 (#8321).

**Config-level cause.** `load.rs` sets busy chunk = solo chunk for a recurrent model for exactly this reason, but the recipe's `[tuning.metal] busy_chunk = 256` takes precedence, and the peel that makes this safe on HIP (`prefill_peel = 16`) was not carried over.

**Which lengths.** plen mod 256 in 2..9 where the solo tail has another residue: about 3 % of lengths (8 of 256, an estimate from residues, not a census). Tail 1 is safe (recurrent slots skip 1-row avoidance, the trace shows [256, 1], same body). Below one busy chunk, tail 10 or more, and long prompts whose solo tail has the same residue (2055, 4099) are bit-equal.

## 3. Why a few rows move by several nats, and which path is right

At other lengths single rows move by nats (plen 258 row 105: lp of 17 -0.03 vs -2.82; plen 1028 row 63: lp of 21 -0.61 vs -0.002). Three independent checks say this is the model's sensitivity to bf16 rounding, not a kernel.

**a. An unrelated implementation with no Metal and no GEMV / NAX split shows the same thing** (#8331, `tools/golden_quark_mxfp4.py` unchanged, fed the solo streams, W4A16, one full-sequence forward; `raw/cmp-hf.txt`). HF **bf16** against HF **f32**, scored like every engine path (median over 5 prompts of the per-prompt median and mean KL):

| path, against HF f32 | median KL | mean KL |
|---|---|---|
| HF bf16 | 2.0e-5 | 2.3e-3 |
| solo (NAX) | 2.2e-5 | 3.7e-3 |
| all GEMV | 2.2e-5 | 4.4e-3 |
| busy | 2.9e-5 | 7.0e-3 |
| tail 3 / 5 / 7 | 2.9e-5 / 4.4e-5 / 2.6e-5 | 2.5e-3 / 4.8e-3 / 3.9e-3 |
| f64 kernels, bf16 tensors | 2.6e-5 | 1.1e-2 |
| CPU f32 kernels | 3.3e-5 | 2.2e-2 |

Rows moved by more than 1 nat among the top 3 ids, against HF f32: 33 of 200 for HF bf16 against 32 for solo at plen 258; 32 against 31 at 260; 6 against 7 at 262; 0 against 0 at 772; 22 against 25 at 1028. So bf16 alone reproduces the count. Means are dominated by single rows, cover different row counts (a path's score stops where its stream leaves the solo stream) and have no consistent order: solo is near the worst at 258 and near the best at 260 and 262, and no path leads on every prompt.

**b. Paths scatter around the reference, row by row** (`raw/cmp-perturb.txt`). Plen 258 row 100, lp of 51544: HF f32 -0.089; solo -0.088, all GEMV -0.22, tail 7 -0.30, busy -0.54, tail 5 -0.85, tail 3 -1.28, f64 -0.39, one-pass -2.14. Plen 1028 row 63, lp of 21: HF f32 -0.108; busy -0.002, all GEMV -0.002, tail 7 -0.0006, tail 5 -0.47, solo -0.61, f64 -0.005, one-pass -0.95. The all-GEMV path agreeing with one side on one row and not on another (coordinator's question) is what scatter looks like: agreement moves from row to row.

**c. Precision alone, with no Metal.** CPU f32 kernels against CPU f64 kernels (both bf16 tensors, #8325, #8328): 10 and 28 rows moved over 1 nat at plen 258 and 260, first id difference at the same token 197 as the Metal paths.

**In the state after prefill, or growing in decode?** Decode is the same kernel in every path (1 row is always GEMV), so everything that differs starts in the prefill state. Row 0 shows no swing: top-3 lp difference 0.007 to 0.08 between solo and busy (1.0 at plen 1028 on low-probability ids, against HF f32). It then grows on some prompts (50-row medians, solo vs busy: 258: 0.031, 0.088, 1.80; 260: 0.038, 0.059, 0.25, 0.31; 265: 0.031, 0.11, 0.29, 0.30) and stays flat on others (772: 0.042, 0.065, 0.050, 0.031; 1540: 0.042, 0.047, 0.040, 0.038). Spikes are isolated and recover on the next rows, so this is a persistent small difference meeting occasional high-gain rows, not a blow-up. HF bf16 vs f32 grows the same way (260: 0.08, 0.14, 0.30, 0.53).

**Does recurrence do it?** Control on Qwen3-8B (attention layers only, no GatedDeltaNet), CPU f32 vs f64, plen 257 and 259, 200 rows (`raw/cmp-8b.txt`): mean KL 1.6e-5 and 3.3e-5 against 5.4e-3 and 3.3e-3 for the 27B, max KL 0.001 against 0.47 and 0.20, 50-row medians flat (0.054 to 0.091) against growing (0.06 to 0.48). This fits recurrence as the amplifier, **but it is two prompts of a different, smaller model, not a test that isolates the GatedDeltaNet**. A one-pass f64 forward differs from free-running f64 decode at plen 260 row 197 by 5.3 nats, so the amplification does not need a GEMV / NAX difference at all, only a bf16 difference somewhere.

**What would falsify this.** One path sitting outside the others against HF f32 on every prompt (a wrong kernel); HF bf16 being materially closer to HF f32 than the engine paths (an engine fault on top of rounding); multi-nat rows on the 27B surviving with f32 activations in the engine. The first two were tested and do not hold. The third could not be: the engine has no f32 activation mode and `--kv-dtype f32` has no Metal attention kernel. `kernels test` admits both kernels with 0 failures (max_abs 3.4e-3 GEMV, 2.0e-3 NAX; rel_l2 2.9e-5, 4.1e-5).

**Verdict: rounding amplified by the model, recurrence the likely amplifier, neither path preferable.** Not a kernel bug, and not "harmless" for the guarantee either: the first line of the contract in `scheduler.rs` ("A request's token stream must not depend on what else runs") does not hold at these lengths.

## 4. The change

`recipes/qwen3_5_vl.toml`, `[tuning.metal]`: `prefill_peel = 8`. Two tests pin the recipe and are updated. **8, not 16:** body 9, 16 and 32 rows are already bit-equal to a longer pass on Metal (t = 10, 17, 33 above), so 8 is the whole boundary. HIP's 16 is for its split-KV attention at 16 rows or fewer, and on Metal 16 would add an extra pass at twice the lengths for nothing.

The other option is busy chunk = solo chunk (`--busy-chunk 2048`, checked on q4 in #8315: solo bits). It leaves solo numerics alone but brings back the decoding-stream stall the recipe comment avoids (stall not measured here).

**Numerics move in one direction.** Busy-path bits never change. Solo runs change at exposed lengths only, onto the existing busy bits.

## 6. Speed

`localeval speed`, single stream, `--max-batch 1 --spec off`, `--gen 128 --reps 3`, one quiet slot (#8361), main / peel / main / peel, 6 reps per arm and size (`raw/speed-table.txt`, run ids `raw/localeval-runs.txt`). localeval's filler is seeded by its size, so sizes were chosen to land on exposed lengths; calibration moves the length by a few tokens, so exposed figures use only the reps that landed exposed. Plain 4096 and 16384 are controls: their lengths are not exposed, so the schedule is the same in both arms.

| size | prompt tokens | exposed reps main / peel | TTFT main -> peel | prefill tok/s | decode tok/s |

| 3861 | about 3845 | 6 / 5 | 4.805 -> 4.850 s (+0.045) | 800 -> 792 (-1.0 %) | 28.3 -> 28.2 |
| 4372 | about 4358 | 6 / 6 | 5.495 -> 5.580 s (+0.085) | 793 -> 781 (-1.4 %) | 28.1 -> 28.0 |
| 4096, control | about 4090 | none | 5.150 -> 5.065 s (-0.085) | 795 -> 807 (+1.6 %) | same |
| 16381 | about 16646 | 6 / 6 | 21.950 -> 21.960 s (+0.010) | 758 -> 758 (0.0 %) | 25.9 -> 25.9 |
| 16396 | about 16642 | 3 / 1 | 21.930 -> 21.810 s (-0.120) | 759 -> 763 (too few reps) | 25.6 -> 25.9 |

**Cost: not separable from noise.** The 4K exposed cells read 1.0 to 1.4 % slower, but the 4K control moved 1.6 % the other way with no schedule change, and localeval's own noise figure for long-prompt prefill is about 3 %. The 16K cell reads 0. Taken at face value the extra short pass costs at most about 0.09 s of TTFT on the 3 % of lengths it touches; other lengths run the same passes as before.

## 7. The 35B on Metal is exposed too (no recipe change)

Solo chunk 4096, busy chunk 2048, no peel, NVFP4 with the same `GEMV_MB = 8` split. Solo vs behind, trace confirms [2048, t] against one pass (#8359, `raw/cmp-35b.txt`): plen mod 2048 in 2..9 tested at 2050, 2052, 2056, 2057: all lose bit identity and all flip an id within 148 tokens (at 23, 25, 147, 24), max KL up to 2.6. Tail 10, 16, 52, 952 are bit-equal, 200 of 200. Exposed: about 0.2 % of lengths (8 of 4096, plen mod 4096 in 2050..2057). This contradicts the comment in `recipes/qwen3_5_moe.toml` that the 35B's prefill is chunk-invariant bitwise on Metal, which holds except at these tails. A 256-based peel on the 35B was not tested and would also split other lengths.
