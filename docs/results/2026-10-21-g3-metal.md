# G3 Metal round: 4k decode gate (clocks vs code), decode profile, levers

Lane G3 of the quota-burn plan (`docs/plans/2026-10-21-quota-burn-plan.md`). The host is endor (Apple M5 Max, 614 GB/s nominal) running Qwen3-8B BF16. The base is origin/main `4be7b7d4`; the work is on branch `perf/metal-g3`. Raw data is in [raw/2026-10-21-g3-metal/](raw/2026-10-21-g3-metal/), and all timed work ran in exclusive fleetq slots on endor (`--mem 24`).

## Headline

- **The 4k decode gate miss is caused by machine state, not code.**
  - A build that passed in 10-15 (`a2102bf`, 87.7 % of 40.6 at 4k on 10-15) and today's main decode the same, interleaved in one slot: 33.82 and 33.67 tok/s (−0.4 %, noise).
  - Within one binary, 4k decode tracks the GPU clock and the host CPU load across 16 runs:
    - r = +0.94 against the GPU MHz;
    - r = −0.93 against the CPU load;
    - r = −0.01 against the GPU temperature.
  - It runs from 31.8 tok/s (1141 MHz, CPU 0.60) to 34.9 tok/s (1364 MHz, CPU 0.12).
  - mlx-lm and llama.cpp fell by the same 5–8 % from 10-15 to today. intarsia's ratio to mlx-lm is unchanged (1.08–1.13× at 4k).
  - Today the clock sat at 1140–1300 MHz while a context-compression proxy kept about 4 cores busy.
- **The gate also has a definition problem.** "85 % of 40.6 tok/s" ignores the KV bytes. At 4k the byte floor is 39.0 tok/s, so the gate really asks for 88.6 % of what the bytes allow. A clock-normalised proposal is in section 2; it is the user's decision, and the gate has not been changed.
- **Decode profile:**
  - In the stream, a 4k step is 28.6 ms busy plus a 0.39 ms idle gap. The context-aware floor is 25.65 ms, so the step runs at 88–90 % of it.
  - The gap splits into three parts:
    - host wake and engine work: 0.145 ms;
    - host encode of the step's first 128-dispatch buffer: 0.115 ms;
    - commit to GPU start: 0.125 ms.
  - Attention at 4k and 16k, and the GEMVs, were already near the byte floor in the 10-15 profile; nothing changed since then (364 dispatches per step, same kernels).
- **Lever shipped:** `FIRST_COMMIT = 4`. After a `synchronize`, the first command buffer is committed after 4 dispatches instead of 128.
  - The step gap drops from 0.395 to 0.295 ms at 4k and from 0.372 to 0.281 ms at 128, consistent in all 6 + 3 runs.
  - That is −0.10 ms per step (about 0.35 %), below the noise of the tok/s figures.
  - It is bitwise: only where buffers are cut changes, and greedy ids were identical in every run (18 at 4k, 9 at 128). The footprint is Metal-only.
- **Levers measured and dropped:**
  - Exact skip of the flash-attention O rescale is 5–6 % *slower* in the kernel bench.
  - Spin-waiting on the last buffer saves nothing (the wake is engine work, not the OS) and costs a core.
- **Gates (rebased on main `7536a5e6`, fleetq #3047), all PASS:**
  - kernels test 27/321 admitted; Metal tests 115/0;
  - 256 greedy ids and both golden logs (21-way KL included) byte-identical to base;
  - `qualify` 11/11 stages PASS, including prefix reuse in acc-long and acc-4k;
  - all 4 mutants of the first-commit logic killed;
  - CPU: 405 tests passed, 0 failed; `cargo check` plain and `--features intarsia-cli/metal` clean.
- **A/B ×3 against base:** flat. 4k −0.2 %, 16k +0.1 %; 128 and 1k flat in rounds 2 and 3. Round 1 dev ran in a CPU-load transient (section 5).
- **Restated Metal speed gate (user, 12:25):** PASS on the dev build, with both intarsia arms admitted:
  - 88.3–90.4 % of the context-aware floor at every size;
  - 1.05–1.12× mlx-lm and 1.03–1.06× llama.cpp;
  - 4k at 35.0 tok/s, which is also 86.2 % of the old flat 40.6.

## 2. Clocks or code (fleetq #2909)

**Same slot, old build against new.** Plain `generate`, 4k prompt, 129 tokens, cb-mode trace, order old/new/new/old twice.
- Old is `a2102bf`, the 10-15 SAFE build that measured 35.63 tok/s (87.7 % of 40.6) at 4k on 10-15, rebuilt from source (md5 `7b29a0df`).
- Old: 33.77 / 33.80 / 33.67 / 34.03 tok/s, mean 33.82.
- New (main `4be7b7d4`): 33.75 / 33.29 / 34.19 / 33.44, mean 33.67.
- The step periods in the cb traces agree: old 28.99 ms, new 29.05–29.52 ms.
- Code has not regressed. The same old binary was 4 % faster on 10-15 than today.

**Clock study.** One binary, 4k prompt, 256 tokens, 8 plain runs plus the 8 A/B runs. Metrics are the macmon medians over each run's decode window (`p1/clock-table.md`, `clock.py`).
- Decode tok/s against GPU MHz: r = +0.94.
- Against CPU load: r = −0.93.
- Against GPU temperature: r = −0.01.
- Fit: tok/s = 33.66 + 0.0141 × (MHz − 1274). The 85 % gate (34.51 tok/s) is crossed at about 1335 MHz.
- The best run, at 1364 MHz, gave 34.92 tok/s, which is 86.0 % of 40.6 and passes.
- The GPU clock falls when other processes load the CPU (shared SoC power budget), not with temperature.

This settles the 10-14/15 claim that decode "hardly" moves with clock. On a contended machine a −11 % clock costs about 6 % decode, because achievable DRAM bandwidth falls with the GPU clock. In the 10-15 study the clock varied without CPU contention, and the effect there was small.

**Clock-normalised gate: proposed here, adopted by the user 2026-10-21 12:25 EDT ("1 yes").** It is implemented in `scripts/metal-speed-gate/` and `docs/benchmarking.md` "Metal speed gate", and passes on the dev build (section 5b).
1. **Floor:** measure decode against the *context-aware* byte floor (weights plus the KV read at the mean context; 39.0 tok/s at a 4k prompt with 256 generated tokens) instead of the flat 40.6. The 85 % threshold stays.
   - Under this floor, 4k passed on every day measured: 10-15 91 %, 10-18 88 %, today 86.7 % (main-2).
   - Today's main-1, at 1177 MHz with the CPU at 0.57, would still fail at 82.9 %.
2. **State admission:** an absolute decode-gate run counts only when, over its decode window, the median GPU clock is at least 1300 MHz and the macmon CPU load is at most 0.30. localeval records neither; today only this lane's macmon wrapper (`sameslot.sh`, `profile.sh`) and `clock.py` do, so adopting the gate needs macmon logging added to localeval or to the gate script.
   - A run outside that window is reported as state-invalid and re-run, not failed.
   - Of today's runs, main-2 at 1283 MHz would be state-invalid as well. That is honest: it was taken in a degraded state.
   - Only 5 of the 16 plain 4k runs fall inside the window. Those 5 gave 33.75–34.92 tok/s: 86.5–89.5 % of the context-aware floor (pass), and 83.1–86.0 % of 40.6 (mostly fail).
   - The always-on context-compression proxy keeps the CPU loaded, so on this machine as it is normally used the absolute gate may rarely be runnable. The same-slot ratios (point 3) still are.
3. **State-independent check:** keep the same-slot ratio gates (at least 90 % of mlx-lm, not below llama.cpp). They are clock-invariant, and today they show 1.08–1.14× mlx-lm and 1.03–1.11× llama.cpp at every size.

The doctor copy probe is not a usable denominator: it reads 143–307 GB/s (phase B). A GEMV-stream microbench beside each run would be a better bandwidth normaliser, but it was not built here.

## 3. Decode profile at 4k and 16k

Sources:
- cb-mode traces (`p1/cbgaps.txt`, `cbgap.py`): step period, GPU busy, and the idle gap split by the trace's host timestamps (`ho`/`hc`/`hr`).
- Kernel-mode traces at 4k and 16k (`p1/kern-*.jsonl.gz`, `kop.py`). These ran at a 338 MHz GPU clock, because kernel mode idles the GPU between dispatches. They are usable for op order and count only; the absolute per-op figures are not representative.

| context | period ms | GPU busy ms | idle gap ms | gap: wake / encode first buffer / commit→start | cf floor ms | busy % of cf |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 128 (e2 base, quiet) | 27.65 | 27.23 | 0.372 | 0.135 / 0.114 / 0.118 | 24.71 | 90.7 % |
| 4k (p1 ab6-new, quiet) | 29.05 | 28.63 | 0.393 | 0.145 / 0.113 / 0.128 | 25.65 | 89.6 % |
| 16k (p1 cb-16k, CPU 0.21) | 31.97 | 31.57 | 0.386 | 0.141 / 0.111 / 0.126 | 28.60 | 90.6 % |
| 4k under CPU load (p1 cb-4k, CPU 0.62) | 31.57 | 30.86 | 0.642 | 0.227 / 0.229 / 0.156 | 25.65 | 83.1 % |

Notes:
- **Attention vs GEMV share was not measured validly today.**
  - Kernel mode ran at 338 MHz.
  - The 16k − 4k busy difference (2.9 ms, against 2.95 ms of extra KV bytes at 614 GB/s) compares runs at different clock and CPU states, so it is consistent with the floor but is not a measurement of it.
  - The share therefore comes from the 10-15 longctx in-stream figures: attention 1.31 ms per step at 4k (36 × 36.3 µs, about 75 % of bandwidth) and 4.4 ms at 16k (550–575 GB/s); the GEMVs about 590 GB/s.
  - The step itself is unchanged since then: 364 dispatches, the same ops, 3 buffers per step.
- **Headroom per step at 4k.** About 3.0 ms per step separates busy time from the floor, made up of three parts:
  - GEMV efficiency (~590 GB/s in stream against 614);
  - about 0.75 ms of small ops (D1, 10-15: fusions flat);
  - about 0.3 ms of attention at 4k (75 % of bandwidth; the split policy is optimal, and changing splits changes bits).
- **The idle gap (0.39 ms, 1.3 %)** is the only bitwise-reachable part left. Under CPU contention the host side of it doubles.

## 4. Levers

| lever | exact? | result | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-commit 4** (`FIRST_COMMIT`, `INTARSIA_METAL_FIRST_COMMIT`): after a `synchronize`, commit the first buffer after 4 dispatches | yes, only buffer boundaries move | encode-first-buffer 0.115 → 0.018 ms; gap −0.10 ms per step (4k 0.395 → 0.295, 128 0.372 → 0.281; medians of 3; per-round deltas −0.08 to −0.18 ms, median −0.10); GPU busy unchanged (4k 28.62 → 28.45 ms, 128 27.23 → 27.26 ms), so the extra buffer per step costs no GPU time; greedy ids identical in every run (18 at 4k, 9 at 128) | **default on** |
| first-commit 16 | yes | gap 0.34 ms (encode 0.04–0.06) | 4 is better |
| spin-wait on the last buffer (`status()` poll instead of `waitUntilCompleted`) | yes | wake 0.147 → 0.140 ms (noise), gap unchanged (0.295 → 0.291), CPU load +0.07 | dropped; code removed |
| flash NAX attention: skip the O rescale when every lane's factor is exactly 1.0 (`simd_all`), new `_rs` kernels | yes by construction (x · 1.0f = x); greedy ids identical at 4k and 16k | `flash_bench` chunk 2048: pos0 0 46.6/45.7 vs 45.9/46.6 TFLOPS (flat), pos0 8192 39.5/39.9 vs 41.6/42.0 (**−5 %**), 14336 39.0/38.9 vs 41.3/41.6 (**−6 %**); in-engine plain prefill A/B at 4k/16k within noise (16k medians 6832 vs 6703 ms) | dropped; patch kept as `raw/.../flash-rescale-skip-experiment.patch` |
| q ∥ kv GEMV concurrency (concurrent encoder + targeted barriers) | yes | not built: the spike measured concurrent-dispatch barriers at about 3 % of decode, against at most about 0.5 % to gain from overlapping one pair per layer | not built |
| device-side token feedback (encode step N+1 before step N ends) | yes | removes the whole 0.39 ms gap (wake, encode and launch), but needs engine.rs changes (shared footprint) | proposal for the user |

Shelved ideas not retried, and why: KV quant failed KL (K error, fp8 KL 6–10e-3; only V-only int8 passed and was never built, since the quant kernels ran at 60–67 % of bandwidth); weight-realign is opt-in and shelved by the user; M3/M4 batching is a no-go (Q14/Q16).

The prefill side has no bitwise lever left on this profile:
- the NAX GEMMs run at 62–63 TFLOPS (about 97 % of peak);
- the SwiGLU and residual epilogues were measured negative or bounded under 1 % (P4/P5);
- the attention kernel's cheap exact change, above, is slower.

The remaining prefill ceiling is flash attention at 48–50 TFLOPS against 63 (−6 % of a 16k prefill at most). Reaching it would mean a kernel redesign that is not exact.

## 9. Decisions (answered by the user 2026-10-21 12:25 EDT, verbatim "1 yes, 2 yes")

1. **Gate:** the clock-normalised decode gate is adopted. Its three parts are in sections 2 and 5b and `docs/benchmarking.md`:
   - the context-aware floor;
   - admission at a GPU clock of at least 1300 MHz and CPU load of at most 0.30;
   - the same-slot ratio gates, kept.
2. **Device-side greedy token feedback** is approved. The coordinator runs it as its own new lane, not in G3. It touches `engine.rs`, which is shared code.
