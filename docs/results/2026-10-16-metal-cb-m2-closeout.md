An admitted request that has not started prefilling is in neither queue gauge. It is in both running gauges. (Prefill starts in the same iteration as the admission, so that state lasts for less than one iteration.)

Reading the soak2 burst: server `--max-batch 4 --max-queue 4`, the server admits 8 requests (`max_queue + max_running`) and the engine queue is sized to hold them (`max_queue + max_batch`, `crates/intarsia-models/src/load.rs:274`), so all 8 can sit in the hand-off channel and the FIFO together until the scheduler thread gets to them. `waiting` counted them as they arrived (peak 7). `queue_depth` was only updated by the busy scheduler thread, so a scrape during a step saw the value from the end of the last iteration (4 after the previous admission filled the slots). The server-side `queue_depth_max` of that run was 7 (`raw/2026-10-16-metal-cb-soak-batched/soak-b8-shed/metrics-end.txt`), equal to the `waiting` peak: the scheduler did hold 7 at the instant it drained. The harness' "queue 4" is the maximum of its sampled `queue_depth` values (`tools/cbsoak.py:1239`), i.e. a lagging gauge, not a different population.

**Decision: a small bug in the scheduler gauge, not two quantities.** Both gauges are defined as "accepted and not yet holding a slot", and they disagreed because one was refreshed only at iteration boundaries. Fix (8ce01d2): `BatchMetrics.queue_depth` is read from the engine's queue counter (`BatchEngine::queued`, which counts channel plus FIFO and drops at the same point as `request_started`) when the snapshot is taken; `queue_depth_max` is taken at each submit; the bound check is a compare-and-swap, so a request that is refused is never counted (the live depth cannot exceed the bound). Test: `queue_depth_is_live_during_a_long_step` (`tests/scheduler.rs:1588`) blocks the model inside a prefill chunk, submits five requests, and asserts the gauge reads 1, 2, .. 5 after each submit, that a sixth is refused and not counted, and the maximum; it fails on the old code ("gauge lags behind the 1 submits"). `Telemetry.waiting` is unchanged (it is shared with the single-request engine), except that its help text now says: counted from `Engine::submit`, includes channel and queue, and a request about to be refused is counted until the refusal.

Rerun of the bursts on the fixed binary (`raw/2026-10-16-metal-cb-m2-closeout/perf/burst`, `--max-batch 4 --max-queue 4`, 16 and 24 simultaneous requests): sampled peaks first burst queue 4 / waiting 4, second burst **queue 6 / waiting 6** (before: 4 / 7); 8 + 16 requests got 429 with `Retry-After: 1`, `intarsia_requests_rejected_total` delta equal, 0 x 503, follow-ups ok; server `queue_depth_max` 7. The scrape interval still misses the instantaneous peak of 7 (6 sampled), but the two gauges now move together.

Help texts now (`crates/intarsia-server/src/metrics.rs`, `docs/telemetry.md`, `docs/observability.md`): `intarsia_requests_running`: requests holding the engine, with `--max-batch N` those that got a slot, prefilling or decoding; `intarsia_requests_waiting`: accepted and not yet started, from submit, channel plus queue; `intarsia_batch_running`: holding a slot as of the end of the last iteration; `intarsia_batch_queue_depth`: accepted, no slot yet, live; the `vllm:` names say they equal the `intarsia_` ones.

## 3. Budget and EWMA: why the fixed-chunk, decode-first policy meets M2

**What exists.** The prefill token budget per iteration is a constant, `busy_chunk x busy_chunks_per_iter = 256 x 1` tokens while any sequence decodes; `step` runs the decode phase first and then at most that many chunks for the oldest prefilling request (`scheduler.rs:799-816`). When nothing decodes the budget is one solo chunk of 2048 (today's speed). The budget never splits a chunk. The EWMA is an observed value (`intarsia_batch_step_ewma_ms`) that feeds nothing.

**Why no EWMA-driven budget.** The design's M2 text says the scheduler "records an EWMA of decode step time to keep p95 ITL under a target". Both the gates and the mechanism say the controller is not needed:

1. *The stall is bounded by construction.* A running stream waits for at most one chunk per iteration, whatever the load, so ITL during a prefill is the decode step at B rows plus one 256-token chunk: measured medians 111 to 125 ms at B=8 (35 to 45 ms step plus about 75 ms chunk, the design estimate being 63 to 73 ms), p95 133 to 141. There is no load-dependent term for a controller to correct. The load-dependent part is the step time at B rows, which the 8-row cap already bounds (p95 45.6 ms with 8 rows decoding).
2. *The gates pass without it.* G1 133.2 ms (this run) and 140.8 ms (earlier) against 150, G2 37.5 / 45.6 against 60, G3 2.10x against 2.5x; the 10-minute soak at 0.8 req/s, above the 60%-of-saturation point, had ITL p95 117 ms pooled and 126.6 ms in-prefill, and the 30-minute soaks at B=8 and B=4 passed all Q13 rows.
3. *The only identity-safe actions are poor trades.* A controller that changes the chunk size would make a request's chunk schedule depend on timing, and the chunk schedule is part of numerical identity (design 4.5; Q2: the reference for a batched request is a solo run with the same chunk schedule). That is why the chunk is chosen once per request and kept (`busy_chunk_is_sticky`). The remaining action is to skip chunks (run a decode-only iteration instead). That keeps identity, but it spends the TTFT margin to buy ITL margin nobody asked for: with a decode-only iteration of about 45 ms inserted after each of the 16 chunks of a 4k prompt, the loaded 4k TTFT goes from 2.1 s to about 2.8 s, 2.9x solo (estimate, arithmetic: 16 x 45 ms = 0.72 s on top of 2.04 s), which fails G3 (2.5x) to lower an ITL that already passes.
4. *ITL tracks the chunk size, so it is a static knob.* 320 against 256: stall p95 176 against 139 ms (+27%, 10 of 10 rounds) for 2% better 4k TTFT (`2026-10-16-metal-cb-chunk320.md`, Q12). `serve --busy-chunk N` (16..2048) is the tuning knob; a smaller value lowers ITL at the price of TTFT.

**Caveats.** (a) `step_ewma_ms` is the wall time of a whole iteration, not the decode step: the decode pass first waits for the previous iteration's chunk to finish on the GPU, because non-final chunks are enqueued asynchronously (the chunk-call histogram showed 1905 of 2352 chunk calls under 1 ms in the soak; that is why it was renamed and its help text corrected in 583cc21). (b) Risk: the in-prefill ITL margin is 11% in this run and 6% in the earlier B=8 run (max 148.5 ms); a GPU clock drop (the overnight report documents 900 to 1300 MHz) would eat it. The remedy is `--busy-chunk 192`, not a controller. (c) `busy_chunks_per_iter` has no CLI flag; leave it at 1.
