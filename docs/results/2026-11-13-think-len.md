# think-len: why intarsiaml thinks about 15 % longer than vLLM, and writes a worked answer more often (2026-11-13)

Lane `think-len`, branch `think-len` from origin/main `662dc50ec`. Question from `docs/results/2026-11-13-cutover-soak.md` arm (c): on the same 85 GSM8K questions, thinking on, greedy, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4, intarsiaml (Bc) writes 2133 against vLLM's 1858 mean output tokens (+15 %, longer on 53 of 85, sign test p about 0.03), and writes a worked final answer more often than vLLM's bare number in every arm.

## Verdict

1. **Prompt, sampling and stop handling are ruled out.** Both engines receive bit-identical prompt token ids (thinking on and off), both were greedy, both stop on EOS, none truncated.
2. **The +15 % is not shown to be an engine effect, and the thinking-on style difference is a marker of it.** The two greedy traces diverge about 37 tokens in, at near-ties, and from there are independent draws of a heavy-tailed length. With 85 such draws the excess is borderline (bootstrap 95 % CI of the ratio of means 1.02 to 1.29; sign-flip p 0.03 on the mean difference, 0.05 on the mean log ratio; without the five largest differences p 0.10). Five questions carry 61 % of the excess. I found no mechanism that makes thinking longer, and the opening-phrase shift below does not (it does not change length). The thinking-on worked-final difference (24 against 11 of 85) is not a separate effect: given the same trace both engines answer the same way (item 3), and a worked final follows a long trace on both engines (mean 3180 and 3554 tokens against 1661 and 1574 for bare finals). intarsiaml simply has more long traces (more than 2500 tokens: 26 against 17 of 85; more than 4000: 6 against 2), which is also where the excess comes from. A larger set is needed to say whether that tail is real (last section).
3. **There is a real, systematic, measurable difference at the near-tie tokens, and it explains the thinking-off style difference.** At the first answer token with thinking off, intarsiaml puts less probability on starting a bare number than vLLM does: mean P(bare) 0.611 against 0.697, a log-odds shift of -0.49 (se 0.05), lower on 151 of 200 prompts. vLLM against a second vLLM run of the same prompts shows no shift (-0.007, log-odds -0.04 (se 0.05), 109 lower of 200), so this is not run-to-run noise. This is the thinking-off worked-answer style difference (a worked answer is chosen at that one token). Cause of the shift: **not found**. It is not the KV dtype (fp8 KV: -0.41, same size). Flash against SIMT attention changes it slightly (-0.40) and is not the main cause; the grouped MoE kernel I could disable is not on this checkpoint's path (bitwise identical output with it disabled), so the real NVFP4 expert path is untested.
4. **A second systematic shift exists at the thinking opening and is the KV dtype.** At the point where the trace says "The user provides [a few | three] examples" intarsiaml (bf16 KV) prefers "three" by +0.56 log-odds more than vLLM (se 0.05, n=35); with `--kv-dtype fp8`, which is what vLLM runs, the shift is +0.11 (se 0.08, not significant). Bc ran bf16 KV, vLLM fp8 KV: intarsiaml wrote "three" in 59 of 85 traces against vLLM's 34 (Fisher p 1e-4). This shows the two engines are not numerically interchangeable at near-ties, but it does not explain length: within each opening, intarsiaml's traces are still longer (table below), and the two openings have similar mean length (pooled 1919 against 2049).
5. **Reproducibility differs, and that matters for reading the soak.** intarsiaml is deterministic run to run (the first 96 thinking tokens of all 85 questions reproduce the soak's Bc trace exactly in three probe runs: `bf16`, `bf16b` and `nomoe`; the soak ran on spark2 and the probes on spark1, so this holds across boxes as well as runs). vLLM is not: its own soak trace and a fresh run of the same prompts agree on only 18 to 22 of 85. So the vLLM arm of the soak is one draw from a noisy distribution and intarsiaml's is one fixed draw. A difference of 15 % between two such draws needs a vLLM self-pair as the null.

No code change is needed for any of this and none is in the PR. The PR adds a regression test for item 1 (prompt token ids against vLLM's), the probe scripts and the raw data.

## 1. Existing data (no GPU): `raw/` of the soak, Bc against V-think, the same 85 question ids

- Token counts: sum 181 316 against 157 923 (ratio 1.148); geometric mean ratio 1.116 (bootstrap 95 % CI 1.00 to 1.24); mean of per-question ratios 1.265 (inflated by the tail). Longer on 53, shorter on 32.
- The excess is not spread evenly: the five largest per-question excesses (3657, 2875, 2733, 2631, 2274 tokens) are 61 % of the 23 393 total, the top ten 101 % (the rest net to zero). Mean difference without the top five: 115 tokens (6 %). No loops: all 170 replies finish with `stop`.
- Where the pairs diverge (reasoning text, character index of the first difference; no pair is identical): 31 of 85 at character 79 (the "provides a few | three examples" token), 61 within 200 characters, 73 within 400, median 161, latest 1066. The two traces are different greedy paths from about token 20 on.
- Opening branch, by engine and length (tokens):

| opening "The user provides ..." | vLLM, 85 traces: n, mean | intarsiaml: n, mean |
|---|---|---|
| a few | 51, 1830 | 25, 2102 |
| three | 34, 1900 | 59, 2135 |

  The mean length is similar for both openings (pooled 1919 against 2049, 76 and 93 traces), and intarsiaml is longer in each cell of the pairing table (a few/a few: 13 of 22 longer; three/three 18 of 31; a few/three 18 of 28). The opening is a symptom of the numerics difference, not the cause of the extra length.
- Style: after thinking, a worked final answer is written 24 times by intarsiaml and 11 by vLLM of 85 (soak doc). Worked finals follow long traces on both engines (mean 3554 and 3180 tokens against 1574 and 1661 for bare finals), and intarsiaml has more long traces: more than 2500 tokens 26 against 17 of 85, more than 3000 13 against 12, more than 4000 6 against 2.

## 2. Prompt equivalence: ruled out

- vLLM `/tokenize` (production `retro` on spark2, chat messages, `add_generation_prompt`, `chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking` true and false) on the soak's 85 pinned GSM8K requests (template sha256 `dd31b48d...`, one user message, no system prompt) against intarsiaml's own chat-template path (minijinja on the checkpoint's `tokenizer_config.json`, then `tokenizer.json`, add_special false): **identical ids on all 85, in both modes** (test `crates/intarsia-models/tests/qwen36_prompt_ids.rs`, run with `INTARSIA_QWEN36_DIR`; the generation prompt is `<|im_start|>assistant\n<think>\n` on and `...<think>\n\n</think>\n\n` off). The soak's `usage.prompt_tokens` (median 483, min 453, max 556) matched on both engines.
- Request body: localeval sends the same body to both (`temperature` 0, `stream`, `chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking`, `max_tokens` 8192, no top_p, no penalties); the two recipes differ only in the engine and KV dtype (vLLM `--kv-cache-dtype fp8`, Bc bf16).

## 3. Sampling and stop handling: ruled out

- Both servers apply the checkpoint's `generation_config.json` (temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20) only to requests without values; the soak requests carry `temperature: 0`, so both take the argmax. `raw/serve-bf16.metrics` shows `intarsia_sampling_defaults{...temperature="1",top_p="0.95",top_k="20"}`; intarsiaml implements no presence, repetition or frequency penalty, and the generation config has none.
- Greedy is direct: at **all 67 050 generated positions across the 8 probes (8445 each for vLLM, bf16 and fp8, 8360 each for the other five), the sampled token's logprob equals the engine's top-1** (vLLM and intarsiaml; `raw/analysis.txt` first block).
- Both stop at EOS (`finish_reason: stop` on all 170), `max_tokens` 8192 on both, nothing truncated.

## 4. Logit probe: what differs and by how much

Method (`scripts/think-len/`, raw in `raw/`): the soak's prompts as token-id lists (from vLLM `/tokenize`) sent to `/v1/completions` with `temperature 0`, `logprobs 20`, to vLLM (spark2 production, concurrency 1, a few hundred short requests, no fleetq slot) and to intarsiaml built from the same commit as Bc (the soak binary, stamp `ab35c-45e6ce56b023`, copied to spark1 and run in the Bc shape `--max-batch 2 --prefix-store on`, bf16 KV; `--max-seq 32768 --mem-budget 40`) under fleetq #9253 and #9254 (spark1, `--quiet gpu`, 45 GB; the 34 GB of spark1 vLLM stayed up and untouched; MemAvailable stayed above 51 GB; both jobs released). Arms: `bf16`, `fp8` (`--kv-dtype fp8`), `nomoe` (`INTARSIA_CUDA_DISABLE=moe_experts_mma_bf16`), `simt` (SIMT attention instead of flash and split-KV), `nomoe-simt`, `bf16b` (a repeat), and `vllm-b` (vLLM a second time). Three probe kinds: thinking-off prompt (200 questions, first answer token), thinking-on prompt (85, first 96 greedy tokens), thinking-on prompt plus the soak's vLLM trace plus `</think>\n\n` (85, first answer token).

### 4.1 The style token (thinking off, 200 prompts)

P(bare) is the summed probability, over the top-20 tokens, of tokens that start with a digit or `$`.

| intarsiaml arm against vLLM | mean P(bare) | mean diff (se) | log-odds shift (se) | prompts with lower P(bare) | argmax differs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| vLLM | 0.697 | | | | |
| bf16 (Bc shape) | 0.611 | -0.087 (0.009) | -0.49 (0.05) | 151 of 200 | 55 |
| fp8 KV | 0.624 | -0.073 (0.010) | -0.41 (0.06) | 150 | 49 |
| SIMT attention | 0.627 | -0.071 (0.009) | -0.40 (0.05) | 145 | 52 |
| no grouped MoE mma | 0.611 | identical to bf16 (the kernel is not used on this path) | | | |
| vLLM, second run (null) | 0.690 | -0.007 (0.008) | -0.04 (0.05) | 109 | 45 |

The argmax is bare on 162 of 200 prompts for vLLM and on 130 for intarsiaml. The mean per-prompt top-20 KL between the engines is 0.12 and between two vLLM runs 0.08 (vLLM's own spread is large per prompt, with no direction), so the size of the difference per prompt is not what separates the engines; the direction is. This is the thinking-off style difference (intarsiaml starts a worked answer more often with bf16 or fp8 KV alike: soak arms Ba 77 and Bb 73 of 200 against vLLM 50 and 53).

After a thinking trace the style gap at the answer token is gone. With the same vLLM trace fed to both engines the answer distributions agree to within 0.001 KL (P(bare) 0.871 on both, argmax identical on 85 of 85). And vLLM on **Bc's** traces (`raw/probe-vllm-tf-bc.json.gz`) has an argmax that is a bare number on 61 of 85, exactly Bc's actual 61 (P(bare) 0.718, against 0.871 on vLLM's own traces): the thinking-on style difference is fully carried by the trace content. Worked finals follow long traces on both engines (output tokens, worked against bare: vLLM 3180 against 1661 mean, 11 and 74 replies; intarsiaml 3554 against 1574, 24 and 61 replies), so it is the long-trace tail again.

### 4.2 The thinking opening and the KV dtype

At the first divergence of the two engines' greedy traces, the context is identical. For the 35 pairs where the divergence is "a" against "three" (bf16 arm), log-odds(three against a): vLLM -0.24, intarsiaml +0.32, paired shift +0.56 (se 0.05). With fp8 KV the shift is +0.11 (se 0.08, n=25); vLLM against vLLM is +0.07 (se 0.10, n=24). Own-path choices of "three": vLLM 27 and 29 (two runs) and 34 (soak) of 85; intarsiaml bf16 60, fp8 35, SIMT 52. So the opening difference is a KV-dtype effect (fp8 KV rounding moves this decision toward vLLM's), not a template effect.

Token 22 is the "a few | three" slot. The gap at divergence is under 0.5 nats on both engines for 45 of 78 diverging pairs. With fp8 KV intarsiaml agrees with vLLM as often as vLLM agrees with itself (17 and 22 of 85).

## 5. What is left, and how to settle the length question

- The +15 % is borderline on 85 paired draws with early divergence, tail-dominated, and the null (vLLM against vLLM) was not measured at the length level. Settling it needs one of: (a) a second vLLM thinking-on run on the same 85 to 100 questions (a fleetq slot on spark2, about 40 minutes of decode) to give the null spread, or (b) a larger paired set (300 or more questions on both engines), with intarsiaml at `--kv-dtype fp8` as a second arm since that is what removes the opening-phrase shift. Both are long thinking-on decodes (about 190k tokens per arm), not light queries, so they were not started here.
- Cause of the thinking-off style shift (log-odds -0.49) is open. Ruled out: prompt, sampling, KV dtype (fp8 does not help), the one MoE kernel that can be disabled (not on the path, so this says nothing about the NVFP4 expert path), flash and split-KV attention as the main cause (SIMT moves it by 0.1, same sign). Candidates not tested: the linear-attention (GatedDeltaNet) state and conv precision (the delta-rule variants are bitwise twins of each other, so they cannot be bisected with `INTARSIA_CUDA_DISABLE`), cuBLAS GEMM, the router top-k and renormalisation, the NVFP4 dequantisation path against vLLM's. The reference to decide who is closer to the true model would be the CPU backend (f64 reference math) on a few of the 200 prompts, as in `2026-10-14-35b-accuracy.md`; the 35B weights are not on endor and I did not run it. That earlier lane judged the CUDA-against-vLLM per-position KL to be noise on its 10 short prompts; a mean shift of one decision across 200 prompts is a different measurement and was not tested there.
- Not tested: the NVFP4 MoE expert path as a source of the thinking-off shift (the one MoE kernel `INTARSIA_CUDA_DISABLE` can hide here is not on the path), tool calls inside thinking, `reasoning_content` compared delta by delta (as the soak noted); the prefix store (0 hits in Bc, and the probe ran with the store on and cold).

## Update (vllm-null)

`docs/results/2026-11-13-vllm-null.md` measured the null this section asked for: two more vLLM thinking-on runs (V2, V3) on the same 85 questions, greedy, same call as the soak arm. vLLM against itself differs by up to 6.4 % in mean length (ratios 1.064, 1.043, 0.980); intarsiaml against the per-question mean of the three vLLM runs is 1.109 (bootstrap 95 % CI 0.992 to 1.232), against V2 and V3 alone 1.079 and 1.101 (CIs include 1, sign p 0.52). The original +15 % was inflated by V1 being the shortest vLLM draw. The excess stays concentrated in the tail: without the five V1 outlier questions the ratio is 1.024, and four of the five remain the largest excesses over the vLLM mean. Verdict: not shown to lie outside vLLM's own spread, not excluded either.
