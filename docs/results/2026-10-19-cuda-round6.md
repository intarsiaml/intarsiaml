# CUDA round 6: where the 35B prefill gap to vLLM actually is, and two bitwise levers (2026-10-19)

Target: spark1 (DGX Spark GB10, sm_121, CUDA 13.0), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4. Base: `origin/main` 0df54872. Branch
`perf/cuda-round6`, not pushed. Raw logs are in `docs/results/2026-10-19-cuda-round6/raw/`, and the fleetq slot logs are in
`raw/slots/<job>-*.log`.

## 1. Headline

- **Two levers ship as defaults. Both give bitwise-identical results to base.**
  - **The flash prefill attention at head_dim 256 now runs 128 query rows per block (8 warps).** Before, it ran 64 rows
    with 4 warps, and that was the only block per SM. The change is 9-10 % faster at 8k-16k KV.
  - **The w4a16 MoE kernel now starts at 768 tokens instead of 1024.** Above 768 tokens it is faster than the grouped
    kernel, which is bitwise the same.
  - In-engine A/B ×3 against base, `--at-most`, prefill: **+4.6 % at 1k, +0.2 % at 4k, +1.1 % at 16k**. Decode is
    unchanged.
  - Greedy ids, KL21 (short and long), `chat_reuse_4k`, the prefix-reuse gate and the 8B goldens all match base to every
    digit. The 8B plan is byte-identical.
- **Round 5's lever was the wrong one: `moe_w4a16` is already within 4-5 % of vLLM's kernel at the chunk size.** I
  benchmarked vLLM's own NVFP4 Marlin MoE inside its container at our shapes, without disturbing the server:
  - T=2048: 3.40 ms for vLLM against our 3.54-3.60 ms.
  - T=1024: 2.60 against 2.92-2.99 ms.
  - Neither kernel is near a roof: about 30 TFLOPS against 122 for the bf16 MMA, and 49 % of the DRAM weight-stream
    floor. vLLM does not do better here than we do.
- **The largest single difference to vLLM at 16k is the fp8 projection GEMMs, not MoE, GDN or attention.** The
  same-shape microbenchmarks (section 4) put it at about 49 ms per 2048-token chunk:
  - vLLM: W8A8 on e4m3 tensor cores, CUTLASS `cutlass_scaled_mm`, 83-135 TFLOPS including activation quant at T=2048.
  - Ours: bf16 MMA over e4m3-valued bf16 (`gemm_reg`), 36-68 TFLOPS, plus a separate `fp8_actq` pass.
  - The like-for-like localeval gap at 16k is 2048 / 5344 − 2048 / 6308 = 59 ms per 2048 tokens. The fp8 path is about
    83 % of it, MoE about 7 ms, and about 3 ms is unexplained.
  - The microbenchmarks were contended and carry roughly ±13 ms per chunk of slop. At 4k the sum (about 56 ms)
    overshoots the localeval gap (33 ms per 2048 tokens), so this is an approximate decomposition, not a closed budget.
  - Our GDN recurrence is at parity with vLLM's FLA chunked kernel at T=2048 (1.71-1.88 against 1.80 ms with its prep).
    Our attention is faster than vLLM's FlashInfer on its fp8 KV cache.
  - Closing the gap needs e4m3 activations in the projection GEMMs. That changes the numerics (round 4 has the KL21 and ids
    failures of our own e4m3 kernel), so it is a user decision (section 14).
- **Opt-in, not bitwise: `--prefill-chunk 4096`.** It is +5.7 % at 4k and +6.1 % at 16k (in-engine, same window).
  - KL21 long goes 4.576e-2 → 4.678e-2; short is unchanged (the prompt is shorter than one chunk).
  - `chat_reuse_4k` and the prefix-reuse gate pass.
  - Greedy ids at 16k diverge from chunk 2048 at token 41 of 64.
  - This is a user decision (section 14). vLLM itself runs `max_num_batched_tokens=2048`.
- **localeval vs vLLM, same evening:**
  - prefill: dev 4310 / 5681 / 5344 tok/s against vLLM 4699 / 6245 / 6308. That is −8.3 % / −9.0 % / −15.3 %.
  - decode: dev 83.2 / 80.8 / 73.1 tok/s against vLLM 78.7 / 79.1 / 76.3.
- **One fleet-rule incident.** About 8 minutes of profile run on spark1 outside a granted slot, with vLLM up and nothing
  taken down. Section 12 has the details.

## 1c. Tried and dropped

Timings below are from microbenchmarks with vLLM up and idle, so they are relative numbers.

- **GDN, hoisting the per-token prep:** compute the q/k norms, beta and the decay once per token and k head in a small
  kernel, through scratch. Bitwise, but no gain: 1718-1721 µs against 1716-1877 µs at T=2048. Reverted. The loads it kept
  are still on the warp's serial path.
- **GDN, a cp.async ring 8 tokens deep for the raw q/k rows:** bitwise (gdn_split passes) but slower: 1753-1760 µs against
  1712-1742. Reverted. The bisect below says the per-token loads and prep cost about 40 % of the kernel, but moving them
  through shared memory adds as many instructions as it removes. With 1.3 warps per scheduler, the kernel is
  issue/latency bound. ncu would show the stall reasons, but `RmProfilingAdminOnly=1` on spark1 rules it out.
- **gemm_reg tile configurations**, all bitwise (none changes the K order):
  - BK 32 with 2 blocks/SM: ±1.4 %, noise;
  - BK 32 with 4 stages: 9-15 % slower;
  - BK 64 with 2 stages: 4-11 % slower.
  - Dropped. The fp8 projection gap is the MMA format, not the tiling (section 4).
- **Chunk 8192:** 16k prefill fell to 3178 tok/s and decode to 36-40 tok/s. That is memory pressure next to vLLM's 64 GB
  on unified memory. Dropped.

## 5. The levers

### 5.1 Flash D=256, 128 query rows per block

- **Bits:** each query row's arithmetic does not depend on the block height. For rows 0-63 of a 128-row block, the key
  tiles past their last visible key are fully masked:
  - the tile max is −inf, so `mn = m` and `cr = exp2f(0) = 1` exactly;
  - P = 0, so the sum is unchanged and O += 0·V;
  - the causal mask is element-wise.
  - `tests/flash_bq.rs` compares the two block heights byte for byte (78 cases plus 4 small ones). The reference sweep
    `tests/attention.rs` passes.
- **Resources:** 101376 B of dynamic shared memory, the opt-in per-block maximum, so 1 block of 8 warps per SM. The fp8 KV
  path stays at 64 rows: its staging would not fit.
- **Speed:** −5 % at kv 2048, −10 % at 8k-16k (section 4.4). End to end: +1.1 % at 16k, +0.2 % at 4k.

### 5.2 `moe_experts_w4a16_bf16` from 768 tokens

| T | grouped (µs) | w4a16 (µs) |
|---|---|---|
| 512 | 2545-2640 | 2688-2722 |
| 640 | 2665-2689 | 2770-2810 |
| 768 | 2938-2956 | 2836-2844 |
| 896 | 2982-3026 | 2911-2957 |
| 1000 | 3062-3091 | 2892-2913 |

Three rounds (`raw/slots`, `dev.sh STEPS=moe`). w4a16 is 3-6 % slower up to 640 tokens and 2-6 % faster from 768 on. Its
bitwise equality with the grouped kernel is already pinned by `tests/moe_w4a16.rs` (4 tests). The 1k-class prompts of the
A/B (about 960 tokens) and of localeval (about 990) now take it: +4.6 % at 1k in-engine.

**Why chunk 4096 changes bits** (`raw/r6chunk`, `raw/r6bisect`):

- Greedy ids and top-20 logits differ between chunks 2048 and 4096 at 5822, 11692 and 16014 tokens.

- Likely suspect: cuBLAS kernels whose tile and split-K choice depends on M. The profile shows `nvjet ... 32x64` plus

## 14. Decisions for the user

1. **Merge the two bitwise levers** (flash BQ 128, w4a16 from 768): +4.6 % / +0.2 % / +1.1 % prefill, same bits
   everywhere, no shared files. Recommended.
2. **Chunk 4096 for the 35B on CUDA:** +5.7 % at 4k and +6.1 % at 16k, but not bitwise.
   - KL21 long goes 4.576e-2 → 4.678e-2 (+2.2 %). Ids diverge at token 41 of 64 on the 16k prompt.
   - Reuse gates pass. The plan's scratch doubles from 84.0 MB to 167.9 MB (`raw/slots/plan35-chunk-memory.log`); the
     activation buffers grow with the chunk as well. vLLM itself uses 2048.
   - Making it the default means changing the recipe's `tuning.cuda` prefill chunk, a shared file (no effect on Metal or
     HIP, which keep their own values). Options: (a) leave it as the `--prefill-chunk 4096` flag (current state); (b) make
     it the CUDA recipe value. I recommend (a) until the e4m3 decision below, because both change numerics and should be
     judged together.
3. **The largest part of the remaining 16k gap is e4m3 activations in the projection GEMMs**: about 49 ms per chunk,
   about 83 % of the like-for-like localeval gap (58.6 ms per 2048 tokens).
   - Closing it needs a CUTLASS-class W8A8 e4m3 kernel: vLLM's runs at 83-135 TFLOPS with quant at T=2048, and our opt-in
     `matmul_fp8_gemm_f8` at about 65.
   - That kernel changes the summation order and rounding of activations, as vLLM's does, but it fails our KL21 / ids
     gates as they stand (round 4).
   - The question for you: will you accept fp8 activation numerics (with KL measured against the vLLM goldens) as an
     opt-in or a default target for round 7? If not, the bitwise prefill headroom left is small:
     - MoE to Marlin parity: about 1.8 % at 16k, a new kernel;
     - GDN schedule: up to about 5 %, not found yet;
     - attention to FlashInfer-bf16 speed: not bitwise.
4. **Round 5's "moe_w4a16 next"** is withdrawn by the headroom data in section 4.1.
