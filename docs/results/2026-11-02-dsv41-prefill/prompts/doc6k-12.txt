## Headline

**Small round. The final defaults add +0.4 to +0.6 % 35B decode at 128 to 4096 tokens. 35B prefill and the 8B are flat
(W9, interleaved x3 vs base `ab887732`). Every default is bitwise identical to base.**

* **Where the decode gain comes from:** page-locked staging of small uploads and downloads (lever 1). It takes the host
  waits out of the per-step token-id upload and argmax download. It is HIP-only.
* **Round 8's "3.4 ms per chunk boundary" was mislabelled.** It is a HIP upload sync plus the decode-graph capture that
  `generate` does per request. The serve path never pays the capture. The shared prefill loop is untouched, so CPU, CUDA
  and Metal are unaffected and no no-op checklist is needed.
* **The ~275 us stall is real without a profiler,** as about 256 us after each dispatch of 1 ms or more. Round 8's split
  is a real gain. The new split trigger (lever 2) only stops cutting launches that would not stall. It measured equal to
  round 8's rule at 1024, 2048 and 4096-token chunks.
* **Split-KV stage 1 at 8 waves per SIMD (lever 3) is opt-in** (`INTARSIA_HIP_SPLITKV_OCC=1`):
  * +1.5 % decode at ~17.6k tokens;
  * -0.6 % at 128 to 2048 tokens, from a 28 B/lane register spill (W8);
  * user decision below.

## The ~275 us stall without a profiler

**About 256 us per dispatch of 1 ms or more, unprofiled, in the engine.** rocprofv3 moves the stall but does not create it:
unprofiled it opens one dispatch later than the profiler shows it. That is why a standalone reproducer that only times the
gap right after a long kernel sees ~16 us.

### Method (W1, stage `pfstamp`; `raw/stamps/`, `raw/prof/stall-*.txt`)

* `INTARSIA_HIP_STAMP=<file>` (new, diagnostics only, off by default) launches a 1-thread kernel that stores the GPU's
  constant-rate wall clock (`wall_clock64`, 100 MHz) before and after every long prefill dispatch: MoE gate_up and down (and
  each piece), the GDN delta rule (and each piece), and flash attention. It uses no events, no completion signals and no
  profiler. The stamps are dumped when the backend is dropped.
* The same 4408-token prefill ran unprofiled and under `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace`, with the split on (default) and off
  (`INTARSIA_HIP_SPLIT_US=0`). The profiled trace gives each bracketed dispatch's duration and shows the stamp kernels.
* `stampgap.py` pairs every bracket with its dispatch. `stampseg.py` sums the unstamped stretches between ops.
* Stamps do not move the total: stamped prefill 513 / 519 ms, unstamped on the same build 515 / 511 / 519 / 514 ms.

### The cleanest case: gate_up<64> -> down

gate_up<64> is one 2.1 to 2.2 ms dispatch in every run and is never split. Its end stamp is followed directly by down's
begin stamp, so nothing runs between those two 1-thread kernels (`stall-delayed-gateup-to-down.txt`). Per layer, in us:

| run | gate_up end stamp -> down begin stamp (40 layers) |
|---|---|
| unprofiled, split on | 3 on layer 0; 250 to 275 (median 260) on the other 39 |
| unprofiled, split off | 5 on layer 0; 250 to 270 on the other 39 |
| rocprofv3, split on | 4 to 5 on all 40 |
| rocprofv3, split off | 4 to 6 on all 40 |

* **Under rocprofv3**, the ~277 us sits between the long dispatch and the next one: the bracket around gate_up grows from
  ~2.19 to ~2.45 ms. This is round 8's picture, and the reproducer's.
* **Without the profiler**, the dispatch right after the long one starts promptly. The dead time inside the brackets of
  >= 1 ms dispatches is a median of 6 to 35 us (`stall-stampgap-split-on.txt`). The ~256 us gap opens **before the
  second dispatch after the long one**.
* Layer 0's gate_up has no gap in any of the four runs. With the split off, an unsplit 3.1 ms GDN launch runs just before
  it in the same layer. I did not find out why.
* Every other long dispatch shows the same. With the split off, unprofiled, the stretch after each unsplit GDN delta rule
  (3.1 ms) and each MoE down (1.1 ms) grows by 236 to 256 us (`stall-stampseg-unprofiled.txt`):
  * +7.7 ms over 30 GDN launches;
  * +7.1 + 2.3 ms over 40 down launches.
* So the engine loses this time with or without the profiler, and round 8's split gain is real. Unprofiled, split on vs off:
  513 vs 526 ms prefill; stamped span 495.1 vs 506.7 ms.
* At 1024-token chunks (W2, `stall-stampgap-chunk1024.txt`), 584 of 600 bracketed dispatches stay under 1 ms (GDN ~0.77 ms,
  gate_up<64> ~0.88 ms), and under rocprofv3 none of them shows the gap. The 16 that reach 1 ms (12 flash launches in the
  later chunks, 3 gate_up, 1 GDN) show the ~277 us under rocprofv3.

**For the ROCm issue:** a reproducer needs three dispatches (long, short, short), timing the gap before the third. Timing
only the gap after the long kernel reads ~16 us. The cause (CP, firmware or driver) is still open.

**What is left of it in the default prefill:** gate_up<64> is the only dispatch of >= 1 ms that is still not split, plus
flash attention at 4096 tokens (3.0 ms, 10 launches). The stamped runs put the gate_up -> down stretch at 10.1 ms per
4408-token prompt (39 x ~256 us, about 2 %). That figure has one caveat: in the stamped runs the dispatch after gate_up is
a 1.4 us stamp kernel. In the unstamped engine it is down's first piece (~550 us), and I did not measure whether the gap
still falls before down's second piece. Round 8 split gate_up and dropped it (the pieces' load-imbalance tails cost 28 ms
against ~11 ms of gaps); I did not retry it.

## Round 8's "3.4 ms per chunk boundary" was mislabelled

Round 8 listed about 3.4 ms of host time at every prefill chunk boundary (3 per 16k prompt), in the shared core prefill loop.
The traces say otherwise (`embgaps.py` on round 8's 4408-token and ~17.6k traces, `raw/prof/chunk-gaps-r8traces.txt`):

* **A chunk boundary costs ~0.5 ms.** That is the recurrent-state snapshot (31 `copyBuffer`, ~345 us of device-to-device
  copies) plus 105 to 190 us of idle GPU before the next chunk's embedding. At 17.6k the three boundaries cost 450 to 480 us
  each.
* **The 3.5 ms gap comes once per prompt, before the last prompt token.** A recurrent model prefills all but the last token,
  snapshots, and then runs the last token on its own (`engine.rs` `prefill_step`). That step goes through the decode graph,
  and `generate` captures the graph right there: the cache is per `SequenceState::id`, which is new per request in
  `generate`. Round 8's API trace shows the token-id upload, its `hipStreamSynchronize`, then `hipStreamBeginCapture` ...
  `hipStreamEndCapture` inside the gap.
* **The serve path does not pay the capture per request.** `LocalEngine` keeps one state for the process, so the graph is
  captured once: every row of every serve bench JSON this round has `"captures": 0`.
* **Why the GPU idles at both:**
  * every `Backend::upload` was a pageable `hipMemcpyAsync` followed by `hipStreamSynchronize` (`ik_rt_memcpy_h2d`);
  * the chunk's token-id upload, and the id / position uploads before the last token's step, therefore drain the queue;
  * the GPU then waits for the host to wake up and launch again, and in `generate` also to capture.

So the lever is HIP-only: it changes how the HIP backend uploads, and the shared prefill loop is untouched (lever 1 below).
