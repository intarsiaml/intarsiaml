## 4. Decode GEMV: the >= 85% of 627 GB/s target

*Tree: 4963b43 + uncommitted worktree (dirty), later committed as a7da13b; exact tree not recorded.*

`cargo test --release -p intarsia-backend-hip --test matmul_bench gemv -- --ignored --nocapture`: one launch per weight matrix, the weights rotate over >= 768 MB of
distinct copies so the 64 MB Infinity Cache cannot serve them, launches back to back on one stream (so launch gaps are included). Final variant `INTARSIA_HIP_GEMV=1,8`
(1 row per warp, 8 x 16 B non-temporal loads in flight per lane, 8 warps per block).

| Shape (Qwen3-8B) | N x K | Time | GB/s | % of 627 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| q_proj | 4096 x 4096 | 60.3 us | 556.5 | 88.7 |
| k_proj | 1024 x 4096 | 23.1 us | 362.7 | 57.8 |
| v_proj | 1024 x 4096 | 22.7 us | 369.7 | 59.0 |
| o_proj | 4096 x 4096 | 59.6 us | 563.0 | 89.8 |
| gate_proj | 12288 x 4096 | 163.0 us | 617.5 | 98.5 |
| up_proj | 12288 x 4096 | 163.9 us | 614.2 | 98.0 |
| down_proj | 4096 x 12288 | 165.1 us | 609.6 | 97.2 |
| lm_head (f32 out) | 151936 x 4096 | 2214.7 us | 562.0 | 89.6 |
| **per token** | 15.14 GB | 25.89 ms | **584.6** | **93.2** |

Weight-stream floor for the 15.14 GB a token really reads (the embedding table is a one-row gather) is 41.4 tok/s; GEMV-only ceiling at the measured rates is 38.6 tok/s.
Variant sweep (aggregate GB/s per token, rows per warp R, loads in flight U): 1,8 **585.1**; 1,4 582.8; 2,8 582.1; 1,16 576.0; 2,4 578.4; 2,2 574.9; 4,4 573.1; 4,2 566.7.
The small k/v projections (8 MB, 13 us at the roofline) are latency and launch bound: two sequential load rounds plus the reduction and the gap between launches. They cost
about 0.65 ms of the 27 ms token; fusing k and v (or a graph) is the fix, not a different GEMV.

## 7. Gaps and what to do next

*Tree: 4963b43 + uncommitted worktree (dirty), later committed as a7da13b; exact tree not recorded.*

1. **Prefill** (2.0-4.4k tok/s): GEMM at 48-81 TFLOPS is about 25-42% of peak and flash attention at 22-27 TFLOPS about 13%. Next: a double-buffered K loop with direct-to-LDS loads or
   `ds_read_tr`, 256-row tiles for N = 12288, a K/V-prefetching flash kernel (the 4k prefill is attention-bound: 3068 vs 3386 tok/s at 1k), and a hipBLASLt route (`Fast`) for comparison.
2. **k/v GEMV** at 58%: fuse the k and v projections (one launch, N = 2048) in the model block; HIP graphs for the decode step.
3. **lm_head GEMV** at 89.6%: every variant gives 562 GB/s, so it is not the loop shape; page-table reach on the 1.2 GB matrix is a suspect (unverified), try a 2 MB-aligned allocation.
4. **Decode attention at 4k+**: 33 us per layer against a 27 us byte floor (16.8 MB of K+V per layer at 627 GB/s); the split-count policy is shared with the CUDA backend and has not been tuned for this card.
5. TP=2 over both cards, quantised weights (MXFP4 x FP8), GDN and MoE: radiance ports (`docs/research/radiance-port-plan.md`).

Total from job start to radiance back: **29 min 02 s** (13 takedowns: five dense-model bursts 17 min 16 s, eight 35B bursts 11 min 46 s); the longest wait after a release was 43 s (#1399), not 15 s.
In one case the lease was released before radiance answered: the CPU-only job #1412 started on the same slots at 02:03:43, four seconds before radiance-rank0 was back (02:03:47, after #1411).
The cause was the burst script: it called `fleetq release` and then started radiance (or waited a fixed 15 s) instead of waiting for `/v1/models` to answer first. Radiance was serving `Qwen3.8` when this was written.

Reproduce a burst (the order matters: the lease is released only after radiance answers):

```
fleetq acquire --host tatooine --takes-down radiance --mem 30 --label "..." --wait      # prints the job id
ssh tatooine systemctl --user stop radiance
ssh tatooine "FLEETQ_JOB=<id> bash <script that runs dev.sh commands, HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0>"
ssh tatooine systemctl --user start radiance
until curl -s -m 4 http://tatooine:8080/v1/models | grep -q '"data":\['; do sleep 5; done   # give up after about 3 minutes and do NOT release
fleetq release <id>
```
