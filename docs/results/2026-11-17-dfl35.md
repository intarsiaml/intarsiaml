# Lane dfl35: could a DFlash2 draft beat MTP k2 for the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4 on one GB10? (feasibility probe, 2026-11-17)

Read-only probe: no code, no default changed, no hardware run, nothing downloaded. Sources read: `.worktrees/scan/findings.md` item 5, `.worktrees/scan/raw/tf-changelog.md` (line 1614), TensorFold at `/opt/code/tensorfold`, this tree at `cc7c8026c`, the DFlash2 and MTP result docs named below, and `ls` over ssh of spark1 / spark2 (endor: `Host key verification failed`, not checked; it holds no CUDA 35B run in this question anyway).

**Verdict: NO-GO as a lane now.** The draft exists and is already on spark2, the engine gap is CUDA kernels plus untested MoE glue (about 16 to 24 h), and the expected gain over our MTP k2 is at best level on code-like text and a loss on prose / chat. Details and the cheap conditions under which it would flip to GO are at the end.

## 2. Loader and engine fit

The hybrid part is already solved: the 27B DFlash2 target is hybrid (64 layers, 48 GDN, 16 gated attention; `2026-10-26-dfh-hip-dflash.md` section on taps) and runs `--spec dflash` on HIP with taps on GDN and attention layers and GDN `SpecRows` rollback shared with `--spec mtp`. The 35B differs by being MoE and by running on CUDA. The "dense, ungated attention model (Qwen3)" refusal at `dflash.rs:178-180` is the v1 path only; `load_head2` (`dflash.rs:300`) has no MoE or gated check.

Product limits that remain even after the blockers (not blockers for a feasibility measurement, but they decide whether it could ever be a default):

- `--spec dflash` refuses the batching engine (`load.rs:851`); our CUDA default is batched MTP at `--max-batch 2`. DFlash would be a single-sequence opt-in.
- Macro prefill is turned off whenever a DFlash draft is loaded (`model.rs:1503`); with a loaded MTP head that cost prefill -20 / -37 / -51 % at 4k / 16k / 34k before lane mpf (`2026-10-27-mpf-macro-prefill-mtp.md` line 4). Lifting it needs per-chunk taps in the macro pass.
- A DFlash draft bypasses the prefix store (`INTARSIA_MTP_STORE` entry in `docs/tuning.md`), and the CUDA-graph step / deferred verify path is MTP-only (`engine.rs:2116,2393,2412`), so every DFlash pass is eager.
- Not covered: tp > 1, sampled requests.

## 3. Expected gain

Our baseline, 35B NVFP4 on spark1, `serve --max-batch 1 --spec mtp --spec-k 2` (`2026-11-17-mcopy2.md` section (c), `2026-11-17-mcopy.md` gate b): **133 to 158 tok/s** over the 7 mcopy cells (chat-short 132.8, code-gen 153.7, reason 153.7, json-xform 149.4, agent-edit 151.7, copy3k 151.5, agent-batch 157.6). `--spec off` is 85 tok/s, so MTP k2 is 1.8x. From `2026-10-29-cmtp-cuda-mtp-gap.md`: 2.45 tokens per pass, so an MTP pass is about 16 ms.

27B DFlash2 k7 vs MTP k2 on HIP tp=1 (`2026-11-06-27b-dflash-hip-tp1.md`): DFlash2 / MTP = 0.95 (chat), 1.19 (code), 0.84 (prose), 1.11 (recall), 1.31 (rcp-code), 0.85 (rcp-prose), 1.15; tokens per pass 2.5 (prose) to 5.1 (code), against MTP's 2.1 to 2.8; a DFlash2 pass costs about 50 ms against 35 ms for MTP on a 28 ms plain step, and break-even is 1.9 tokens per pass over plain.

Estimate for the 35B on GB10 (273 GB/s, my arithmetic, not measured):

- draft: about 1 GB of bf16 draft weights plus the 248k-row head over 7 rows, 8 to 10 ms;
- verify8: the 35B step is weight-bandwidth bound; 8 rows hit about 60 distinct experts a layer (about 2 MB each at NVFP4) against 8 for one row, so verify8 is roughly 25 to 30 ms against 11.8 ms plain and about 16 ms for MTP's 3 rows;
- pass about 38 to 42 ms, eager. To match MTP k2's 150 tok/s it needs about 6 tokens per pass; to reach TF's 130 it needs about 5.2. The 27B reached 4.8 to 5.1 on code and 2.5 to 3.9 elsewhere.

So on code-like or copy-heavy text DFlash2 could land near MTP k2 (0.9 to 1.2x) and on prose, chat and reasoning it should lose (0.5 to 0.8x), the same split as on the 27B. The copy-heavy cells are what `INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY` (mcopy / mcopy2, opt-in) already wins by 1.7 to 2.06x without a draft model.

**TF's 130 vs 99 does not transfer.** The 1.31x is TF's DFlash2 against TF's MTP at 99 tok/s on one 4.2K prompt. Our MTP k2 already runs 133 to 158 tok/s on the same GPU class, 1.3 to 1.6x TF's number, so TF's DFlash2 figure (130) is below our MTP k2 on every cell. The gap is plausible as a TF-side result (their MTP path is weaker: a gap of this kind is what lane cmtp / mpf / mtp2s closed on our side) and not plausible as our gain. The prompt is unnamed in the changelog; a repetitive 4.2K prompt would also favour a draft model, so one cell says little.

## 4. Verdict: NO-GO now

Reason: the best case is parity with MTP k2 on code-like text, bought with a CUDA kernel port, untested MoE tap glue and 16 to 24 h, while giving up batching, macro prefill, the prefix store and graph replay; prose, chat and reasoning would regress. The one cell family where DFlash2 can win is already covered by the opt-in copy drafts.

What would flip it to GO (cheap to check first, no engine work):

1. TF's own numbers on a prose cell and a code cell, same box, to see tokens per pass of this draft on this target (acceptance is the unknown; the 27B numbers above are the only proxy).
2. If tokens per pass is at least 6 on our cells, the lane is: B2 (DynConv CUDA kernel) -> B1/B3/B4 on spark1 with the draft copied from spark2 -> B5 cpu-ref MoE tap test -> B6/B7, then the gate below.

Gate if built (ids and speed, nothing default-on): greedy ids equal `--spec off` on short, 4k and 16k prompts (and `INTARSIA_DFLASH_DEVICE_TOPK=check` shows 0 differing rows); decode tok/s of `--spec dflash --spec-k 7` against `--spec mtp --spec-k 2` on the 7 mcopy cells (`scripts/mcopy/ab.sh`, ABBA, 3 rounds x 3 reps), required at least 1.03x median on the cells it is meant for and no cell below 0.95x, or it stays an experiment.
