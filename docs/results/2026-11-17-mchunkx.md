## 1. Verdict

**Stop rule (task 4) hit: findings only, no engine change, no knob, no default changed.** The PR carries the timing test (`tests/rows_exact.rs`, `timing_chunkx`,
`#[ignore]`), the scripts, the raw data, this doc and the LOG row; nothing in `crates/*/src`, `kernels/` or `docs/tuning.md` changes.

* **At 8 rows and below no split of any kernel beats the single launch** (2 and 3 rows have no split: a launch takes 2 rows or more). At 4..=8 rows the best alternative split is 1.09x to 2.0x slower than today's one launch, on every kernel and every shape,
  in both timing runs (section 4). A launch re-reads the weights, and none of these kernels is compute-bound per row the way MXFP4's `_m8` shader is (mchunk4):
  their cost per row falls with the launch size all the way to 8 rows (table A).
* The ship cells are 6 and 8 streams on the 35B and on the 27B. A batched step has one row per decoding stream, so those cells launch 6 or 8 rows. Today's rule is the
  optimum there, so any table that differs from today can differ only from 9 rows up; a server at its `--max-batch 8` would dispatch byte for byte what main does, and the
  ABBA the brief asks for would compare main with itself. It was not built or run (section 8 lists every gate not run, and why).
* **The 27B has nothing to gain from these kernels at `--max-batch 8`**: its MXFP4 projections are `matmul_mxfp4_gemv_rows` (mchunk4's table, left alone), and the only other row-exact
  launch of its decode step is the fp8 head (`matmul_fp8_gemv_rows`, 248320 x 5120, one launch per step). It dispatches no bf16 or nvfp4 rows kernel.
* **Where a split does win: 9..=12 rows, i.e. `--max-batch 16` only** (not a default; Metal's recipe and batch cap are untouched). The fp8 rows kernel runs 9..12 rows today as halves (5+4, 5+5,
  6+5, 6+6), each half on the simdgroup-matrix form that costs the same at every M from 5 to 8; one 8-row launch plus a 2..4-row tail is 8-25 % cheaper per step of the kernel, replicated in both runs
  (section 5). That is a lead for a follow-up lane, not a result: timing-derived, no ABBA (section 6).

## 2. What was timed and how

`timing_chunkx` (`crates/intarsia-backend-metal/tests/rows_exact.rs`, `#[ignore]`; `scripts/mchunkx/timing.sh`): for each kernel group, **one decode step's launches of that kernel** at the real shapes
(section 3), for M = 2..=16 rows, run as **every split of M into launches of 2..=8 rows** (what `row_chunks` can do: the kernels take 2..=8 rows per launch, and a 1-row chunk is not an option for a
`_rows` kernel) and as today's rule (`cur`: the M-row call through `launch`, which halves above 8 rows). Launches are built chunk by chunk as `row_chunks` slices them (input 0 and every output).
Weights are cycled through up to 400 MB of distinct copies (the step's layers), so they are not cache-hot; activations random, fp8 with a static input scale (q(x) pass on) and the `_xd` /
simdgroup-matrix scratch as the engine passes it, the heads with f32 out. Best of 3 rounds of 6..12 steps per number; the whole sweep (200 lines per group) was run twice (`raw/timing/run1.log`,
`run2.log`); every table takes the minimum over the two runs.

Groups: `fp8_35b`, `nvfp4_35b`, `nvfp4_head_35b`, `pair_35b`, `fp8_head_27b` (dispatched by the served decode); `bf16_35bshapes`, `bf16_27bshapes` (`matmul_bf16_gemv_rows`), `logits_bf16_35b`,
`logits_bf16_27b` (`logits_bf16_gemv_rows`) (not dispatched by either model's served decode: for the record, at the models' own matrix sizes). `moe_experts_bf16_rows` is not timed: it is excluded from
`row_chunks` while `moe::rows_native()` (the default per-pair kernels, up to 16 rows in one launch, lane mdec; the trace shows it as one launch per layer and call).

## 5. Why the fp8 halves lose at 9..12

The fp8 rows kernel runs 5..=8 rows on the simdgroup-matrix form, whose launch costs the same at every M (35B layers 4503 us at 5..8 rows, the 27B head 3436 us). A 9..12-row call
halved as 5+4 / 5+5 / 6+5 / 6+6 is two such launches, each re-reading the weights; 8 rows plus a 2..4-row tail is one full-price launch and one at the 4-row `_xd` price (2303 us for the head, against
3436). At 13 and 14 rows (8+5, 8+6) the tail is again a matrix-form launch and the gain is gone (1.00); 15 and 16 rows prefer 8+7 and 8+8 (today's halves).

## 6. The lead: what 9..12 rows could gain on `--max-batch 16` (not measured end to end)

The 35B's decode step on main at `--max-batch 16` (cb-trace decode iteration p50, one visit, 2 requests x 256 tokens per stream, `scripts/mchunkx/step35.sh`, `raw/step35/`):
**8 streams 19.1 ms, 10 streams 27.0, 12 streams 30.2, 16 streams 36.2** (aggregate 370 / 333 / 353 / 387 tok/s). The fp8 kernel alone is 4.5 ms of the 8-row step and 8.9 ms of the 10-row
one in this bench, i.e. more than half of the 19 -> 27 ms step jump when `--max-batch` goes past 8. Replacing the fp8 halves by the plans above would save, per step:

| rows | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35B fp8 plan | 7+2 | 8+2 | 8+3 | 8+4 |
| 35B saves (fp8_35b) | 0.87 ms | 1.62 ms | 1.18 ms | 0.76 ms (+0.09 nvfp4 7+5) |
| 27B head plan | - | 8+2 | 8+3 | 8+4 |
| 27B saves (fp8_head_27b) | - | 2.0 ms | 0.9 ms | 0.7 ms |

Against the 35B's measured steps (about 25-30 ms at 9..12 rows) that is roughly 3-6 % at 9..11 and 2.5 % at 12; against the 27B's 80-120 ms steps it is under 2 %. These are timing-derived estimates of the kernel's
share, not an ABBA; the microbench's step is a warm queue of launches and the engine's command buffers differ. A follow-up lane would add one fp8 table (`matmul_fp8_gemv_rows` only: 9 = 7+2, 10 = 8+2, 11 = 8+3,
12 = 8+4, everything else as today), check it bitwise (every row of a row-exact kernel is the one-row launch, so any split is), and ABBA the 35B at `--max-batch 16`, 9..12 streams. Nothing here
changes a default cap: `--max-batch` unset is still 1, and the 35B's default deployment on endor does not batch.
