## 1. Result

fp4 weights dequantised in registers. **Bitwise identical to `moe_experts_mma_bf16` (v1)** on every test input, so accuracy,

## 2. Profile of the old kernel (where the time goes)

ncu is not permitted (ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM), so: nsys per-kernel times, temporary instrumented variants that switch parts of the
main loop off, a DRAM stream probe, and arithmetic. Instrumentation patch (v1): `raw/base/exp-instrumentation.patch`.

nsys, v1 per call (`raw/dev/nsys-v2a.out`, `raw/base/v1-nsys-4096.out`):

| T | gate_up | down | reduce |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 | 1790 us | 1089 | 121 |
| 2048 | 2263 | 1565 | 284 |
| 4096 | 3672 | 2561 | 623 |

Variant bisect of v1 (bit 1 = no fp4 dequant, bit 2 = no mma, bit 4 = no loads issued in the loop; whole call, us;
`raw/base/v1-variants.out`):

| variant | T=2048 | T=4096 |
|---|---|---|
| v1 as is | 4264 | 7107 |
| no dequant | 3925 | 6098 |
| no mma | 3658 | 5423 |
| no loop loads | 3905 | 6651 |
| no dequant, no mma | 2539 | 4247 |
| none of the three | 1856 | 3130 |

Reading (inference from these timings, not a counter measurement): v1 is not simply DRAM bound. Dequant and mma each cost
15 to 25 % when present and they add up, because the dequantised bf16 tile goes through shared memory (write, barrier,
ldmatrix, mma) in one serial chain per k step and the 85 KB of shared memory leaves one block per SM, so nothing overlaps
that chain; and a floor of 1.9 / 3.1 ms remains with all three switched off (prologue, barriers, epilogue, reduce, which
also do not overlap a second block).

v2 (first version, before tuning; its temporary bisect hooks were removed again and are not kept, unlike v1's patch) bisect at T=2048 (`raw/dev/exp-v2-2048.out`, `raw/dev/exp-v2-loads.out`; bit 8 = no A
refill, bit 16 = no packed-weight refill; gate_up / down us):

| variant | gate_up | down |
|---|---|---|
| v2 as is | 2000 | 1210 |
| no dequant | 2023 | 1212 |
| no mma | 2061 | 1156 |
| no dequant, no mma | 2008 | 1141 |
| no dequant, no mma, no packed-weight refill | 861 | 806 |
| no dequant, no mma, no A refill | 1767 | 1050 |
| nothing in the loop | 746 | 700 |

In v2 the tensor-core and dequant work is fully hidden (removing both changes nothing); what remains is the packed-weight
cp.async stream. Weight bytes per layer call with all experts active: gate_up 268 MB + 33.5 MB scales, down 134 MB + 16.8 MB,
453 MB in all.

**DRAM probe** (`kernels/cuda_checks/stream_runs.cu`, `raw/base/stream-runs.out`, one read pass over 256 MB, units of
`run` contiguous bytes per 1 KB row): 32 B 65-72 GB/s, **64 B 172-176**, 128 B 167-171, 256 B 189-194, **512 B 258-259, 1024 B
259-260**. Every grouped kernel here (v1, v2, and the FP4 kernel) reads 64 bytes of each weight row per pipeline unit (k = 128
of an fp4 row), so the weight stream runs at the 64 B rate: 453 MB / 175 GB/s = 2.6 ms against 1.7 ms at 260 GB/s.

**What limits v2 depends on T** (inference from the numbers above, not a counter measurement):

* Up to T=2048 the weight stream dominates. v2 gate_up + down take 3.2 ms there (including A, h and y traffic), the FP4 kernel
  2.8 ms, both near the 64 B ceiling; removing the packed refill halves the loop; the contiguous-unit simulation (section 5)
  gains 11-16 % at 1024-2048; the FP4 kernel is only 6 % ahead of v2 at 2048.
* At T=4096 something else grows. The simulation gains only 3 %; in the (early, BM=128) v2 at 4096 removing the A refill
  (2553 -> 2082 us in gate_up) costs about as much as removing the packed refill (-> 1959); the FP4 kernel is 20 % ahead of
  v2 there against 6 % at 2048. Weight bytes do not grow with T, activation bytes do: the bf16 A tile is re-read from L2 once
  per column block, gate_up P x H x 2 B x (I / 64 = 8) and down P x I x 2 B x (H / 128 = 16), about 1.6 GB per call at T=4096
  and 0.8 GB at 2048 (P = 8 T pairs) against 453 MB of weights, where the FP4 kernel moves 4-bit activations (a quarter of
  those bytes). Together with the fixed floor (epilogue, reduce 0.62 ms at 4096, prologue) I expect the A re-reads, not the
  weight layout, to be the next limit at 4096. Not measured: a variant with A traffic removed at 4096 on the shipped
  configuration, or fewer column blocks per tile (wider blocks, which cost registers/shared memory).

## 3. What was built

`kernels/cuda/moe_w4a16.cu`, launcher `ik_moe_experts_w4a16_bf16` (declared in `kernels/cuda/ik_cuda_abi.h`, registered in
`crates/intarsia-backend-cuda/src/quantmoe.rs`, dispatch in `device_quantmoe.rs`). Same op, inputs, scratch layout
(`moe_experts_mma_scratch`) and grouping (`moe_group.cuh`) as v1. Per block: one (expert, up to BM pairs) tile times 64 gate
+ 64 up columns (gate_up) or 128 columns (down). What differs:

* **No bf16 weight tile in shared memory.** The staged packed bytes (64 B per row and 128-k unit) are read by the warp that
  owns those rows straight into registers; per k16 the B fragment is built by two `prmt`s (bf16 high bytes, low bytes), two
  to interleave, a sign multiply, and one `bf16x2` multiply by the block scale. Exact (e2m1 x e4m3 has at most 6 significant
  bits), the same bf16 values as v1's tile.
* **Scales travel through the cp.async ring**: 8 bytes per row and unit beside the 64 payload bytes (row stride 80 B, 16-byte
  reads conflict-free), instead of per-thread global loads.
* **Each warp owns distinct weight rows across all pairs of the tile** (no second warp along M repeating the dequant), and
  skips m16 tiles past the expert's last pair (block-uniform).
* **Smem 34 KB (BM=32) / 48 KB (BM=64) per block, 512/JW threads, two blocks per SM** (v1: 85 KB, one block). 124-166
  registers, no spills (`raw/final/microbench-and-tests.out`, "resources").
* Pipeline: A tile ring of 3 (one k64 step each), packed ring of 2 units, one `cp.async` group per k64 step with the wait
  for the group two back, one barrier per step (same shape as the FP4 kernel's ring).
* Tile height `BM` by mean pairs per expert (<= 56: 32, else 64; `INTARSIA_MOE_BM=32|64` overrides). BM=128 was slower.
* Numerics are v1's: each k16 product sum is formed from a zero accumulator by one mma and added to the running f32 sum by a
  normal fp32 add in k order; same epilogue (scale, SiLU-GLU with bf16 rounding points, tile staged through shared memory,
  gather/scatter by `pairs`). Nothing is reordered, hence bitwise v1.
* Registration: `moe_experts_w4a16_bf16`, `.when(m >= 1024 && experts <= 1024 && k % 128 == 0 && n % 128 == 0)`, priority 15
  (v1 10, FP4 opt-in 20). Kill switch `INTARSIA_CUDA_MOE_W4A16=0`. `kernels::tests::moe_experts_selection_by_batch_size`
  pins the choice at the 35B shape (w4a16 from 1024 tokens, v1 for 32..1023): an end-to-end accuracy gate cannot, because the
  kernels are bitwise equal (`raw/final/mac-selection-test.out`).

Optional compensated accumulation: **not done.** v2 keeps v1's plain fp32 add of the k16 results, which is the error source
of `docs/results/2026-10-17-moe-mma-small-t.md` (branch `acc/moe-mma-small-t`). In v2 the add is hidden (mma and dequant
removal changes nothing, above), so a compensated version would probably be cheap here (not measured), but it changes the
numerics, and that branch's variant moved 35b-long KL21 to 4.394e-2 and failed the prefix-reuse gate on two prompts; I kept
the task's bitwise-to-v1 bar.

## 5. Tried and dropped (kept in the log, not in the kernel)

Configuration sweeps `raw/dev/cfg-sweep{1,2,3}.out`, `bm-sweep1.out`, `pfs-sweep{1,2}.out`; everything bitwise v1.

* **Tile heights** (`raw/dev/bm-sweep1.out`, v2 whole call, T=1024 / 2048 / 4096): BM=32 2992 / 3546 / 6100, BM=64 3093 / 3596 /
  5324, BM=128 (8 m16 tiles per warp, one block per SM) 3208 / 3981 / 6662. Hence BM=32 up to 56 pairs per expert, 64 above;
  BM=128 dropped.
* **Deeper rings** (`cfg-sweep{2,3}.out`): a packed ring of 3 units (44 KB) is equal to 2 (no gain); 4 or 5 units (55-74 KB)
  leave one block per SM and take 4796-5540 us at T=2048 and 8179-9758 at T=4096. Dropped. (The FP4 doc found the same: 2
  stages best.)
* **Persistent grid with the cp.async ring running across work items** (one wave of blocks, items b, b+G, ..., epilogue from
  registers, next item's first stages requested during the last steps): 5249-5273 us at T=4096 (BM=64), within noise of the
  non-persistent 5345, worse at 1024 (3198-3232 against 3017). The local tag `w4a16-persistent-experiment` (commit a5bb569f, pre-squash; the tag is not pushed) holds the code.
* **L2 prefetch of the next item's weight rows in 1 KB contiguous runs** (four schedules, on the persistent kernel,
  `pfs-sweep{1,2}.out`): T=4096 5490-5990 us against 5273 without (+4 to +14 %), T=2048 3767-4294 against 3549 (+6 to
  +21 %); at T=1024 one schedule gave 2826-2834 against 3232 (-12 %) and the others 3295-4002. Not shippable. Wrong
  assumption: that the 64 B-run inefficiency can be repaired by pulling long runs into L2 ahead of the pipeline; the
  prefetch competes with the in-flight stream instead. This is the second failed attempt on the stream-efficiency idea, so I
  stopped there.
* **Layout simulation (timing only, results wrong)**: each row group's unit stored contiguously (4 KB for gate_up, 8 KB for
  down; scales too), as an offline Marlin-style re-layout would store it. Median of 3 rounds (`raw/final/microbench-and-tests.out`,
  `raw/dev/layout-sim.out`): T=1024 3000 -> 2533 us (-15.6 %), T=2048 3598 -> 3194 (-11.2 %), **T=4096 5356 -> 5188 (-3.1 %)**.
  So the stream-efficiency upper bound is 11-16 % at 1024-2048 and 3 % at 4096: not worth the cost below.
  A real re-layout would need a second copy of the expert weights (decode kernels, v1 and the FP4 kernel all read the
  original layout): +16 GB for the 35B (40 layers x 402 MB), or changing every consumer. Not done.

## 6. Marlin (vLLM W4A16 MoE) comparison

Sources fetched with `gh api` from `vllm-project/vllm` main on 2026-10-18 (commit sha not recorded):
`csrc/moe/marlin_moe_wna16/marlin_template.h`, `csrc/moe/marlin_moe_wna16/ops.cu`, `csrc/quantization/marlin/dequant.h`,
`csrc/quantization/marlin/marlin.cuh`. Read: `pipe_stages = 4` ("4 pipeline stages fit into shared memory",
marlin.cuh), `default_threads = 256`, thread tiles such as {128,128,256} / {64,256,256} (ops.cu), "Each threadblock
processes one 'stripe' of the B matrix" with a global reduce under barrier locks (marlin_template.h), the fp4 dequant
`dequant<half2/nv_bfloat162, kFE2M1f>` by mask-and-shift of the e2m1 bits into the 16-bit float fields followed by one
multiply for the exponent bias, with the comment "reverse indexing is intentional because weights are permuted". Not
verified from the fetched text: whether B fragments avoid `ldmatrix` entirely (ldmatrix helpers exist; their use for B was not
traced), the offline repack kernels (`gptq_marlin_repack.cu`, not read), and Marlin's measured bandwidth.

What I took: register dequant (no smem bf16 tile), scale applied in registers, cp.async multistage ring, smaller smem for more
blocks per SM. What I did not: the offline weight pre-permutation (which also makes each cp.async unit one long contiguous
run: by the probe of section 2 that is worth up to 1.5x on the stream, but the simulation shows only 3-11 % at T >= 2048 for
our kernel, and it needs a second weight copy), the 4-stage ring (slower here, section 5) and the stripe partition (tried as
the persistent grid, no gain).

* The 1024-token threshold leaves prompts under 1024 tokens (including the usual 1k bench prompt, 1006 tokens) on v1; only
  batches of 1024+ tokens (a 2048-token chunk is the production case) gain. Prefill chunks of 512 or 256 do not.
* The kernel is bitwise v1, so the old accumulation-error behaviour of v1 (section 3) is unchanged.
* Gain is about +8 % prefill at 4k/16k; vLLM's 6.5k tok/s is still 30 % ahead. Where the rest of the kernel gap sits is
  inference only (section 2): weight stream up to 2048 tokens, repeated bf16 activation reads plus the fixed floor at 4096. The
  contiguous-unit weight re-layout is worth 3 % at 4096 in the simulation, so do not start there; a +16 GB copy of the expert
  weights would be the price. The rest of the prefill (outside this kernel) was not investigated.
* The A/B ran 2 reps per size per round (the existing window script), not 5.
