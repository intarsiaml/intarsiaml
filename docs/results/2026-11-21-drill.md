# drill: cutover restart drill, intarsiaml 35B on spark2

Lane `drill`, measurement and docs only: no engine code and no default changed. Binary `drill-5d78422ae3f5` (origin/main `5d78422ae`, built on spark2, fleetq #10485), shipping 35B config `intarsia serve --backend cuda --model <nvidia/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4 snapshot 1355db6a> --port 8191 --host 0.0.0.0 --served-model-name guidance --max-seq 131072 --mem-budget 40 --profile-every 0 --max-batch 2 --prefix-store on`, no environment variables (current defaults: PREFAULT, KV once, MTP K=2; the copy drafts of #474 are mb1-only and not reached at `--max-batch 2`). Production vLLM on the same box was never touched (its 32,342 MiB stayed in `nvidia-smi` throughout). Drill job fleetq #10490 (`--mem 52 --expect 100`, 01:15 to 01:48 EST 2026-11-21, 33 min), smoke job #10487, build #10485.

## Summary

**Result: every drill passed; no server bug found (one measurement-client leniency in localeval, outside this repo and not fixed: it counts a stream that ends without `[DONE]` or `finish_reason` as ok, which hid 8 of the 12 requests cut by SIGKILL in the load client's own error count; see row 4).** Under steady 2-stream mixed load (greedy and T=1.0), the server stops cleanly on SIGTERM (in-flight work finishes, exit in 2.7 to 3.9 s, port closed at once), dies without leaving anything behind on SIGKILL (all three shapes: both processes, child only, supervisor only), returns all of its memory, restarts to a healthy `/health` in 70.7 to 76.9 s (mean 74.0 s over 7 shipping starts), comes back with a cold prefix store, and answers the 20 reference greedy requests byte-for-byte as before the drill (360 of 360 replies over 9 restarts, cold and warm pass).

What this means for a cutover: a restart of the 35B server costs about 75 s of connection-refused (no `Retry-After`, no 503 phase) plus a cold prefix store (the first request of each conversation prefills from scratch again: 1.5 s for 8k tokens), which a consumer has to ride out with its own retry (about 80 s at 2 s steps is enough); `intarsia serve`'s supervisor restarts a killed child by itself after 2 s plus the boot.

Things to know (none is a defect found in this drill):

- The graceful drain is bounded by the supervisor's grace of 30 s (`crates/intarsia-server/src/supervisor.rs`), after which the child gets SIGKILL and its in-flight streams are cut as in a hard kill. The drill's requests were short (at most 300 tokens, a 3.7 s drain); a generation that still has more than 30 s to run at the stop is cut. The case was not measured: a stage for it was prepared (`ONLY='S10|on|term sup' SKIPVERIFY=1 LONGTOK=12000` in `scripts/drill/drill.sh`, a 12,000-token generation in flight at the SIGTERM; the options were added after the main run and have not been exercised) but its fleetq job (#10492) waited behind another lane's GPU job with a start estimate over an hour away and was cancelled before it ran. Measured afterwards in [`2026-11-21-drill2.md`](2026-11-21-drill2.md) (spark1, 3 of 3: child SIGKILLed at 30.0 to 30.1 s, streams cut with no `finish_reason` or `[DONE]`, port free in 0.09 s, nothing left). Expected, from the code: the supervisor's SIGKILL at 30 s, port free at once, the stream cut as in the hard kill. A container runtime's default stop timeout (10 s) or a systemd unit's is a second limit if the server is ever run that way.
- The shipping store is RAM only: every restart is cold (row 3). The disk tier exists (`--prefix-store host_mb=...,disk_mb=...,dir=...`, "survives restarts of the same build") and recovered cleanly after SIGKILL in the one drill run here (S7 to S8), but it is not the shipping config and one kill is a small sample (whether a write was in flight at the kill is chance).
- No container kill drill: the 35B server on spark2 is a bare process here (the only container on the box is the production vLLM, which was left alone). SIGKILL of both processes is the same effect.
- Signal-to-exit times are taken on spark2 itself at 50 ms polling (`scripts/drill/ctl.sh`); the client-side times use this Mac's clock, converted with a measured offset (spark2 minus Mac 0.15 to 0.27 s, round trip 0.35 to 0.58 s), so they are good to about 0.3 s.
- The outage has three separate parts: exit (0.07 s after SIGKILL, 3.7 to 4.7 s after SIGTERM), the drill's own gap between the kill and the next launch (4.6 s after SIGKILL, 8 to 9 s after SIGTERM: the controller waits for the +3 s memory snapshot and an ssh round trip), and the boot (70.7 to 76.9 s). A supervisor restart has no controller gap (S5: new child after 2.0 s, healthy 75.7 s after the kill).

## Method

- **Chain.** One script (`scripts/drill/drill.sh`, run under fleetq #10490) walks a chain of servers S0 to S9 on spark2 (`scripts/drill/ctl.sh` runs on the box, only ever signals a PID whose `/proc/<pid>/exe` is our binary, and times everything locally). Each server is started (launch to listening, to `/health` 200, to first content token), put through the 20-request verification twice (cold store, then warm), put under load, and killed the way its row says; the next server's start is the restart under test and the consumer keeps running through it. Stage plan: S0 SIGTERM supervisor, S1 SIGTERM supervisor and child (systemd control-group style), S2 to S4 SIGKILL both (x3), S5 SIGKILL child only (the supervisor restarts it, the server that comes up is S6), S6 SIGTERM, S7 and S8 with the disk tier (`host_mb=8192,disk_mb=20000,dir=...`; S7 SIGKILL both, S8 SIGKILL supervisor only), S9 shipping again, SIGTERM.
- **Load** (the existing client): `scripts/soak2/replay2.py`, 2 streams, interleaved, `--temp-mode mixed` (conversations 0, 1, 4, 5 greedy, 2, 3, 6, 7 at T=1.0), the soak's plan, 8k to 33k-token multi-turn prompts, 160 tokens, a fresh seed per stage (101 to 110) so no prompt is replayed on a server that holds it. Started 40 s before the signal (both streams in flight), one row per request.
- **Consumer** (`scripts/drill/consumer.py`, OpenAI Python SDK with a long-lived client per thread, default `max_retries=2` and its backoff): every 2 s a streaming 16-token chat call, plus one 300-token streaming call kept in flight; started 8 s before the signal, stopped 10 s after the next server's first healthy `/health`; one JSON line per call with attempts (httpx request hook), outcome and exception. It adds a third and fourth client to the two load streams for the last seconds (queue limit 64, no 429 seen).
- **Verification** (`scripts/drill/verify.py`, `cmp.py`): 20 greedy requests (temperature 0, 100 tokens, thinking off, one at a time): conversations 0 to 2 of the ab35e replay (seed 0, plan and vLLM-recorded history from `docs/results/2026-11-20-ab35e-cutover-scoreboard/raw/`), turns 1 to 6, and conversation 3 turns 1 and 2; 8k to 33k-token prompts. Pass 1 runs on the cold store (turns of a conversation hit the entries stored by its earlier turns), pass 2 right after, fully warm. Replies are compared as the raw streamed text, plus every request's `cached_tokens`.
- **Memory and leaks**: a `before-all` snapshot (`free -m`, `nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps`, port, `/dev/shm`, our processes) before the first launch and after every kill at +0 and +3 s; a 2 s `MemAvailable` watcher would have killed our processes below 15 GB (lowest 37.9 GB, never triggered). Another tenant was on the box (the production vLLM, and untracked GPU work, `fleetq-spark2-before-kill-*.txt`), so GPU-memory claims rest on the process list and the compute-apps list, not on one total.
- **Pass criteria fixed beforehand**: byte identity of pass 1 vs S0 pass 1 and of pass 2 vs S0 pass 2, plus equal per-request `cached_tokens` on pass 1 (the proof of a cold store); restore identity (pass 1 vs pass 2 on one server) is a separate property and `cached_tokens` differ between those two by design; a request cut by SIGKILL must surface as an error or an absent `finish_reason`, a SIGTERM request must end `stop` or `length`; a "graceful" exit near 30 s would mean a forced kill.
- **Smoke run** (#10487, two stages) validated the scripts first; its raw files are not part of the results. An idle check before each verification (`running + waiting = 0` on `/metrics`) was 0 s every time.
