Forecast for step 2: the existing full-model TP=2 trace (`dsv41-graphs/.scratch/full-w9/ab1`, rank 0) has 40 chains a pass, so -0.8 ms a pass needs -20 us a chain. The best class here gives -9 us, and the verify classes give -5 to +21 us. On those figures, 40 chains give at most -0.37 ms a pass (extrapolated, not measured). ## Which assumption failed

The hypothesis (Fable report section 1.3, D2) was that TF's `griddepcontrol` launch on the pair explains TF's 70 us `down` lead and a 0.8 ms or larger saving for us. Three findings, the first two read from these traces:

1. Off, there is nothing between the kernels to hide. Gate/up + epilogue + down already equals the chain (297 + 4 + 151 = 452 against 451 us, 1 row), and the epilogue is 4 to 8 us.
2. On, `down` overlaps only 7 to 25 us of gate/up, and its heavy part still waits. Down's recorded time grows by 11 to 25 us and epilogue's by 11 to 35 us (time in the wait), while the chain does not move. Before the wait, a program can only issue its first ring stages of weights (S - 1 steps, a few tiles).
3. TF's overlap uses more than the attribute (read: `zig/src/families/dsv41/exl3_experts.zig:406-415`). Its epilogue is inside the gate/up kernel (`DecodeEpi` on `gu`), and a `down` program waits on its own place's `ready == epoch`, not on a whole-grid `griddepcontrol.wait`. So TF's `down` can do real work for finished experts while other experts' gate/up still runs. Ours has to wait for the whole gate/up grid and then the whole epilogue grid. Whether TF's 70 us buys time there is not separable from its trace.

Inferred, not measured: the lead is bounded by one gate/up program, because `launch_dependents` releases the dependent only after every block has started, and split K (z = 16 for the draft block) makes our programs short.

Not done, as the brief says: per-expert ready flags, a fused epilogue, PDL on other kernels. Those would be a new design, with no estimate from here.
