# memmix: leak or allocator retention in the mixed-traffic host-memory creep? (2026-11-20)

Lane `memmix`, morning of 2026-11-20, spark1 (main run) and spark2 (arms), vLLM up beside the server on both and never touched. intarsiaml at origin/main `4ae934a5f` (binary stamp `memmix-4ae934a5f9d8`, built on each box: fleetq #10376 spark1, #10377 spark2), `serve --backend cuda --model <Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4 snapshot 1355db6a> --max-seq 131072 --mem-budget 40 --profile-every 0 --max-batch 2 --prefix-store on`, nothing else set (memgrow's command line). No engine change, no default change.

## Summary

**Verdict: allocator retention (glibc heap fragmentation around the prefix store's host buffers), not a leak of live data. The retained part is still creeping slowly after 150 minutes and a plateau was not shown; periodic `malloc_trim(0)` bounds it.** In 150 minutes of memgrow's mixed traffic (2,440 requests, 0 failures) the live heap is flat, the resident floor after a `malloc_trim(0)` is flat, and what grows is memory that malloc has freed and keeps.

- **Live heap flat.** glibc's own accounting (`malloc_info`: arena read-write part minus free chunks plus directly mmapped chunks, every 5 minutes) says the process has **9,397 to 9,441 MB allocated** from minute 6 to minute 150 (9,421 MB at minute 15, 9,435 MB at minute 150). Fits from minute 30: **+5 +- 3 MB/h** over the 28 distinct blocks (+2 +- 4 on the 19 periodic blocks, +10 +- 4 on the 9 after-trim blocks): at most about 0.4 GB per day at 2 SE, 50 to 100 times below memgrow's +0.5 GB/h; a real residue of that size is not excluded. The server's descriptor count (39 to 41), thread count (28 to 33), prefix-store entries (88 to 136) and host objects (1,538 to 2,324) are flat for the whole run (fit from minute 30: 0 +- 0 fds/h, -0.4 +- 0.9 entries/h), so a socket or handle leak on the abort path and a growing store index are both absent.
- **Floor flat.** Right after each `malloc_trim(0)` (called inside the engine every 15 minutes, `RssAnon` read before and after in the engine itself) `RssAnon` is **10,312 MB at minute 15 (fill still ending), then 10,416 / 10,392 / 10,435 / 10,402 / 10,390 / 10,417 / 10,415 / 10,432 / 10,455 MB** at minutes 30 to 150: +17 +- 10 MB/h from minute 30 (39 MB in two hours). A leak would raise this floor.
- **The creep is retention.** `RssAnon` just before each trim is 11,281 MB at minute 15 and 11.6 to 12.0 GB later; the trim hands back **968 MB (minute 15), 1.18 to 1.35 GB (minutes 30 to 90), 1.56 GB (minute 105) and 1.40 to 1.43 GB (minutes 120, 135, 150)**. The part that trims do not reset is still moving: the arenas' committed size is 11.14 GB at minute 30, 11.3 to 11.8 GB over the last hour (11.55 GB at the end) and fits **+190 +- 36 MB/h from minute 30, +243 +- 50 from minute 60, +98 +- 100 from minute 90** (slowing, not shown to be flat); free chunks in the arenas (freed, not coalesced into a heap top, so not given back by glibc itself) went 2.27 GB (minute 30), peaked at 2.90 GB (minute 115) and are 2.4 to 2.7 GB over the last 30 minutes. memgrow's "+0.5 GB/h with no plateau" in run B is the first hour of this fill (non-store anon, 30 s samples, 15-minute means: 3.0 GB in the first 15 minutes, 3.15 to 3.58 GB with no trend in minutes 15 to 150; the trims clip the peaks).
- **No request kind grows it.** Arms of 25 minutes each (spark2): **aborts only** (1,678 requests, 922 mid-decode and 756 mid-prefill socket closes): floor 10,051 to 10,059 MB, live heap 9,282 to 9,284 MB, trim hands back 0.36 to 0.51 GB per 5 minutes; **tool calls only** (2,148 requests): floor 9,922 to 9,924 MB, live heap 9,341 to 9,345 MB, 1 to 27 MB handed back per 5 minutes after the first (169 MB). The tools arm is also the best in-lane evidence for the mechanism: there the store's 8.4 GB sits in 8.4 GB of directly mmapped chunks (arena live 0.9 GB) and nothing is retained; in the main run the mmapped chunks fall to 550 MB, about 8.9 GB of live data is carved from the arenas, and 1.4 GB is retained per cycle (consistent with the store buffers' pattern causing it; not proven by an intervention here). No bisect to long prompts was needed (the floor never rose); that arm was not run.
- **Un-trimmed ceiling.** An un-trimmed server holds at most its live heap plus the committed free space in its arenas plus non-malloc anonymous memory: at the end 11.55 GB of arena heaps (the main arena included) plus 0.55 GB of directly mmapped chunks plus at most 1.0 GB outside malloc (floor 10.46 GB minus live 9.44 GB), about 13.1 GB of `RssAnon`, and the committed part is still rising by about 0.1 to 0.2 GB/h. With a trim every 15 minutes `RssAnon` stays between the floor (10.4 GB) and floor + 1.4 GB. MemAvailable beside vLLM in this run: 43.1 GB in the first 15 minutes, 42.6 to 43.0 GB after, lowest sample 40.7 GB; **without trims expect about 1.4 to 2.5 GB less, about 38 GB** (memgrow's lowest was 38.8 GB).
- **Code reading (read-only sub-agent, not a profile; I checked the host buffer functions myself): no per-request map, queue or cache that is only cleaned on the normal-completion path.** Abort and disconnect go through a `CancelGuard` and the scheduler's reaping of queued and running sequences, `tool` and `json_schema` state lives in per-request parsers, the store drops objects and dependent entries on eviction. The allocation pattern that matches the retention is the store's host buffers: `vec![0u8; n]` per new object, many sizes from 1 to 30 MB, freed on evict and demote, recycled only for the same size (`crates/intarsia-core/src/prefix_store.rs:2237` `host_buf`, `:1570` `release_host`, `:1581` `take_host_buf`). No suspect for a leak; no fix written.
- **`MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` does not flatten it** (section 3, 25 minutes, trims every 8 minutes, spark1 against spark2 so indicative): arenas 19 to 2, resident floor 0.55 GB lower (9,410 to 9,418 MB against 9,955 to 9,989 MB), but the trim still hands back 0.8 to 1.4 GB per cycle (1,428 / 811 / 1,173 MB against 1,338 / 867 / 1,192 MB); store insert 58.3 against 57.4 ms and settle 33.7 against 36.5 ms per request (no slowdown visible, one run each). `MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_` was not run (section 5).
- **`INTARSIA_PREFIX_STORE_PREFAULT=1` removes the retention in a short arm** (17 minutes, trims at 8 and 17 minutes, spark1, 311 requests): the trim hands back **210 and 37 MB** per cycle against 1,338 / 867 MB without it, the floor is the same (9,960 / 9,899 MB against 9,955 to 9,989 MB), `RssAnon` just before the trims is 10,170 / 9,936 MB against 11,293 / 10,842 MB (about 1 GB lower), store insert 47.4 against 57.4 ms and settle 28.1 against 36.5 ms per request (no slowdown visible; cross-box, one short run, a different request mix: 8 long prompts against 16). The knob gives every new store host buffer a mapping of its own, outside glibc's arenas: the mechanism the tools-only arm and the main run's contrast pointed to. Two trims are a signal, not a trend; hours of behaviour with it were not measured.

**What to do.** Nothing is needed for the cutover on memory grounds: against 24 GB of margin the footprint (about 13 GB of anonymous memory, 27.4 GB of GPU memory, about 38 GB MemAvailable left beside vLLM) is harmless for a week; the creep of the committed free space is the one thing a days-long run could still show, and it is monitored by the sampler (`scripts/memmix/sampler.sh`, 30 s, no root) and cut by a trim. If the margin is ever needed, in order of cost: (1) `malloc_trim(0)` from the engine every 15 to 30 minutes (this run: 94 to 231 ms per call with an 8 GiB store, which stalls any thread that allocates meanwhile, so call it between requests; returns 1.0 to 1.6 GB and resets the creep's resident part); (2) `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` in the service environment: 0.55 GB lower resident floor, no visible store cost, no effect on the retention or the creep, lock contention between two arenas not measured, so a footprint trim and not a fix; (3) the opt-in `INTARSIA_PREFIX_STORE_PREFAULT=1` (page-aligned mappings of their own for new host buffers, which glibc never sees) attacks the cause: retention 1.3 GB to 0.04 to 0.21 GB per cycle in the 17-minute arm at the same floor and no store slowdown. It is the one to soak for hours before anything is recommended (a 6 to 24 h run with the sampler, trims off, would show the un-trimmed plateau and whether the knob holds); its default is the user's call and it is not flipped here. Nothing was flipped.

## Method

**What is compared.** memgrow (PR #468, `docs/results/2026-11-20-memgrow.md`) measured the shipping config's host memory under mixed traffic (its run B: 996 requests of 0.5k to 93k tokens, aborts mid-prefill and mid-decode, tool calls, json_schema, thinking, 60 minutes) and found the engine's anonymous memory beyond the store creeping +0.5 GB/h (2.3 to 5.4 SE) without a plateau; one `malloc_trim(0)` at the end of its run D handed 1.1 GB back. The two readings are a leak (live data that grows) or glibc retention (freed pages the allocator keeps, fragmentation). They predict different things after a `malloc_trim(0)`: retention falls back to a flat floor, a leak keeps a floor that rises with the live data.

**Server, load, sampler.** The binary, flags, 30 s sampler (`sampler.sh`: `/proc/meminfo`, the engine's `status` and `smaps_rollup`, per-process GPU memory, vLLM's cgroup, the server's `/metrics`), watchdog (kill the server below 15 GB MemAvailable, polled every 20 s; 0 kills) and load generator are memgrow's, copied into `scripts/memmix/` (PR #468 is not merged; the copies carry one change, `mixed.py --weights`, which picks the request kinds of an arm and leaves the default draw sequence untouched, so the main run is seed 7 of memgrow's run B, same sequence of requests). Load on endor against the Spark, 2 streams. Nothing else was changed in the engine.

**The trim probe** (`scripts/memmix/libmemstat.c`, LD_PRELOADed into the server, memgrow's helper plus two things): on a trigger file it calls `malloc_trim(0)` inside the engine and prints, from the engine's own `/proc/self/status`, `RssAnon` and `VmRSS` immediately before and after (line `[memtrim]` in the serve log, so no 30 s sampling skew), and `malloc_info()` (64-bit XML) before, after and every 5 minutes: per arena the read-write part of the heap, the free chunks, and the directly mmapped chunks. `run.sh` touches the trigger every TRIMEVERY minutes of load and once more at the end of the load. Reading the three numbers: **RssAnon before trim** (what the process holds), **RssAnon after trim** (the floor: live data, partly used pages, non-malloc anonymous memory), **live total** = arena read-write part minus free chunks plus mmapped chunks (what malloc thinks is allocated; it does not change at a trim, because `malloc_trim` hands pages back to the OS but the chunks stay free chunks). A leak raises the live total and the floor; retention raises only the difference between before and after. The store's host tier (8 GiB budget, about 8.4 GB resident) is inside the live total, so slopes are taken of the live total itself, not of a difference to the store's gauge (the gauge swings 0.2 to 0.6 GB between samples and puts that noise into any difference).

Each run: 45 to 90 s of base (vLLM only), server start, 1 to 2 min idle, the load, a final trim, 1 to 5 min idle, stop, 0.5 to 2 min after. fleetq slot for every job (builds `--load cpu --mem 55`, runs `--mem 52`), FLEETQ_OWNER `claude-overnight-memmix`, never held beyond a run. spark1's earlyoom fires at 2 %, so the watchdog line is 15 GB; the lowest MemAvailable seen is listed per run below.

**What the arms were for.** The brief's step 2 (bisect by request kind) applies if the floor rises. Arms `aborts` and `tools` ran early, in parallel with the main run on spark2, as insurance. The `long` and `rest` arms were dropped once the main run's first hour showed a flat floor and a flat live total (decision at 07:12, before they started); see the verdict.

Decode speed was not measured (a memory run on a loaded Spark, no matched ABBA); with 2 arenas, threads that allocate concurrently (store settle, tokenizer, HTTP) share two arena locks, and that contention is not measured here.
- Clocks: SM 2,333 to 2,405 MHz (base), 2,301 to 2,463 MHz (amax) and 2,294 to 2,457 MHz (prefault) at more than 50 % utilisation, SW Power Capping counter unchanged over all three loads.

**`INTARSIA_PREFIX_STORE_PREFAULT=1` (17 minutes of load, 311 requests, 0 bad; trims at 8 and 17 minutes; fleetq #10405, started after the other arms because spark2's queue would not have reached it before 10:00):** the trim hands back **210 MB and 37 MB** per cycle (against 1,338 / 867 MB without it and 1,428 / 811 MB with 2 arenas), `RssAnon` just before the trims is 10,170 and 9,936 MB and the floor 9,960 and 9,899 MB (the same floor as the base arm, 9,955 to 9,989 MB). With the knob the store's new host buffers are mappings of their own, so glibc's accounting no longer contains them (live total 1.2 to 1.3 GB, arenas 1.4 GB, 19 arenas): the retained 1.3 GB per cycle of the default allocation path is gone, which is the intervention that the tools-only arm and the main run's mmapped-versus-arena contrast pointed to. Store cost, indicative only (cross-box, one run each, a shorter arm with a different request count): 47.4 ms per insert (1,285 inserts) against 57.4 ms, settle 28.1 ms per request against 36.5 ms, so no slowdown (storeprefault, #467, measured it faster). Two trims in 17 minutes on one box is a signal, not a trend: it does not show how the knob behaves over hours (huge-page mappings, free-time `munmap`s with the 8 GiB store), and `MemAvailable` is not comparable across boxes.



### 4. Why this is retention and not a leak, and what is not shown

1. **Live data is flat.** What malloc has handed out and not had back is 9,421 MB (minute 15) and 9,435 MB (minute 150), +5 +- 3 MB/h (at most about 0.4 GB/day at 2 SE across the three fits), over blocks that range from 9,397 to 9,441 MB. Descriptors, threads, store entries and host objects do not move.
2. **The floor after a trim is flat**, 10.39 to 10.46 GB for two hours (+17 +- 10 MB/h).
3. **What grows is the freed-but-held part**, which a trim returns: 1.0 to 1.6 GB per 15 minutes. The tools-only arm (store buffers in direct mmaps, retention about 0) against the main run (buffers carved from the arenas, 1.4 GB per cycle) is the in-lane contrast that points at the store's host buffers; memgrow's run D (1.1 GB returned by one trim) and its run E (pinned mmap threshold, 3.2 GB less anonymous memory) are the same effect from the other side.
4. **Does it level off? Not shown.** Trims reset the resident part every 15 minutes, so the un-trimmed plateau cannot be read from this run. The quantity that a trim does not reset is the arenas' committed size: 11.14 GB at minute 30, 11.55 GB at minute 150, +190 +- 36 MB/h from minute 30 and +98 +- 100 MB/h from minute 90, i.e. slowing, not flat within SE. The retained part per cycle did level (1.40 to 1.43 GB for the last three trims, 1.56 GB at minute 105) and the free chunks fell back from 2.9 GB to 2.4 to 2.7 GB, but 150 minutes is too short to call a plateau. Upper bound for an un-trimmed `RssAnon` at the end: 11.55 GB arenas + 0.55 GB mmapped chunks + at most 1.0 GB outside malloc = about 13.1 GB, rising at most 0.1 to 0.2 GB/h; with a trim every 15 minutes it stays at floor + 1.4 GB (about 11.9 GB). Days of uptime are not covered.
5. **The code reading finds no cleanup that only the normal path performs** (section 5 lists what was checked and what was not), and the flat descriptor count is a direct measurement of the abort path (255 mid-decode and 187 mid-prefill closes in the main run).

### 5. Not shown, not done

- **No days of uptime.** 150 minutes of synthetic traffic (memgrow's generator; nothing is real missioncntrl traffic, thinking on half the time, 2 streams). A residue of +2 to +10 MB/h in the live heap (up to about 0.4 GB/day at 2 SE) is not excluded, and its cause was not separated (store entries and objects, descriptors and threads are flat). Harmless for a week against 24 GB of margin; over months it would matter.
- **The trims change the allocator's state** (every 15 minutes the resident retention is reset), so the un-trimmed plateau is argued from the quantities trims do not touch (the arenas' committed size and the free chunks), not measured here.
- **The `long` and `rest` bisect arms did not run** (not needed once the floor was flat); no bisect to a request kind was possible because no kind grows the floor. The aborts and tools arms are 25 minutes, 5 trims each: fits over so few points are not reported as trends.
- **`MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_` was not run, and `INTARSIA_PREFIX_STORE_PREFAULT=1` only for 17 minutes with two trims** (spark2's queue was full of other lanes' jobs; the prefault arm ran on spark1 after the others). On `MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_` (per the mallopt(3) documentation, not measured here): setting the trim threshold also turns off glibc's dynamic mmap threshold, so on its own it leaves the mmap threshold at its 128 KiB default and every 1 to 30 MB store buffer becomes a fresh mmap, close to memgrow's run E (a 1 MiB pin; insert 44 vs 27 ms, settle 111 vs 28 ms per request). Pairing it with `MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=33554432` keeps the buffers in arenas, which only trims heap tops earlier and does not release holes in the middle; not tried.
- **Code reading is a sub-agent's read-only audit** (no profiler): `CancelGuard` in `crates/intarsia-server/src/api.rs:58`, queued and running reaping in `crates/intarsia-core/src/scheduler.rs:1194` to `:1211`, `engine/batch.rs:1014` to `:1060`, the per-request grammar clone in `structured.rs:720`, the store's `remove_entry` / `drop_obj` (`prefix_store.rs:1598`, `:1628`). I read `host_buf`, `release_host` and `take_host_buf` myself; the rest is not independently checked. llguidance's internal caches, the CUDA backend's pinned host allocations and per-`Seq` host buffers were not audited.
- **Clock guard:** these are memory runs, no timing ratio is published. For the env arms the SM clock stayed at 2,333 to 2,420 MHz and the SW Power Capping counter did not move between load start and load end.
