# memmix: leak or allocator retention in the mixed-traffic host-memory creep? (2026-11-20)

Lane `memmix`, morning of 2026-11-20, spark1 (main run) and spark2 (arms), vLLM up beside the server on both and never touched. intarsiaml at origin/main `4ae934a5f` (binary stamp `memmix-4ae934a5f9d8`, built on each box: fleetq #10376 spark1, #10377 spark2), `serve --backend cuda --model <Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4 snapshot 1355db6a> --max-seq 131072 --mem-budget 40 --profile-every 0 --max-batch 2 --prefix-store on`, nothing else set (memgrow's command line). No engine change, no default change.

## Summary

**Verdict: allocator retention (glibc heap fragmentation around the prefix store's host buffers), not a leak, and it levels off. In 150 minutes of memgrow's mixed traffic (2,440 requests, 0 bad) the live heap is flat, the resident floor after a `malloc_trim(0)` is flat, and what grows is memory that malloc has freed and keeps; that part grew for about 100 minutes and has sat at about 1.4 GB per 15 minutes since.**

- **Live heap flat.** glibc's own accounting (`malloc_info`: arena read-write part minus free chunks plus directly mmapped chunks, every 5 minutes, 41 blocks) says the process has **9,400 to 9,441 MB allocated** from minute 15 to minute 150: 9,421 MB at minute 15, 9,435 MB at minute 150 (fit from minute 30: +6 +- 2 MB/h, about 0.15 GB per day, about 80 times below memgrow's +0.5 GB/h; whether that residue is a real per-request leak or store metadata was not separated).
- **Floor flat.** Right after each `malloc_trim(0)` (called inside the engine every 15 minutes, `RssAnon` read before and after in the engine itself), `RssAnon` is **10,312 MB at minute 15 (fill still ending), then 10,416 / 10,392 / 10,435 / 10,402 / 10,390 / 10,417 / 10,415 / 10,432 / 10,455 MB** at minutes 30 to 150: +17 +- 10 MB/h from minute 30 (39 MB in two hours). A leak would raise this floor; it did not move.
- **The creep is retention.** `RssAnon` just before each trim is 11,281 MB at minute 15 and 11.6 to 12.0 GB later; the trim hands back **968 MB (minute 15), 1.18 to 1.35 GB (minutes 30 to 90), 1.56 GB (minute 105) and 1.40 to 1.43 GB (minutes 120, 135, 150)**. The free chunks in the arenas (freed, not coalesced into the top of a heap, so not given back by glibc itself) went 2.27 GB (minute 30), peaked at 2.90 GB (minute 115) and are 2.4 to 2.7 GB over the last 30 minutes; the arenas' committed size is 11.1 GB at minute 30 and 11.3 to 11.8 GB over the last hour (11.5 GB at the end). memgrow's "+0.5 GB/h with no plateau" in run B is the first hour of this fill: here the non-store anon (RssAnon minus the store tier, 30 s samples, 15-minute means) is 3.0 GB in the first 15 minutes and 3.15 to 3.58 GB with no trend in minutes 15 to 150 (trims every 15 minutes clip the peaks).
- **No request kind grows it.** Arms of 25 minutes each (spark2): **aborts only** (1,678 requests, 922 mid-decode and 756 mid-prefill socket closes): floor 10,051 to 10,059 MB, live heap 9,282 to 9,284 MB, trim hands back 0.36 to 0.51 GB per 5 minutes; **tool calls only** (2,148 requests): floor 9,922 to 9,924 MB, live heap 9,341 to 9,345 MB, 1 to 27 MB handed back per 5 minutes after the first (169 MB). No bisect to long prompts was needed (the floor never rose); that arm was not run.
- **Un-trimmed ceiling.** An un-trimmed server cannot hold more than its live heap plus the free pages in its arenas plus non-malloc anonymous memory: 10.4 GB floor plus up to the 2.5 to 2.9 GB of free chunks, about 13 GB of `RssAnon` at worst, the same as memgrow's run A plateau (12.85 GB). MemAvailable beside vLLM in this run: 43.1 GB in the first 15 minutes, 42.6 to 43.0 GB after, lowest sample 40.7 GB (trims every 15 minutes clip the peaks; they do not change the footprint of the live data).
- **Code reading (read-only sub-agent, not a profile; I checked the host buffer functions myself): no per-request map, queue or cache that is only cleaned on the normal-completion path.** Abort and disconnect go through a `CancelGuard` and the scheduler's reaping of queued and running sequences, `tool` and `json_schema` state lives in per-request parsers, the store drops objects and dependent entries on eviction. The allocation pattern that matches the retention is the store's host buffers: `vec![0u8; n]` per new object, many sizes from 1 to 30 MB, freed on evict and demote, recycled only for the same size (`crates/intarsia-core/src/prefix_store.rs:2237` `host_buf`, `:1570` `release_host`, `:1581` `take_host_buf`). No suspect for a leak; no fix written.
- @@AMAX_SUMMARY@@

**What to do.** Nothing is needed for the cutover on memory grounds: the footprint is bounded (about 13 GB of anonymous memory plus 27.4 GB of GPU memory, 40 GB of MemAvailable left beside vLLM). If the margin is ever needed, in order of cost: (1) `malloc_trim(0)` from the engine every 15 to 30 minutes (this run: 94 to 231 ms per call with an 8 GiB store, which is a stall of any thread that allocates meanwhile, so call it between requests; returns 1.0 to 1.6 GB); @@AMAX_REC@@ (3) the opt-in `INTARSIA_PREFIX_STORE_PREFAULT=1` (page-aligned mappings of their own for new host buffers, which glibc never sees) is the candidate that attacks the cause, and it was not measured here (the spark2 slot did not come up in time; see section 5). Nothing was flipped.

## Method

**What is compared.** memgrow (PR #468, `docs/results/2026-11-20-memgrow.md`) measured the shipping config's host memory under mixed traffic (its run B: 996 requests of 0.5k to 93k tokens, aborts mid-prefill and mid-decode, tool calls, json_schema, thinking, 60 minutes) and found the engine's anonymous memory beyond the store creeping +0.5 GB/h (2.3 to 5.4 SE) without a plateau; one `malloc_trim(0)` at the end of its run D handed 1.1 GB back. The two readings are a leak (live data that grows) or glibc retention (freed pages the allocator keeps, fragmentation). They predict different things after a `malloc_trim(0)`: retention falls back to a flat floor, a leak keeps a floor that rises with the live data.

**Server, load, sampler.** The binary, flags, 30 s sampler (`sampler.sh`: `/proc/meminfo`, the engine's `status` and `smaps_rollup`, per-process GPU memory, vLLM's cgroup, the server's `/metrics`), watchdog (kill the server below 15 GB MemAvailable, polled every 20 s; 0 kills) and load generator are memgrow's, copied into `scripts/memmix/` (PR #468 is not merged; the copies carry one change, `mixed.py --weights`, which picks the request kinds of an arm and leaves the default draw sequence untouched, so the main run is seed 7 of memgrow's run B, same sequence of requests). Load on endor against the Spark, 2 streams. Nothing else was changed in the engine.

**The trim probe** (`scripts/memmix/libmemstat.c`, LD_PRELOADed into the server, memgrow's helper plus two things): on a trigger file it calls `malloc_trim(0)` inside the engine and prints, from the engine's own `/proc/self/status`, `RssAnon` and `VmRSS` immediately before and after (line `[memtrim]` in the serve log, so no 30 s sampling skew), and `malloc_info()` (64-bit XML) before, after and every 5 minutes: per arena the read-write part of the heap, the free chunks, and the directly mmapped chunks. `run.sh` touches the trigger every TRIMEVERY minutes of load and once more at the end of the load. Reading the three numbers: **RssAnon before trim** (what the process holds), **RssAnon after trim** (the floor: live data, partly used pages, non-malloc anonymous memory), **live total** = arena read-write part minus free chunks plus mmapped chunks (what malloc thinks is allocated; it does not change at a trim, because `malloc_trim` hands pages back to the OS but the chunks stay free chunks). A leak raises the live total and the floor; retention raises only the difference between before and after. The store's host tier (8 GiB budget, about 8.4 GB resident) is inside the live total, so slopes are taken of the live total itself, not of a difference to the store's gauge (the gauge swings 0.2 to 0.6 GB between samples and puts that noise into any difference).

### 4. Why this is retention and not a leak

1. **Live data is flat.** The only quantity that a leak must raise is what malloc has handed out and not had back, and that is 9,421 MB (minute 15) and 9,435 MB (minute 150), +6 +- 2 MB/h, over 41 blocks that range from 9,397 to 9,441 MB.
2. **The floor after a trim is flat**, 10.39 to 10.46 GB for two hours.
3. **What grows is the freed-but-held part**, which a trim returns: 1.0 to 1.6 GB per 15 minutes, with the free chunks in the arenas peaking at 2.9 GB and 2.4 to 2.7 GB afterwards. Run D of memgrow (1.1 GB returned by one trim) and this run's 10 trims are the same effect.
4. **It levels off, but this run cannot show the un-trimmed plateau directly**, because the trims reset the resident part every 15 minutes. What the trims do not reset (arena commit 11.3 to 11.8 GB over the last hour, free chunks 2.4 to 2.7 GB over the last 30 minutes, retention per cycle 1.40 to 1.43 GB in the last three) is flat or slowly moving at the end; memgrow's run A (no trims, 105 minutes) plateaued at 12.85 GB of anonymous memory, which is this run's floor plus about 2.5 GB. A fragmentation creep at +0.2 GB/h that stops after about 100 minutes is what the data show; days of uptime are not covered.
5. **The code reading finds no cleanup that only the normal path performs** (section 5 lists what was checked and what was not).

### 5. Not shown, not done

- **No days of uptime.** 150 minutes of synthetic traffic (memgrow's generator; nothing is real missioncntrl traffic, thinking on half the time, 2 streams). A residue of +6 MB/h in the live heap (0.15 GB per day) is inside what this run cannot separate from store metadata noise, and a leak far below that would not show.
- **The trims change the allocator's state** (every 15 minutes the resident retention is reset), so the un-trimmed plateau is argued from the quantities trims do not touch and from memgrow's run A, not measured here.
- **The `long` and `rest` bisect arms did not run** (not needed once the floor was flat); no bisect to a request kind was possible because no kind grows the floor. The aborts and tools arms are 25 minutes, 5 trims each: fits over so few points are not reported as trends.
- **`INTARSIA_PREFIX_STORE_PREFAULT=1` (the cause-side candidate) and `MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_` arms were not measured:** spark2's queue was full of other lanes' jobs and the slots would have come after the 10:00 stop. On `MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_`: per mallopt(3), setting the trim threshold also turns off glibc's dynamic mmap threshold, so on its own it pins the mmap threshold at 128 KiB and every 1 to 30 MB store buffer becomes a fresh mmap; that is memgrow's run E (insert 44 vs 27 ms, settle 111 vs 28 ms per request). Pair it with `MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=33554432` to keep buffers in arenas, which is then the same retention with an earlier top-trim; not tried.
- **Code reading is a sub-agent's read-only audit** (no profiler): `CancelGuard` in `crates/intarsia-server/src/api.rs:58`, queued and running reaping in `crates/intarsia-core/src/scheduler.rs:1194` to `:1211`, `engine/batch.rs:1014` to `:1060`, the per-request grammar clone in `structured.rs:720`, the store's `remove_entry` / `drop_obj` (`prefix_store.rs:1598`, `:1628`). I read `host_buf`, `release_host` and `take_host_buf` myself; the rest is not independently checked. llguidance's internal caches, the CUDA backend's pinned host allocations and per-`Seq` host buffers were not audited.
- **Clock guard:** these are memory runs, no timing ratio is published. For the env arms the SM clock stayed at 2,333 to 2,420 MHz and the SW Power Capping counter did not move between load start and load end.
