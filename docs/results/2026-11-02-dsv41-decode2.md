## 2. Is per-call overhead the cause of the dense excess? (task 2)

Yes for `wo_a`, and it is device-side, not launch cost. From the existing window-2 nsys trace of our rank 0 (`dsv41-draftdev/.scratch/win2/ab1`, not committed, 4 MB CSV):

- Graph replay leaves 0.1 us between kernels. A `wo_a` slice is `rot_in` 1.25 us, `split_kernel` 17.1 us (grid 8x8, 2.6 MB), `split_finish` 1.54 us, so 19.9 us a slice, 79.6 us a layer plus two transposes (1.3, 1.1), against 41 us for the 10.5 MB at 254 GB/s. The graphs lane's own fit for the split-K kernel is 6.5 us + bytes / 254 GB/s (`2026-11-01-dsv41-graphs.md`, "EXL3 dense linears"): the 6.5 us fixed cost per call is ramp and tail of a small kernel plus its two helpers, which launch order cannot hide.
- Microbench `bench_wo_a_fork` (new, `crates/intarsia-backend-cuda/tests/exl3.rs`; fleetq #7843, spark2, exl3 test binary from #7842): four slices of 1024 x 4096, K2 = 10, `matmul_exl3_splitk` (the engine's kernel for this grid), cold weights, 20 layers in one graph, median of 15 replays, us a layer:

| M | one stream (as recorded now) | fork over 4 streams |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 72.9 | 55.5 |
| 2 | 73.0 | 55.6 |
| 4 | 73.9 | 56.0 |

17.4 us a layer, 0.7 ms a pass at 40 attention layers, from `wo_a` alone. The fork does not reach the 41 us byte floor (4 slices side by side share the memory system and each still has its own ramp), so the cost is partly per call and partly the sequence.

- The Fable falsifier ("kernels per pass 2375 -> <= 1600 with dense down >= 1.5 ms; under 0.7 ms means the cost is not per launch") was written for folding launches. I did not fold (no `rot_in` or finish merged into a neighbour, no grouped kernel: both change sum order or need a new kernel, and the graphs lane bounded grouping `wo_a` at -0.31 ms a token). I overlapped the calls instead, which keeps the kernels and their bytes. Kernel count per pass is unchanged. Whole-pass ms fell by 0.8 to 1.4 ms (table below), so in that sense the cost is per call; the falsifier's 0.7 ms floor is passed, its 1.5 ms is not.

### Lane forks

`Step::lane` (graph.rs) tags the steps; in a recorded pass `run_stage` (runtime.rs) records an event on the pass stream, lets streams 2..5 wait for it, launches each lane step on its stream with a scratch slice of its own (`step_scratch`; the scratch is grown before the capture for 4 strides), and joins before the next lane-0 step, before any extra, and at the end of a stage. Eager passes (prompts longer than a verify) stay on one stream. Same kernels on the same bytes, so bitwise. To fork `wq_a` with `wkv` the builder emits `wkv` right after `wq_a` (they were `wq_a, norm, wq_b, rope, wkv`); the other steps keep their order.

## 3. L2 prefetch (Fable D3)

Fable said ship it; the earlier result (-0.24 ms a token, three layers) did not justify that on its own, so I measured it on the full model. W3 (#7882): shipped 50.8 and 50.3, shipped + prefetch 51.9. W4 (#7892), ABAB: prefetch 51.3, shipped 50.6, prefetch 51.9, shipped 50.7 (code 68.1 / 68.1 / 69.2 / 67.8, chat 47.1 / 46.4 / 48.1 / 46.5, prose 42.2 / 41.6 / 42.5 / 41.5, recall 54.7 / 53.4 / 54.6 / 53.8). ## What did not work, and what is open

- Retuning the chooseK table alone: no measurable change (section 1).
- The 35B check failed twice for script reasons before it passed (section 4).
- Not built: folding `rot_in` and `split_finish` into neighbours, a grouped EXL3 kernel for the four `wo_a` slices (D1 proper), and PDL on the MoE pair (D2 step 1, `exl3_moe_cp.cu`). The lanes data favoured D1's direction and I stopped there; D2 needs kernel work and a capture test I did not start.
- More lanes are possible (the indexer's `wq_b` with `wq_b`, the shared expert with the router). The ones left are each about 0.3 ms a pass, below the 0.2 ms a token bar, and need step reordering; not done.
- The table entries are about 1 ms above what lanes leave (above). Not re-tuned.
- Remaining gap to the gate on the same prompt set: code 68.7 against 71.2, prose 42.4 against 44.8. About 2.5 tok/s each, roughly 1.1 ms a token.
