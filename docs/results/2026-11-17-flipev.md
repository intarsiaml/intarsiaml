## 1. Verdict

Measurement lane, no engine change. Nothing became default-on.

* **herd: no loss found in any cell measured** (4K, 16K, 32K shared prefix; simultaneous, 0.5 s and 2 s staggered; with unrelated traffic). Gains of -52 % (4K) to -75 % (32K) TTFT sum; ids identical.
  Not covered: prefixes under the store's `min_tokens` / the 2048 grid (they cannot herd), `--max-batch 16`, a leader that is slow for other reasons.
* **sjf: the stutter worry is not borne out in the tail** (max / p99 inter-token gap of ongoing decodes is not worse), but while shorts overtake a long prefill the ongoing decodes get about
  17 % fewer tokens inside the window, because the shorts' own prefill happens inside it (under FIFO that work comes after the long prompt). The 4-decoder cells are void (section 4).
* ids: 672 / 672 herd replies and 160 / 160 sjf contended replies equal their solo references (sections 5).
* Extra cell (section 6): `--prefix-store on` costs 12 to 14 % on 8 unrelated cold 8K prompts, but **not through packed prefill** (it never engaged, store off included); and its greedy ids differ from store-off in 14 of 16 prompts (unexplained, deterministic per config).

## 2. Method

Spark1 (GB10, CUDA), `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4`, binary built from origin/main `36f21e2ea` (+ this lane's scripts only), one fleetq job (#9937, `--quiet gpu,cpu --mem 62`, 95 min expected, ran 55 min; spark2 was
blocked by another session's overdue #9891, so the run moved to spark1 per the coordinator). vLLM (34 GB) stayed up, earlyoom active, lowest MemAvailable 49.9 GB, memwatch killed nothing.
One server per visit, ABBA (A = feature off, B = on), clients on the Spark over HTTP, token-id prompts (`/v1/completions`, temperature 0, `ignore_eos`, 48 tokens), TTFT from each request's own send.
Scripts `scripts/flipev/`: `run.sh` (phases pack, herd, q4), `herd_bench.py` (herd's client plus `--stagger`, mode `mix`), `q4.py`, `table.py`; raw data and `tables.md` in `docs/results/2026-11-17-flipev/raw/`.

* herd phase: `serve --max-seq 49152 --max-batch 8 --prefix-store on --mem-budget 40`, `INTARSIA_PREFIX_HERD=1` on B. Per visit: 4K prefix (4160 shared + 200 own) x 3 seeds, 32K (32832 + 200) x 2, 16K
  (16448 + 200) staggered 0.5 s x 2 and 2.0 s x 2, mix (4 requests on a 16K prefix + 2 unrelated 8192-token prompts, released together, thread order S U S S U S) x 2; each cell is followed by the same prompts one at a time (solo = the id reference). n = 4 visits' worth: 8 cells per arm for 4K (2 visits x 3), 4 cells per arm for the others.
* q4 phase: `serve --max-seq 49152 --max-batch 4`, `INTARSIA_CB_NO_REUSE=1`, `INTARSIA_PREFILL_ORDER=shortest INTARSIA_PREFILL_AGE=8` on B. 2 or 4 decoders (100-token prompt, 1000 tokens) start at 0; a 40K-token prompt at +2 s;
  scenario longshort adds two 1K prompts at long +1.5 s and +4 s. 2 reps per visit, so 4 runs per cell and arm. Window = long submit to long first token. ITL = gap between consecutive SSE events of one decoder.
* pack phase (coordinator's extra cell; section 6 corrects its premise): `--max-batch 8 --max-seq 16384`, 8 distinct cold 8192-token prompts, store off vs `--prefix-store on`, 2 seeds x 2 visits per arm.

## 4. Question 4: sjf and the decodes' inter-token gaps

Valid cells are the 2-decoder ones. With 4 decoders at `--max-batch 4` the long prompt has no free slot and waits for a decoder to finish (long TTFT 25 s, no prefill inside the window, the window ITL equals the baseline ITL); those cells
say nothing about prefill stutter (the shorts also queue). 3 decoders + long would fit but was not run; 4 decoders + shorts needs `--max-batch 8`, not run.

| 2 decoders, 4 runs per cell | sjf | window gaps n | mean ms | p99 ms | max ms | gaps > 200 ms | long TTFT s | shorts TTFT s | decode tok/s/stream in window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| long only | off | 869 | 80.8 | 235.9 | 269.8 | 29 | 8.82 | - | 12.3 |
| long only | on | 884 | 79.2 | 225.4 | 251.6 | 30 | 8.87 | - | 12.4 |
| long + 2 shorts | off | 869 | 80.8 | 221.2 | 268.8 | 26 | 8.83 | 7.56 / 5.57 | 12.3 |
| long + 2 shorts | on | 772 | 95.4 | 220.3 | 249.1 | 28 | 9.29 | 0.69 / 0.50 | 10.4 |

(The p50 is not reported: the client reads several SSE events at once, so many gaps are 0.)

Answer: when sjf pauses the long prefill for a short one and resumes it, the decodes' tail gaps do not get worse: p99 220 vs 221 ms, max 249 vs 269 ms, gaps over 200 ms 28 vs 26, and the long-only
scenario (no overtaking possible, so the same work either way) matches. What does change: inside the window the decodes advance 17 % less (10.4 vs 12.3 tok/s per stream, mean gap 95 vs 81 ms) and the long prompt's TTFT is 0.46 s
later (9.29 vs 8.83 s), because the two shorts' prefills and first tokens run inside the window; under FIFO that work happens after the long prompt, outside the window (shorts wait 7.56 / 5.57 s instead of 0.69 / 0.50 s).
The long prompt's chunks beside a freshly decoding short stream (sjf doc section 2) are in these runs and show no outlier gap.

## 6. Extra cell: `--prefix-store on` on a burst of unrelated 8K prompts (`--max-batch 8`)

| | n | TTFT sum s | TTFT p50 s | TTFT last s | wall s | prefill chunks | chunk time s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| store off (default) | 4 | 51.29 (51.14 to 51.45) | 6.40 | 11.71 | 12.44 | 60 | 17.96 |
| `--prefix-store on` | 4 | 57.64 (57.31 to 57.96) | 7.23 | 13.34 | 14.10 | 66 | 19.94 |
| cost | | +12.4 % | +13 % | +13.9 % | +13.4 % | +6 | +11 % |

(chunks and chunk time: one server each, first visit of each arm, covering the 2-prompt warm-up too.)

**The coordinator's hypothesis does not hold: packed prefill did not engage in either arm** (`intarsia_batch_pack_passes_total 0` and `packed_chunks_total 0` with the store off as well). A CUDA packed pass only takes whole prompts
that fit one chunk (a prompt mid-way never packs), and an 8192-token prompt is four 2048-token chunks, so there was nothing to pack. The 12 to 14 % cost of `--prefix-store on` here comes from something else; the metrics show 6 more
prefill chunks (66 vs 60, same 132096 prefill tokens) and 11 % more chunk time, i.e. a different chunk schedule (store capture alignment) plus the captures, not a packing refusal. To measure the packing refusal itself the burst has to be
prompts of at most one chunk (for example 1K to 2K tokens), not run here. Under herd these 8 requests would not benefit (they share nothing).

**Ids side finding (unexplained):** the greedy ids of these 8 prompts are bitwise the same in the two store-off visits and in the two store-on visits, but differ between store off and on in 14 of the 16 prompts (seeds 71 and 72, 48 tokens each).
No solo run was taken in this phase, so it is not known which arm equals a solo run. The two configs run different chunk schedules (60 vs 66 chunks), and random-id prompts have flat logits, so a numerical difference between chunkings can flip a greedy choice;
that is a hypothesis, not tested. The herd and sjf id gates are unaffected (each compares within one configuration). Worth a follow-up before anyone treats store on and store off as id-equal on CUDA.

## 7. Flip recommendation

**herd (`INTARSIA_PREFIX_HERD=1`): flip to default-on is supported by the evidence for `serve` with the prefix store on.** 4K to 32K shared prefixes, simultaneous and staggered: TTFT sum -13 % to -75 %, never worse for any
follower or for the sum; unrelated prompts unchanged as a pair; 672 / 672 replies equal; CPU gates in the herd lane. It only acts when the store is on and a request shares at least `min_tokens` leading tokens with an older
prefilling request. Remaining doubts: `--max-batch 16`, non-CUDA backends (CPU tests only), one unrelated request can be about 0.35 s slower in a mixed burst, and the store itself costs 12 to 14 % on unrelated 8K bursts (section 6), which is a
store-default question, not herd's. Revert: unset the variable (or one-line default).

**sjf (`INTARSIA_PREFILL_ORDER=shortest`): do not flip on this evidence alone; it is a policy choice.** Tail stutter is not a problem (max gap 249 vs 269 ms; p99 equal), ids 160 / 160 equal. The price stays what the sjf lane found:
long prompts +17 % p50 TTFT in bursts, and in the window of an overtaken long prefill the ongoing decodes run 17 % slower and the long prompt's TTFT is 0.46 s (5 %) later, in exchange for shorts at 0.5 to 0.7 s instead of 5.6 to 7.6 s.
Flip if short-prompt latency matters more than long-prompt latency for the default workload. Unmeasured: 3 or 4 decoders with shorts (needs `--max-batch 8`), `INTARSIA_PREFILL_AGE` other than 8 on hardware, sjf together with herd on hardware.
