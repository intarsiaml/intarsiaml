# HIP fp8 GEMV: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4 decode on one R9700 (branch perf/hip-fp8-gemv)

Base `c00af21` (origin/main), tatooine R9700 #0 (gfx1201, ROCm 7.2.4 dev image, `HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0`, graphs on,
`--profile-every 0`). Both trees are rsynced without `.git`, so binaries report `unstamped`. Scripts: `scripts/hip-decode/`
(`burst.sh`, `remote.sh`, `build.sh`, `mutate.py`, `analyze.py`), raw logs of every burst in
`docs/results/2026-10-17-hip-fp8-gemv/` (`burst-logs/`, `profile-*.txt`, `hipdecode-*-{A,B}*.json`, `qualify-*-quick-report.md`).

## 1. Result

The fp8 GEMVs were already at the streaming rate plus a fixed per-launch cost; no block shape, prefetch depth, or arithmetic
change moves a single projection. The one gain is the **fused qkv+z / k+v pair kernel at one row per warp** (bitwise the old
kernel): 92 us less kernel time per step, **+0.8 % decode** end to end (the A/B below), prefill untouched.

## 2. Where the time goes: per-shape bandwidth

In-model rocprofv3 trace (157 steady decode steps of a 160-token greedy run, 677 launches per step before and after;
`analyze.py` groups the fp8 launches by kernel and grid, bytes follow from the launch geometry). Before = origin/main, after =
this branch:

| shape (launches per step) | before: us, GB/s (% of 627) | after: us, GB/s (% of 627) | us per step before -> after |
|---|---|---|---|
| qkv + z pair, 12288 x 2048, 25.2 MB (30 GDN layers) | 46.8, 538 (86 %) | **44.0, 572 (91 %)** | 1404 -> 1320 |
| k + v pair, 1024 x 2048, 2.1 MB (10 attention layers) | 6.5, 321 (51 %) | **5.7, 368 (59 %)** | 65 -> 57 |
| out_proj / o_proj, 2048 x 4096, 8.4 MB (40) | 16.9, 495 (79 %) | 17.1, 489 (78 %), same kernel | 678 -> 686 |
| attention q, 8192 x 2048, 16.8 MB (10) | 31.4, 535 (85 %) | 31.3, 536 (85 %), same kernel | 314 -> 313 |

Per step: kernel time 6.393 -> 6.286 ms, step wall (rocprof, first start to last end) 8.527 -> 8.411 ms, 677 launches both, idle
inside the step 2.13 ms (25 %) both. (`docs/results/2026-10-14-hip-decode-2.md` quotes the pair at "about 690 GB/s, above
the DRAM figure": that divides the 25.2 MB of the large pair by the mean of all 40 pair launches, 36.7 us, which mixes in the
10 small k + v pairs. The large pair alone was 538 GB/s.)

Why nothing else moves: the DRAM-cold scaling run (`bench_fp8_sweep`, v2 kernel, K = 2048, microbench in `quantmoe.rs`) gives

| N | 16 | 256 | 1024 | 2048 | 4096 | 8192 | 16384 | 32768 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MB | 0.03 | 0.52 | 2.10 | 4.19 | 8.39 | 16.8 | 33.6 | 67.1 |
| cold us | 3.6 | 4.8 | 7.1 | 11.1 | 17.9 | 30.9 | 57.1 | 109.4 |

A tiny launch costs 3.6 us (x load, weight load, reduction, store: the dependent-kernel floor). Past 8 MB the increments are
640 to 650 GB/s (8.4 -> 16.8 MB: 12.9 us / 8.39 MB; 33.6 -> 67.1 MB: 52.4 us / 33.5 MB), i.e. the card's 640 GB/s. So time is
about 4.5 us + bytes / 645 GB/s at every shape that matters, and the 79 to 86 % figures above are that fixed cost over a short
transfer, not a bandwidth shortfall: out_proj 8.4 MB predicts 17.5 us (measured 17.2 in the microbench), q 16.8 MB predicts
30.5 us (30.9). The large pair with two rows per warp was the only shape above this line (46.8 us against 43.5 us).

Not the VALU and not the DRAM page pattern either: the same loop with the weight conversion removed (section 4, dot4) is
equally fast, and a "hot" run that re-reads the same buffers every launch is no faster than the cold one (o_proj 18.0 vs
17.2 us, q 32.4 vs 31.0), so the ~645 GB/s is the rate of the pattern end to end. The cause of the hot case not being faster was
not investigated.

## 3. What was built

* `matmul_fp8_pair_gemv` (qkv + z, k + v, 40 launches per step) runs one row per warp and eight warps per block instead of two
  rows per warp (`kernels/hip/fp8.hip`: `fp8_gemv_body` and `fp8_gemv_pair_kernel` take the warps per block `W` as a template
  parameter, `pair_cfg()` picks R = 1, W = 8; the other staging variants Q = 1, 3 keep v2's shape). Every row is summed in the
  same order whatever (R, W), so the kernel is **bitwise** the two `matmul_fp8_gemv_v2` launches it replaces. Same kernel name,
  same registry entry, same admission (38 kernels, 613 cases on the 35B shapes: ADMITTED); the block shape is internal.
  `INTARSIA_HIP_FP8_PAIR="R,W"` (R 1, 2, 4; W 4, 8, 16) forces a shape and is listed in `ENV_KNOBS`.
* Tests: `crates/intarsia-backend-hip/tests/fp8_pair_shapes.rs` runs the pair at all nine (R, W) on seven shapes (the 35B pairs,
  a first segment ending mid-block, a one-row segment, K past the prefetch window) with W8A8 and W8A16 segments mixed, and
  requires bitwise equality with `matmul_fp8_gemv_v2` (189 comparisons). `fused_decode.rs` (pair vs two v2 launches, bitwise,
  plus the cpu reference at 1e-3) now runs the new default.
* Mutations (`mutate.py`, added to the default list of `remote.sh mutate`): `pair-nb0` (the kernel splits blocks between the
  segments with two rows per warp while the launcher uses R) and `pair-grid` (one block count for both segments). Result, tatooine,
  the intended test fails each: fp8-scale, fp8-convert (decode_v2), pair-block, fp8-pair-scale, pair-nb0, pair-grid (fused_decode):
  **6 of 6 fail**. The passing tree: `fused_decode` 4/4, `fp8_pair_shapes` 1/1 (189), `decode_v2` 5/5, `admission` 6/6.
* `remote.sh`: `fp8sweep` stage, `<stage>@base` runs any stage on the origin/main tree and its own cargo target volume
  (interleaved A/B), `acc35` now uses the golden CUDA uses (`...-top20-meta.safetensors`, sha256 `b6b51e1b1699c66c`), the
  mutation stage touches the sources afterwards (it shares the cargo target volume: **rebuild before running binaries after it**;
  the rebuild after B4 was done with every file touched, 27 s). `analyze.py` prints the per-shape fp8 table.

## 4. Dead ends (kept in commit 66498a3, removed in the next)

1. **Block-shape sweep of the single projections** (v3 kernel: rows per warp R 1, 2, 4; prefetch depth UN 4, 8 chunks per
   lane, which at K = 4096 holds a whole row in flight; warps per block W 4, 8, 16; 18 shapes each, DRAM-cold, bitwise v2):
   o_proj 2048 x 4096 v2 17.2 us, best 16.8 (2,4,16), UN = 8 is 1.5 to 2.0 us *worse*, R = 4 worse; q 8192 x 2048 v2 31.0, best
   30.3 (1,4,8); k or v 512 x 2048 v2 5.8, best 5.0; qkv 30.9 -> 30.1. Everything within 0.8 us or worse, no shape worth a
   registry entry, so no per-shape selection through admission was added.
2. **dot4**: `v_dot4_f32_fp8_fp8` (available on gfx1201) on the raw e4m3 weights and e4m3 activations staged as bytes: no weight
   conversion, 4 instructions per 16 weight bytes instead of ~48, half the LDS traffic. Same speed (o_proj 17.2 vs 17.2 us,
   N-scale 8.4 MB 17.2 vs 17.9, 67 MB 108.8 vs 109.4): the GEMV is not ALU bound. Not shipped (and its sum order is not v2's).
3. **Split-K / persistent kernel**: not built. The only tall-K shape (K = 4096) is at the fixed + streaming line, a split needs a
   reduction (a second launch costs the 3.6 us floor again, atomics are not deterministic), and a persistent grid can only remove
   block-launch ramp that the scaling fit already shows is inside the 4.5 us.
4. Idea-3 items v2 already has: 16-byte non-temporal weight loads (`ik_ldcs`), 2 rows per warp for singles (R = 1 and 4 are
   not better), the activation broadcast through LDS, software prefetch of the first batch before the staging. Scale loads:
   fp8 scales are per tensor (two floats), nothing to coalesce.

## 5. What would help (estimates, not built)

The step is 8.4 ms, of which 2.13 ms is the ~3.1 us command-processor gap between 677 launches and ~0.35 ms is the fixed cost
of the 90 fp8 launches. Neither shrinks by tuning a GEMV; only fewer launches does. Candidates: the input rmsnorm into the
qkv+z / q+k+v GEMV prologue (the block already stages the activation row; 40 launches), the gated rmsnorm into out_proj
(30 launches), q joined to the k + v pair (10 launches). Each launch removed saves about 3 us gap plus 1.5 to 2 us kernel: about
5 us, so ~80 launches is ~0.4 ms or ~4 to 5 % of the step. These need new fused ops across core, cpu reference, model and CUDA,
which is outside this task.
