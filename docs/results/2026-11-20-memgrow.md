# memgrow: is the MemAvailable decline of the ab35d soak bounded? (2026-11-20)

Lane `memgrow`, overnight 2026-11-19 / 20, spark1 (GB10, 121 GB unified memory), vLLM (`missioncntrl-guidance`, flight / guidance / capcom) up beside the whole time and never touched. intarsiaml at origin/main `6d2eda2e4` (binary stamp `memgrow-6d2eda2e4da8`, built on spark1 with fleetq #10331, 6 min), `serve --backend cuda --model <Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4 snapshot 1355db6a> --max-seq 131072 --mem-budget 40 --profile-every 0 --max-batch 2 --prefix-store on`, everything else default (ab35d's command line; host-tier store budget 8 GiB, no disk tier, MTP N=2, herd, SJF). Nothing in the engine was changed; no default changed.

## Summary

**Verdict: on the load ab35d ran, the decline is bounded: the server's footprint is 40.4 GB, reached in 10 to 20 minutes and flat after that (-15 +- 44 MB/h over the next 76 minutes). Under other request mixes the process's host memory moves by a few GB, and one mixed run (B) crept up 0.5 GB/h without reaching a plateau in 60 minutes, which this lane could not resolve. No leak was shown; a leak below about 0.1 GB/h on A's load or 0.5 GB/h on B's is not excluded by runs of this length. The component is the intarsia process's anonymous host memory (the 8 GiB store plus glibc heap retention around it), not vLLM, not the page cache, not GPU memory.**

- **ab35d's -18.6 MB/min was the tail of the fill phase, not a trend.** Its own series, refit: -18.6 +- 10.3 MB/min when the fit starts at sample 5, +1.5 +- 10.9 when it starts at sample 8, +21 +- 16 from sample 12; the first 5 samples alone moved MemAvailable by 11 GB (host store and workspaces filling). 0.47 GB over 25 minutes against an 8 GiB store budget could never have been the store still filling anyway: the store was full in minute 3 (`host.bytes` 8.58 GB of 8.59 GB).
- **Run A, the same load generator for 105 minutes (2,646 requests, 0 errors), sampled every 30 s: the server's footprint is 40.4 GB, reached in 10 to 20 minutes, then flat.** MemAvailable 82.0 GB before the server, 53.1 GB with it loaded and idle, **41.6 GB from minute 20 to minute 106** (sd 175 MB, min 40.9, max 41.9). Slope from minute 30: **-15 +- 44 MB/h** (from minute 10: -60 +- 31 MB/h, still settling). With the server stopped MemAvailable returned to 82.3 GB (+0.24 GB against before; 0 processes of ours left).
- **Components (box-wide counters, closing to 0.2 GB):** the process's GPU memory 27.25 GB (nvidia-smi per process, flat to +-130 MB for 105 minutes; weights 21.4 GB + 5.4 GB KV pool + workspaces) plus its anonymous host memory 12.85 GB (`RssAnon`: the 8.4 GB host store tier plus 4.4 GB of other heap) plus 0.2 GB of kernel slab, page tables and shared memory. **Page cache did not move (Cached +47 MB)**: the store has no disk tier by default (budget 0), so the "page cache from store disk writes" explanation is excluded by configuration. vLLM did not move (GPU 26,768 MB and RSS 3,312 MB in every sample of every run, cgroup 8,252 to 8,254 MB).
- **Other request mixes move the process's host memory by a few GB but did not grow without bound in the 30 to 60 minutes tried.** Mixed traffic (run B: sizes 0.5k to 93k tokens, aborts, tools, thinking, 996 requests, 60 min): non-host anon crept from 2.5 to 3.1 GB, **+0.5 GB/h (2.3 to 5.4 SE) and not saturated when the run ended**: the one unresolved signal. Many short requests (runs C and D, 3,500 and 3,050 requests): non-host anon swung between 1.9 and 6.6 GB (a burst of +2.7 GB in 3 minutes and a drop of 1.3 GB in C; flat in D). One `malloc_trim(0)` at the end of D handed 1.1 GB back, so a good part of the swings is free memory glibc keeps (arena tops, holes), not live data.
- **Operational reading for a cutover:** at the plateau the server holds 40.4 GB; with vLLM resident the lowest MemAvailable seen in any of the six runs was 38.8 GB (run C, 30 s sampler) (kill line 15 GB, earlyoom at 2 %), so there are about 24 GB of margin. A leak below roughly 0.1 GB/h (A) or 0.5 GB/h (B's creep) is not excluded by runs of this length; a days-long run on production-like traffic with the sampler of this lane (`scripts/memgrow/sampler.sh`) would settle it, and the mitigation below is cheap if B's creep turns out real.

- **A tested mitigation, not a recommendation: `MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=1048576` in the server's environment (run E, A's load, 40 min) cut the process's non-host anon from 4.3 to 1.2 GB and raised the plateau MemAvailable from 41.6 to 44.9 GB (+3.3 GB); `malloc_info()` shows the live heap flat at 1.2 GB.** It costs speed: decode and wall were a few percent worse in E on matched seeds (not clock-guarded, vLLM's idleness not checked), and the store's own counters show why: insert 44 against 27 ms, settle 111 against 28 ms per request (same night, run F as the baseline). Opt-in by construction (an environment variable, no code change); with 24 GB of margin it is not needed, and it is the user's call.

## 1. What the ab35d data say (reasoning from the existing raw files)

`docs/results/2026-11-19-ab35d-cutover-scoreboard/raw/sample-w4.tsv` (PR #465; 26 samples, 60 s apart, 22:20:45 to 22:45:48):

| sample | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 to 5 | 6 to 25 |

| store host tier MB | 0 | 6,394 | 8,590 | 8,272 to 8,583 | 7,812 to 8,589 |

- **18.6 MB/min times 25 min is 0.47 GB, 6 % of the 8 GiB store budget.** The store reached its budget in minute 2 to 3 (`intarsia_prefix_store_tier_bytes{tier="host"}` 8.59 GB of 8.59 GB budget, host evictions started in sample 2), so it cannot still be growing toward the budget, and a disk tier was off (`tier_bytes{tier="disk"}` 0, budget 0).
- **The reported slope depends on where the fit starts:** from sample 5 (the "store full" point of the doc) -18.6 +- 10.3 MB/min (1.8 SE); from sample 8 +1.5 +- 10.9; from sample 12 +21 +- 16. The first five samples contain the fill (48.6 to 37.3 GB). The residual wobble (+-0.3 GB) matches the store tier's own sawtooth (7.8 to 8.6 GB).
- **Its `rss_mb` column (162 MB, constant) never measured the engine**: `serve` runs a supervisor and the engine as its child; `ps -o rss -p <supervisor>` is the supervisor's 162 MB. Neither does RSS cover the GPU allocations on a GB10 (unified memory, cudaMalloc is not in /proc). This lane samples the child and reads the GPU side from nvidia-smi per process.

So the data could not say more than "flat after the fill, within noise". The longer runs below test that.

## 2. Method

**Box and rules.** spark1 only; vLLM serving flight / guidance / capcom stays up (nothing `--takes-down`); fleetq slot for every job (build #10331 `--load cpu --mem 55`; runs `--mem 52`); watchdog on the box (`wd.sh`, every 20 s, kills the supervisor and its children by pid below 15 GB MemAvailable; 0 kills, lowest MemAvailable 38.8 GB); earlyoom active; `df` 92 %, the box dir `~/intarsiaml-memgrow` (build, out) was removed at the end. (Each `window-*.log` ends with "processes of ours left: [<pid>]": that pid is the `pgrep -f` matching its own ssh shell, not a server; the final check was `ps -eo pid,cmd | grep '[i]ntarsiaml-memgrow'`, empty.)

**Sampler** (`scripts/memgrow/sampler.sh`, runs on the box, 30 s): a keepalive-bound loop that survives the server, so each run has a **base** phase (vLLM only, 90 s), **start** (server loading), **settle** (server up, idle, 2 min), **load**, **idle** (the load stopped, server up), **stop** and **after** (server gone, 2.5 min). Per row: `/proc/meminfo` (MemFree, MemAvailable, Buffers, Cached, Active/Inactive anon and file, Unevictable, Mlocked, AnonPages, Mapped, Shmem, Dirty, Writeback, Slab, SReclaimable, SUnreclaim, KernelStack, PageTables, Percpu, VmallocUsed, AnonHugePages, SwapFree); the engine's `/proc/<pid>/status` (VmRSS, RssAnon, RssFile, RssShmem, VmSwap, VmData, Threads) and `smaps_rollup` (Pss, Pss_Anon, Pss_File, Pss_Shmem, Private_Dirty, Locked, AnonHugePages), where `<pid>` is the supervisor's child, re-resolved every tick (the ipid column); the vLLM container's cgroup (`memory.current`, anon, file, shmem, ...) and RSS of its main and EngineCore processes; **per-process GPU memory from `nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps` (works on this GB10: engine and vLLM EngineCore each have a number; the box-level `memory.used` is N/A)**; and the server's `/metrics`: requests, kv bytes used, device memory free, store entries, inserts, bytes inserted and restored, host / device / disk tier bytes, objects, evictions. Raw: `docs/results/2026-11-20-memgrow/raw/mem-<run>.csv` (77 columns), phase marks `marks-<run>.tsv`, watchdog log `wd-<run>.log`, server log `serve-<run>.log` (A to D and F; E's `malloc_info` XML log is reduced to `memstat-E.txt`).

**Loads** (`scripts/memgrow/soak.sh`, `soak-mixed.sh`, `soak-d.sh`; the generators run on endor against `spark-4e77:8191`, as in ab35d):

## 5. Attribution

| candidate | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| page cache from store disk writes | **excluded** | no disk tier (budget 0, `tier_bytes{tier="disk"}` 0); Cached +46 MB over 105 min; MemAvailable counts clean cache anyway |
| host tier growing toward its budget | **excluded** as a continuing cause | full in minute 1 to 3 in every run; mean 8.4 GB, sawtooth 7.8 to 8.59 GB, slope 0 +- 50 MB/h |
| vLLM or other processes | **excluded** | vLLM GPU 26,768 MB and RSS 3,312 MB constant in all samples of all runs, cgroup 8,252 to 8,254 MB; box before / after the server within 0.25 GB |
| GPU memory of the engine (cudaMalloc, KV pool, workspaces) | **excluded** | nvidia-smi per process 27.1 to 27.5 GB, slope -6 +- 41 MB/h from minute 30 in A |
| anonymous host memory of the engine (`RssAnon`) | **the whole decline, and bounded under the shipping load** | A: plateau 12.85 GB from minute 20, +17 +- 44 MB/h from minute 30; B: +0.5 GB/h not saturated (unresolved); C / D: arena sawtooth, 1.1 GB reclaimable by `malloc_trim(0)` |
| a leak (live data growing) | **not shown, not excluded for small rates** | flat for 90 minutes in A, returned to the OS at exit; B's creep unresolved; no heap profiler (malloc_info of E, below, is the only live-vs-free split) |

### 5b. Run E: a pinned mmap threshold removes most of the retention (one run, A's load)

glibc raises its mmap threshold (up to 32 MB) the first time it frees a large mmapped chunk and from then on serves allocations of up to that size from arenas, where freed pages stay resident until the top of an arena is trimmed; the store's host objects (1 to 30 MB, many sizes, allocated and freed on every demote / evict) are exactly that pattern. Setting `MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=1048576` pins the threshold at 1 MiB (a fixed value also turns the dynamic adjustment off), so every store buffer is a direct mmap and is returned to the OS when freed. Run E is A's load for 40 minutes with that variable in the server's environment, nothing else changed (plus the `libmemstat.so` thread, which only prints):

| minute of load | non-host anon, A | non-host anon, E | MemAvailable, A | MemAvailable, E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 to 10 | 3,476 | 1,055 | 43,029 | 45,713 |
| 10 to 20 | 4,330 | 1,187 | 41,699 | 44,990 |
| 20 to 30 | 4,360 | 1,184 | 41,720 | 44,918 |
| 30 to 40 | 4,408 | 1,158 | 41,708 | 44,899 |
| RssAnon before the first request (server up, idle) | 1,813 | 251 | 53,068 | 54,593 |

- **The plateau is 3.2 GB lower and MemAvailable 3.3 GB higher with the pinned threshold, on the same load, GPU memory unchanged (27.1 to 27.3 GB).** Run F (A's load again, no variable, a fresh server 20 minutes) reproduced A's numbers: non-host anon 3,143 / 4,178 / 4,261 MB and MemAvailable 43.5 / 41.8 / 41.8 GB per 10 minutes, RssAnon 905 MB before the first request. Run E's stop returned MemAvailable to 82,296 MB (+27 against before).
- **`malloc_info()` (`raw/memstat-E.txt`, every 2 min; the only live-versus-free split of this lane):** the arenas hold 1.3 to 1.4 GB read-write of which 0.17 to 0.2 GB is free chunks, live bytes in the arenas 1,108 / 1,187 / 1,198 / 1,211 / 1,204 MB at minutes 8.6, 16.6, 24.6, 32.6, 40.6 (a flat 1.2 GB from minute 16; the free part moved +35 MB), and 1,160 to 1,240 directly mmapped chunks of 8.2 to 8.3 GB (the store's host tier). So with the variable the process's host memory is 8.3 GB of store plus 1.2 GB of live heap, and **the live heap does not grow under A's load**. Without it, A held 4.4 GB beyond the store: taking the same 1.2 GB of live heap, about 3.2 GB of free pages kept by glibc, and 0.9 to 1.8 GB of that was already there before the first request (model loading: 1.8 GB in A, 0.9 GB in B, C, D, F), against 0.25 GB with the variable.
- **What it costs (indicative only: one run each, not clock-guarded, vLLM's idleness during E and F not checked):** on the same 11 seeds (201 to 211) run A (start of the night), run F (the same load without the variable, 05:20, same box) and run E: TTFT sum 74.7 / 74.0 / 73.9 s, decode median 46.3 / 46.6 / 44.5 tok/s, wall 116.5 / 116.1 / 119.8 s, aggregate output 63.7 / 64.1 / 61.9 tok/s (F reproduces A, so the night was steady). The store's own counters say where it goes (`raw/E-metrics-late.txt`, `raw/F-metrics-late.txt`, `raw/F-metrics-mid.txt`): insert time per insert **44 ms in E (174.3 s for 3,940 inserts) against 27 ms in F (57.4 s for 2,167)**, and settle time per request **111 ms in E (109.1 s over 980 requests) against 28 ms in F (15.0 s over 539)**; the settle runs on the scheduler thread between decode steps, which is where the few percent of decode and wall come from. Fresh `mmap` buffers page-fault on every allocation where glibc's retained arena pages did not (the host pool recycles only same-size buffers); that retention is what the 3.2 GB are. So the variable buys 3.3 GB of available memory for a few percent of decode speed and 80 ms of scheduler-thread time per request: a footprint trade the user can make (it is an environment variable of the server, no code and no `INTARSIA_*` knob), not something to switch on by default with 24 GB of margin already there.
- **What E does not show:** it is A's load, so it says nothing certain about B's creep; that B's non-host anon (2.5 to 3.1 GB, below A's plateau) is the same retention still filling is a hypothesis, not tested (no B-profile run with the variable, no time left).


## 6. Verdict

**On the load ab35d ran: bounded.** With 2 streams of 8k to 33k-token multi-turn requests, store on, defaults, the server's total footprint beside vLLM is **40.4 GB (27.25 GB GPU + 12.85 GB anon + 0.2 GB kernel), reached after 10 to 20 minutes; the drift after that is -15 +- 44 MB/h (A, 76 minutes from minute 30), i.e. no component grows faster than about 0.1 GB/h at 2 SE.** The component that moves at all is the engine's anonymous host memory (store host tier 8.4 GB plus 4.4 GB of other heap, of which about 3.2 GB is free pages glibc keeps, run E), not vLLM, not the page cache, not GPU memory. ab35d's -18.6 MB/min is the end of the fill, refit to +1.5 +- 10.9 MB/min from its sample 8.

**Under other traffic: moves by several GB, not shown to grow without bound, one signal unresolved.** C and D (short requests) show an arena sawtooth of up to +2.2 GB over A's plateau (C's RssAnon peaked at 15.1 GB against A's 12.85 GB, +4.7 GB over its own level before the first request burst) that glibc partly hands back (1.1 GB on a trim); B (mixed, aborts, long prompts) crept +0.5 GB/h for 60 minutes without a plateau.

**Not closed:** (1) run B's +0.5 GB/h creep under mixed traffic did not saturate in 60 minutes; the hypothesis (glibc arena retention, same family as C and D) is supported by D's `malloc_trim` (1.1 GB freed) but not proven: the live-vs-free split by `malloc_info` exists only for run E's load. (2) Real production traffic (thinking on, tools, client disconnects at random, more than 2 streams) was not available; the six runs are 4.9 hours of synthetic load in all. (3) Days: nothing here covers a restart, a model swap or days of uptime. (4) Thread count differed between runs (27 to 32 in A, 7 to 13 in B to D) with the same binary and flags; not investigated.

**What to do for the cutover:** run the sampler (`scripts/memgrow/sampler.sh`, 30 s, no root needed) beside the first production shadow day, alert on MemAvailable below 20 GB (kill line 15 GB, earlyoom 2 %), and expect 38.8 to 42 GB of MemAvailable at steady state with vLLM resident. If the footprint ever matters (a second model beside it, or B's creep turning out real), `MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=1048576` in the service environment is the one lever measured here (section 5b: -3.2 GB of anon, a few percent of decode speed); `malloc_trim(0)` on a timer would be the other (run D: -1.1 GB in one call, no speed measured), and neither is in the engine today.
