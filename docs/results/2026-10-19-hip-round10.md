### Method

* **W1, `lnprof` stage:** `generate` twice unprofiled, then once under `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace`.
  * 35B on GPU 0 and 8B on GPU 1, in the same window.
  * Prompts: `1k` is the first quarter of `prompt4k.txt`; `4k` is `prompt4k.txt`; `16k` is four copies; `dec` is the
    story prompt with 128 tokens (35B) or 64 tokens (8B).
* **`scripts/hip-decode/longnext.py`:**
  * lists every dispatch of >= 1 ms in the trace with the dispatch right after it (+1);
  * the durations come from the profiler, which times kernels correctly (only the gap moves under rocprofv3, shown as
    `pgap` ~277 us);
  * predicted unprofiled cost per launch = max(0, 260 us - dur(+1)) for longs of >= 1.05 ms;
  * the model is the gap_repro `pat` result (tools/rocm-gap-repro `42420fbf`): +1 cannot finish before ~260 us after it
    starts, +2 and later are unaffected, threshold 1.00 vs 1.05 ms.
* **`raw/prof/longnext-*.txt` caveats.** These were written by the first version of the script:
  * their "%" divides by the profiled span, which includes model load;
  * their groups include one-off first launches.

  The table below divides by the unprofiled time and drops one-off groups. The script now has `--ms` and `--min-n` for
  this.
* **Stamps:** `pfstamp@base:s4k` (the round 9 stamp method at 4408 tokens, unprofiled and profiled) ran in W1. The data
  is in `raw/stamps/` and **not analysed yet**. Stamps make the 1-thread stamp kernel the +1, so they measure the window W
  rather than the real +1's cost (round 9 measured W ~250 to 275 us).

## Levers (step 2)

### Lever 1: row split of long bf16 GEMM / GEMV launches (`INTARSIA_HIP_GEMM_SPLIT`), BUILT, RUN 2026-10-21: NO WIN, OPT-IN OFF

`kernels/hip/matmul.hip` (`split_cfg`, `split_plan`, `split_rows`, used by `gemm_launch` and `gemv_launch`).

* **What it does:** a launch estimated above `INTARSIA_HIP_GEMM_SPLIT_MIN_US` (default 1100) is cut along independent
  output rows, read at every launch:
  * a GEMM is cut over token rows, in 128-row granules (the largest token tile);
  * a one-row GEMV is cut over weight rows, in 1024-row granules.
* **Modes:**
  * `tail`: two pieces, the second about `INTARSIA_HIP_GEMM_SPLIT_TAIL_US` (default 400) long. The second piece absorbs
    the window (>= 260 us) and is too short to stall what follows. It needs one extra launch per long GEMM.
  * `even`: pieces of at most ~900 us, round 8's rule.
  * `0` / unset: one launch.
* **Estimates:**
  * GEMM 5.0e7 MAC/us on rows rounded up to the granule. Measured 4.7e7 to 5.1e7 on the 8B's gate / up / down / qkv at
    ~1k and 2k tokens.
  * GEMV 620 bytes/ns: the 8B lm_head, 1.25 GB in 1.94 ms.
* **What the defaults cut** (`tests/gemm_split.rs` plan pins):
  * 8B gate / up / down at ~1k: [0, 640, 1059];
  * down at 2048: [0, 1536, 2048];
  * qkv at 2048 (~1.02 ms, no stall): one launch;
  * lm_head: [0, 121856, 151936].
  * No 35B launch reaches the trigger: the 35B uses f8 / dq GEMMs, and its gemm_wmma launches are short.
* **Bitwise by construction:**
  * every output element is computed by the same kernel, with the tile chosen from the full M, over the same K loop;
  * the gemm_simple fallback and multi-row GEMVs are never cut;
  * piece offsets stay 16 B aligned.

  `tests/gemm_split.rs` compares every forced cut bit for bit against one launch. It covers M not a tile multiple, a
  1-row last piece under M = 129, the f32 logits path, the GEMV over weight rows, and bf16 GEMV. **It has not run yet.**
* **Kill switch and admission:** `INTARSIA_HIP_GEMM_SPLIT=0`. The three knobs are in `ENV_KNOBS`.
* **Host seam:** `ik_hip_gemm_split_rows` / `gemm_split_rows`, for the plan test and for mutants.
* **Expected gain** if the window model holds in the engine:
  * up to the predicted cost above, minus one extra launch and a partial wave per cut long GEMM;
  * roughly +3 to 5 % 8B prefill at ~1k, +1 % at 4k and 16k, +0.9 % 8B decode.
* **Why `even` exists (advisor):** whether a >= 260 us piece absorbs the window has been shown only in gap_repro with a
  spin kernel. Round 9's stamped and unstamped totals were equal although stamps should have cost ~10 ms. So the A/B must
  compare tail, even and off. If even wins clearly, the absorption assumption is wrong in the engine, and that also
  changes the "free" rows of the inventory.

  ranges overlap.
* 2048 reproduces round 9's -0.6 %.

**`auto` mode (pick the kernel by KV length): rejected with the coordinator.**
* The decode step is a captured graph, recorded once per `SequenceState`, and serve's `LocalEngine` captures once per
  process (`crates/intarsia-core/src/model/graph_step.rs`).
* The position is a device scalar, so the kernel choice is frozen at capture. A host-side auto would freeze whatever the
  first decode step chose.
* The two ways to make it work:
  * **(a) device-side:** launch both kernels and let blocks exit on the device position. That adds one graph node per
    attention layer, ~50 to 60 us per step (about 1 %), which is more than it can win.
  * **(b) a shared-core hook:** a `Backend` decode variant that makes `graph_step` recapture when it changes (~3.5 ms per
    crossing, a CUDA / Metal no-op by default).
* Both were rejected for at most +0.3 % above 13k.
* OCC stays opt-in and off. The `splitkv_occ.rs` threshold test was skipped as agreed.
