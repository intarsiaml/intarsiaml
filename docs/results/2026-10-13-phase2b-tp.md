## What was built

* `intarsia_core::comm`: `Comm` trait, `HostCommGroup` (works on any backend, so the cpu backend tests the sharding without a GPU).
* `Model::load_tp` / `load_shard` / `from_shards`: one `Model` per rank (own backend, weights, workspace, KV); `forward` on rank 0 runs ranks 1.. on scoped threads. Column-parallel q/k/v/gate/up (row slices, contiguous in the file), row-parallel o/down (column slices gathered on the host, only the slice uploaded). KV split by head (4 of 8 KV heads per rank). q_norm/k_norm, layer norms, embedding replicated. `lm_head`: **replicated on rank 0 only** (the simpler choice; see ADR for the cost).
* Plan: `ldims` (per-rank heads/KV heads/FFN) feed the sweep shapes and the workspace; `plan show` prints per-rank dims, per-rank weights and KV capacity, and the all-reduce count and bytes per decode step. Admission runs once on rank 0's plan shapes (identical for rank 1, which adopts the result).
* HIP: `kernels/hip/allreduce.hip` + `crates/intarsia-backend-hip/src/comm.rs`, ported by design from radiance libr4d v1.4.0 (Apache-2.0): `kernels/hip/NOTICE-radiance`, `LICENSES/Apache-2.0-radiance.txt`.
* Tests (Mac, no GPU): `crates/intarsia-models/tests/tp.rs` (tp=2 vs tp=1 f32 prefill/decode, bf16 greedy decode with chunking and truncate, per-rank shapes and weight bytes, refusal of 3-way, all_gather, abort). `cargo test --workspace` and `cargo check -p intarsia-cli --features hip` pass. GPU tests: `crates/intarsia-backend-hip/tests/comm.rs`.
* `plan show --tp 2` output (excerpt): `tensor parallel x2 (hip-p2p collective)  per rank: 16 heads, 4 kv heads, ffn 6144 (model: 32/8/12288)  all-reduce 72 calls/decode step, 1179648 B/rank`; rank 0 8998 MiB weights (includes lm_head), rank 1 7811 MiB, 4096 tokens KV capacity each.

## 4. Accuracy (`accuracy --backend hip --mode both`, bf16 floor thresholds)

| Run | prefill top-1 | prefill KL | decode top-1 | decode KL | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tp=1, short | 100.00% | 5.623e-4 | 100.00% | 9.611e-4 | PASS |
| tp=2, short (f32 partials, default) | 99.77% | 6.545e-4 | 100.00% | 9.948e-4 | PASS |
| tp=2, short (bf16 partials) | 100.00% | 7.111e-4 | 100.00% | 9.934e-4 | PASS |
| tp=1, long | 99.51% (7 of 1434 positions differ) | 7.866e-4 | 100.00% | 8.810e-4 | PASS |
| tp=2, long (f32 partials, default) | **99.44%** (8 differ; 99.65% counting the 3 fp16-reference ties) | 8.629e-4 | 100.00% | 9.356e-4 | **FAIL** (prefill top-1 < 99.5) |
| tp=2, long (bf16 partials) | **99.37%** (9 differ) | 8.281e-4 | 100.00% | 9.345e-4 | **FAIL** |

The gate fails on the long golden's prefill top-1 only; every KL, tail-mass and non-finite check passes, and decode passes everywhere. I did not change thresholds or tune to get a pass.

* **Wrong assumption, named:** I expected the extra flip to come from the reduce rounding twice (bf16 partials). It does not: f32 partials with a single rounding, which is what one unsharded matmul does, still give 8 differing positions against tp=1's 7 (bf16 partials give 9).
* **Strongest evidence it is noise at the metric's edge:** the strict metric counts the reference's fp16 ties as disagreement. Counting them as agreement (the gate prints this number too), tp=1 and tp=2 both score **99.65%** on the long golden, 5 real disagreements each. tp=1 passes the strict threshold by exactly one position.
* tp=2 vs tp=1 directly (self-golden, prefill): 1 top-1 flip of 434 on the short set, 5 of 1434 on the long set; mean 21-way KL 3.3e-4 / 5.9e-4, the same order as tp=1 vs the fp16 reference (8e-4). The self-golden budgets (mean KL 1e-4) are for same-numerics comparisons and fail here, as they would for any different summation order; they are not a gate for tp.
* This is a single comparison of one run per variant, not a proof that no sharding defect contributes. The unit tests (tp=2 vs tp=1 at f32 agree to rel L2 < 1e-5 on the tiny model) are the evidence that the sharding itself is right.
* Decision for the user: keep the strict `>= 99.5` for tp>1 (then tp=2 fails on this golden), or count reference ties as agreement (both pass at 99.65%). I left the gate as written.

## 5. Speed (`serve --tp N` + `bench speed`, sizes 128/1024/4096, gen 128, reps 3, 1 warmup)

Two floors, both at 627 GB/s per card. tp=1: 15.14 GB per token (the embedding table is a one-row gather) = 41.4 tok/s. **Plain 2-GPU byte floor** (all bytes over both cards' bandwidth): 15.14 GB / (2 x 627) = **82.8 tok/s**. **Busiest-rank floor with the replicated head**: rank 0 reads its half of the layers plus the whole lm_head, 8.19 GB (rank 1 reads 7.0 GB), = 76.6 tok/s. The ~1.2 GB imbalance from the rank-0-only head is 6.6 percentage points of the gap between the two.

| Prompt | tp=1 decode | % of 41.4 | tp=2 decode | % of 82.8 (plain) / % of 76.6 (busiest rank) | speedup | tp=1 prefill | tp=2 prefill | TTFT tp=1 / tp=2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 | 36.9 | 89.1 | 56.0 | 67.6 / 73.2 | 1.52x | 2090 | 2496 | 0.06 / 0.048 s |
| 1024 | 36.3 | 87.7 | 55.2 | 66.7 / 72.1 | 1.52x | 3380 | 4276 | 0.29 / 0.233 s |
| 4096 | 35.1 | 84.9 | 53.4 | 64.5 / 69.8 | 1.52x | 3080 | 3959 | 1.32 / 1.037 s |

Decode runs eagerly (no graphs on HIP) and the numbers include the default 1-in-16 profiled steps, for both tp=1 and tp=2. Prefill gains only 1.2 to 1.3x: prefill is compute bound, and tp=2 adds 72 all-reduces of `T x 4096` f32 per chunk.

### All-reduce cost

Per decode token: 72 all-reduces (2 per layer x 36), each 16 KiB per rank (`[1, 4096]` f32 partial).

* Micro-benchmark, back-to-back on a stream (`comm.rs --ignored`): 8 KiB bf16 5.8 us, 16 KiB 6.3 us, 64 KiB 8.2 us, 512 KiB 32 us (16.4 GB/s), 2 MiB 108 us (19.3 GB/s). With the fence drain (`INTARSIA_HIP_AR_DRAIN=1`): 11.5 us at 8 KiB, i.e. `s_wait_storecnt` halves the small-message latency; both modes pass the exactness tests.
* In the model, decode, 192 tokens (`generate --ignore-eos`, no profiling): tp=1 37.76 tok/s (26.5 ms/step); tp=2 57.98 tok/s (17.25 ms/step); tp=2 with the collectives skipped (`INTARSIA_TP_NO_COMM=1`, output is garbage, timing only) 62.58 tok/s (15.98 ms/step). So the collectives cost **1.27 ms per token, about 17.6 us per call, 7.4% of the step**.
* Profiled steps (every op synchronised, `--profile-every 1`) measure 21.7 us per call; that includes the skew between the two ranks, so it is an upper bound.
* Where the rest of the gap to the floor is (busiest-rank floor): step 17.25 ms vs 13.06 ms = 4.2 ms; 1.27 ms is the all-reduce; the remaining ~2.9 ms is eager launch overhead on two threads, the thread spawn per forward, and rank skew. The f32 partial path also doubles the bytes of the 72 reduces. Not optimised further.

## Not done

* Fused all-reduce + RMSNorm (radiance's `r4d_ar_ln_*`): the all-reduce output is read again by `rmsnorm_add`. Would save the extra pass and a launch per layer; estimated at a fraction of the 1.27 ms.
* Step 6 (35B-A3B at tp=2): GatedDeltaNet head sharding, MoE expert/intermediate sharding, NVFP4 shard loading (the block scales are in a separate layout).
* Vocab-parallel lm_head (rank 1 idles during the head, about 2 ms of the floor).
* Graph capture for tp>1; per-forward thread spawn replaced by persistent rank threads.
* `all_gather` on the P2P comm (the model does not use it; the host comm has it, tested).
* The P2P all-reduce is two-rank only; more ranks fall back to the host comm.

### Self-golden gate: tp=2 against tp=1 (same weights, long fp32 prompts, default budgets)

tp=1 wrote `--write-self-golden` during its long-golden run (top-64, prefill and decode); tp=2 was compared with `--self-golden ... --mode both --allow-same-code --revlist`.

| mode | mean KL(21-way) (<= 1.0e-4) | max position KL (<= 5.0e-3) | top-1 flips | mean abs tail diff (<= 5.0e-4) | worst prompt KL (<= 5.0e-4) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prefill (1434 pos) | 5.896e-4 FAIL | 5.024e-2 FAIL | 5 (<= 7) ok | 1.499e-4 ok | 9.833e-4 [chat_multiturn] FAIL | **FAIL** |
| decode, teacher-forced (160 pos) | 5.071e-4 FAIL | 1.464e-2 FAIL | 0 (<= 1) ok | 2.134e-4 ok | 1.971e-3 [chat_system] FAIL | **FAIL** |

**Verdict: the self-golden gate FAILS at default budgets.** Flips, tail mass and finiteness are within budget; the mean, max-position and worst-prompt KL are 5-6x, 10x and 2-4x over. No gate was loosened. The floor result says tp=2 is as close to fp32 as tp=1 is; the self-golden says the two engines' bf16 distributions differ by a mean KL of about 6e-4, which is the size of the engines' own distance to the fp32 reference (3-4e-4 each). That is consistent with different f32-partial summation order being rounded to bf16 at 72 points per token, but it is not evidence that the order explains all of it. The default budgets (1e-4) were set for same-numerics changes and are not met by a two-rank sum. Deciding on a TP-specific budget needs a measured noise floor and is left open.
