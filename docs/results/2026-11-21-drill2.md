# drill2: SIGTERM past the supervisor's 30 s grace, intarsiaml 35B on spark1

Lane `drill2`, measurement and docs only (no engine code, no default changed). It runs the one case [`2026-11-21-drill.md`](2026-11-21-drill.md) left unmeasured: SIGTERM to the supervisor of the shipping 35B server while generations are still running and will outlast the supervisor's 30 s grace (`crates/intarsia-server/src/supervisor.rs`: SIGTERM to the child, SIGKILL after 30 s).

Binary `drill-2ed220514cde` (origin/main `2ed220514`, built on spark1, fleetq #10496, `--load cpu`, 6 min 30 s), config `intarsia serve --backend cuda --model <nvidia/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4 snapshot 1355db6a> --port 8191 --host 0.0.0.0 --served-model-name guidance --max-seq 131072 --mem-budget 40 --profile-every 0 --max-batch 2 --prefix-store on`, no environment variables. Production vLLM on spark1 was never touched (its GPU process, 26,768 MiB in `nvidia-smi`, was unchanged in every snapshot). Drill job fleetq #10497 (`--mem 52 --expect 40`, 05:04 to 05:13 EST, 9 min).

## Result

**Summary: no server bug in the lifecycle (exit, port, memory, orphans all clean, 3 of 3). One thing a consumer must know: a generation that outlasts the grace is cut at 30 s, and the cut is visible to a client only as a transport error (aborted chunked body), with no `finish_reason`, no `[DONE]` and no SSE error event.** A client that treats "stream ended" as "stream complete" (localeval's streaming client does) takes the truncated reply as finished. Not fixed here.

Method: per repeat, a fresh server (R1 to R3), two streams of `max_tokens` 12,000 at temperature 0 ("write the integers 1 to 20000", about 110 tokens/s per stream, so about 110 s to finish) started 6.4 s before the signal, SIGTERM to the supervisor only (`scripts/drill/ctl.sh term <tag> sup`) at about 6 s into the generations; the child gets the signal from the supervisor as in production. Stream A goes through the OpenAI Python SDK (default `max_retries=2`), stream B through a raw httpx SSE reader that records `finish_reason`, `[DONE]`, error events and how the connection ended (`scripts/drill/longgen.py`). Times are on spark1 (50 ms polling) for the process side, and the Mac's clock minus the measured offset (0.17 to 0.19 s, round trip about 0.4 s) for the client side, so client times are good to about 0.3 s.

What happens, in order: the supervisor logs `signal 15: stopping the server` and the child stops listening at once (port free after 0.09 s: a new connection is refused from then on) but keeps decoding the two in-flight streams; at 30 s the supervisor's SIGKILL ends the child (30.03 to 30.14 s after the signal; the supervisor itself exits 0.6 to 0.8 s after the child), the kernel resets both connections, and the last content chunk of each stream reaches the client within a few tens of ms of the end (no tail is lost: last chunk to end of stream under 10 ms). The streams were at about 3,940 of 12,000 tokens (about 33 %) when cut. The supervisor writes no log line for the SIGKILL, and no line says that in-flight work was dropped (`serve-R*.log` shows only `[supervisor] signal 15: stopping the server`). Memory after the child is gone: `used` 42.42 to 42.45 GB at +0 s and +3 s against 42.75 GB before launch (the 0.3 GB difference is page cache noise: the vLLM and other tenants are on the box); the GPU apps list is back to the vLLM process only; `/dev/shm` unchanged (1 entry); nothing of ours left. Lowest `MemAvailable` in the window was 47.4 GB (the 15 GB kill line never came near). The same SIGTERM on an idle server (stage V) is gone in 0.13 s (child) / 0.82 s (supervisor), port free 0.14 s.

Restart check (stage V, fourth server): launch to `/health` 63.3 s (R1 94.2 s: first boot after the build; R2 75.1, R3 72.5), first token 0.66 s after `/health`. 20 greedy replies (`scripts/drill/verify.py`, thinking off, 100 tokens) **20 of 20 byte-identical** to the baseline taken on the R1 server before its long generations, and the per-request `cached_tokens` are equal too (279,478 tokens in total: cold store). They are also byte-identical (20 of 20, same `cached_tokens`) to the first pass of the spark2 drill's S0 (`2026-11-21-drill/raw/verify-S0-p1.json`): same replies on the two Sparks (`raw/cmp-vs-spark2-S0.txt`).

## Is it a bug?

Judgement: **not a defect, a documented limit with one sharp edge.**

- The cut is bounded and by design (30 s grace); nothing leaks and no orphan stays; the port frees at once, so a load balancer sees the server go away immediately.
- The sharp edge: the client gets HTTP 200 and then a connection that ends in the middle of the chunked body, never a final `finish_reason` chunk or `[DONE]`. A spec-following client (the OpenAI SDK) raises; a client that only reads until EOF-or-error and does not check `finish_reason` may take the shortened reply as complete. A final SSE error event, or at least a `finish_reason` chunk (for example `abort`) before the supervisor's SIGKILL, would make the cut explicit; that is an engine/server change, outside this lane. The supervisor also logs nothing about the SIGKILL (a one-line `child did not stop within 30 s, SIGKILL` would make a cut visible in the log).
- Operational consequence for a cutover: stop traffic (or wait for in-flight work) before SIGTERM when generations can run longer than 30 s (a 12,000-token generation at 110 tokens/s takes 110 s). A container runtime's 10 s stop timeout or a systemd `TimeoutStopSec` shorter than 30 s would cut earlier still.

## Not measured / caveats

- Two concurrent streams only (`--max-batch 2`, no third request queued); the streams ran at about 110 tokens/s each with nothing else on the GPU of ours. At a heavier mix the generations are slower and the cut leaves more of them unfinished, but the mechanism is the same.
- The signal went to the supervisor only (not the whole process group). In the group case (systemd control-group stop, drill S1) both processes receive SIGTERM; the child's own drain then ends when its work does or at the supervisor's 30 s, whichever first. Not re-run here.
- Client times are good to about 0.3 s (clock offset), so "29.9 s after the signal" and "child gone 30.03 to 30.14 s" agree within that error.
- spark1 had another GPU tenant (5197, 26,768 MiB) beside the vLLM line of `fleetq status`; the GPU total is therefore not used, only the process list.
