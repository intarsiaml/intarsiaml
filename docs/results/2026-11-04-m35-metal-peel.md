# m35 Metal tail peel: the Qwen3.6-35B (2026-11-04)

Question: the 27B got `prefill_peel = 8` on Metal in #297 because a prefill pass of 8 rows or fewer runs `matmul_mxfp4_gemv` and a longer one `matmul_mxfp4_nax`, with other bits, so load-dependent chunking changed output. The same flip lane found the 35B exposed too and left its recipe alone. Does the same peel fix the 35B, at which lengths is it exposed, which other Metal models are exposed, and does the peel move the model closer to or farther from an f32 reference?

**Answer.** Branch `m35/metal-peel` (not merged, not pushed) sets `prefill_peel = 8` under `[tuning.metal]` of `recipes/qwen3_5_moe.toml`. 8 is the boundary and it fixes the exposed lengths: solo equals behind-a-decoder on 7 of 7 exposed prompts (main: 0 of 7). **But it is not the same case as the 27B, and one gate fails.** The peel cuts at multiples of 256 (`peel_tail`), and the 35B's busy chunk is 2048, not 256. So the peel also splits every prompt whose length mod 256 is 2..9 above 256 tokens, where solo and busy already agreed: 120 of every 4096 lengths (2.9 %) change bits against main, 8 of them (0.2 %) were exposed. "Non-exposed lengths unchanged against main" fails on 8 of 8 tested such lengths (258, 260, 265, 516, 772, 1028, 1540, 4356; first changed token 0 to 247). The 27B has no such waste (busy chunk 256: every peeled length was exposed). Two ways out, neither implemented here (section 8): a peel whose period is the busy chunk (code), or `busy_chunk = 4096` (one line; measured here: solo equals behind on 9 of 9, nothing else moves, cost not measured).

* Exposure of the 35B on Metal: prompt length mod 4096 in 2050..2057, 8 of 4096 lengths (0.2 %). Confirmed: main flips an id on 7 of 7 such prompts (tokens 10 to 147); tails of 10 or more are bit-equal (section 1).
* Other Metal models: only the 27B and the 35B are exposed. Qwen3-8B has different solo and busy schedules and is bit-equal on 6 of 6 lengths; Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B run chunk 256 in both. MiniCPM-V-4.7 has no Metal tuning (256) and does not load here (section 3).
* Accuracy at exposed lengths (section 7): neither side is clearly closer to the reference. 27B, 27 decode windows against HF f32: mean KL 2.48e-4 before the peel, 2.61e-4 after; the peeled side is farther on 17 of 27 windows (median ratio 1.11, sign test p = 0.25); in prefill mode only the last 1 to 8 rows of a prompt differ and the per-prompt means agree (ratio 0.999). 35B, CPU oracle: see the table in section 7, one prompt moves a single row by 5 nats.
* Speed: one extra short pass on the lengths the peel touches, +20 to +35 ms TTFT at 1k to 2k tokens (+5 to +14 %), +15 ms at 6k (+1 %), 0 on controls (section 6).

Raw data: `2026-11-04-m35-metal-peel/raw/`. Scripts: `raw/scripts/`. Binaries (built from this tree, `--features metal`): `intarsia.main` (`253c3dc76`, sha256 a759e1c9...), `intarsia.peel` (sha256 022a98a2..., this branch's first commit `c50932226`; the later commits change the recipe's comments and docs only, checked at the end by building the head and comparing `plan show`), `intarsia.pre297` (this tree with `recipes/qwen3_5_vl.toml` as of `03f8dc2a3`, the only functional difference between `03f8dc2a3` and `253c3dc76`, so its 27B behaviour is the pre-#297 one; it also carries the 35B peel of this branch, which the 27B runs do not touch).

## 0. What the scheduler does with the 35B (reading)

* `crates/intarsia-core/src/scheduler.rs`, `prefill_plan`: the chunk C is chosen once, on the request's first chunk: `busy_chunk` if any sequence is decoding then, else `solo_chunk`. The 35B's Metal recipe sets 4096 and 2048 (`recipes/qwen3_5_moe.toml`). A prompt of p tokens runs passes of C rows and one final pass of the rest.
* Trace of the real server (`raw/ids35-table.txt`): p = 2050 solo `[2050]`, behind a decoder `[2048, 2]`; p = 6146 solo `[4096, 2050]`, behind `[2048, 2048, 2048, 2]` (4 passes).
* A final pass of t = 2..9 rows runs the GEMV kernels (body 1..8 rows), a longer one the NAX GEMM. So a prompt is exposed when the busy schedule ends in a pass of 2..9 rows and the solo schedule does not: p mod 2048 in 2..9 and p mod 4096 in 2050..2057. A tail of 1 row is safe (the 27B note, `2026-11-04-m27-batch-flip.md` section 1). Prompts with p mod 4096 in 2..9 end in the same short pass in both schedules (`[4096, 3]` for 4099).
* 8 of 4096 = 0.195 % of lengths. This is the flip lane's own count (section 7 of `2026-11-04-m27-batch-flip.md`: "plen mod 4096 in 2050..2057, 8 of 4096"); the "mod 2048 in 2..9" of the task brief is the busy side only, and the other half of the condition (solo does not end short) removes the lengths with p mod 4096 in 2..9. The reading and the measurement below confirm the lane's claim.
* `peel_tail(n, peel)`: a pass whose rows n exceed 256 and have n mod 256 in 1..=peel runs its first n - n mod 256 rows and then the rest. Serving cuts the headless body (p - 1 rows), so the peel touches p mod 256 in 2..9, the same residues as the exposure of the 27B, but with period 256 for every pass, not the busy chunk.

## 1. Measured exposure of the 35B, and what the peel does (`raw/ids35-table.txt`)

The boundary: 2057 (tail 9, body 8) flips on main and is fixed by the peel; 2058 (tail 10) is bit-equal on main. So 8 is the boundary for the 35B as for the 27B, and the peel value is right. The last row is the problem: at those eight lengths solo and busy already agreed on main, and the peel changes both. Counting the lengths in one period of 4096 (arithmetic, not a census): the peel changes 120 lengths, 8 of them exposed.

## 2. The change

`recipes/qwen3_5_moe.toml`, `[tuning.metal]`: `prefill_peel = 8`, with a comment that states the 256-period waste, and a correction of the comment that called the 35B's Metal prefill chunk-invariant (true except at these tails). `crates/intarsia-models/src/recipe.rs`: the unit test that pins which recipes set a peel now expects the 35B on Metal too. `docs/tuning.md`: the `INTARSIA_PREFILL_PEEL` row names the 35B. `INTARSIA_PREFILL_PEEL=0` turns it off per run. Nothing in `crates/` outside that test changed.

## 7. Accuracy at exposed lengths (the open item of #297)

`intarsia accuracy --backend metal` with the f32 references the tool already has: for the 27B `tools/golden_quark_mxfp4.py --act none --dtype float32` (HF transformers f32, MXFP4 weights dequantised, W4A16, KV f32, top-20, one full-sequence forward per prompt), for the 35B the CPU oracle of `docs/results/2026-10-22-fx1-oracle-gate.md` (`accuracy --backend cpu`, f32 kernels on bf16 tensors, `--prefill-chunk 256`, top-64 self-golden written from an ids-only golden, then used as `--golden`). The 35B oracle is the same engine on the CPU, not an independent implementation; in the #297 data that path had the largest KL to HF f32 of the engine paths, so "closer to the oracle" is weaker evidence than "closer to HF f32" is for the 27B. Both sides score the same prompts: 27B "before" is `intarsia.pre297` (the commit before #297, recipe of `03f8dc2a3`), "after" is `intarsia.main`; 35B "before" is `intarsia.main`, "after" the branch. `INTARSIA_PREFILL_PEEL=0` on the "after" binary reproduces "before" bit for bit (all per-position dumps equal: 27B set 1, 35B all sets), so the two ways of switching the peel off agree. Per-position dumps: `INTARSIA_ACC_POS_DUMP`. Metric: KL(ref || ours), 21-way (the tool's gated KL), mean over the rows named.

**What the tool exercises.** The tool runs the prompt as one forward pass of n rows (no headless split), so its pass is peeled when n mod 256 is in 1..8, one lower than the serving prompt length. Prefill mode scores all n rows, but only the last t = n mod 256 rows differ between the sides. Decode mode prefills n - 64 (27B) or n - 32 (35B) tokens and then feeds the reference tokens one at a time, so a different prefill state shows up in every row of the window. A prompt counts as "reached" only when the two sides' per-position output differs; byte-identical means the peel did not run (the trap of #297's accuracy gate). The pairs below are only those.

**27B (HF f32 reference).** 11 prefill prompts and 27 decode windows (64 rows) reached, at tool lengths n mod 256 in 1..8 over bases 512, 768, 1024, 1280, 1536 (`raw/acc27/`).

| | prompts reached | mean KL before | mean KL after | after / before (mean, median over prompts) | after closer / farther | sign test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prefill, all rows of the prompt | 11 | 1.6204e-4 | 1.6195e-4 | 0.999, 1.000 | 5 / 6 | p = 1.0 |
| decode window, 64 rows | 27 | 2.4803e-4 | 2.6072e-4 | **1.051, 1.110** | 10 / **17** | p = 0.25 |

The prefill rows that differ are the last 1 to 8 rows of each prompt (50 rows: after closer on 24, farther on 26). In the decode windows 1728 rows differ: after closer on 809, farther on 919 (rows of one prompt are correlated, so this is not a test). The prompts are slices of one corpus (`corpus_docs.txt`) at offsets a few tens of tokens apart, so the windows are not independent samples, which makes p = 0.25 optimistic and the "not resolved" reading safer. The mean is pulled by single prompts in both directions (d1092 and d1605 improve by 43 % and 61 %, d1347 worsens by 38 %).

**Is either side closer?** Not shown. The pooled difference is 5 % of the mean KL and well inside its standard error (paired difference -1.3e-5 +- 2.0e-5, t = -0.6). But the point estimate and the sign count lean the same way as #297's section 3a: the post-#297 path (GEMV tail) is farther from HF f32 on 17 of 27 windows, by a median 11 %. With 27 windows I cannot tell 11 % from nothing (p = 0.25), and I do not explain the lean away: it may be real and small. Earlier evidence ("neither should be closer") is consistent with the data only in the sense that a difference this size is not resolved.

**Is either side closer?** Not shown, and the two summaries point in opposite directions. The mean says the peeled side is 2.4 times farther, and all of that is one window: `d2082` (serving length 2051), where row 18 of 32 moves from 0.23 to 5.25 nats (a high-gain row, like the "single rows move by nats" of #297 section 3) and the other 31 rows average 0.019 before and 0.028 after, farther on 18 of 31. That window is in the data and stays in the mean. Without it the peeled side is 9 % closer, and on the other 8 windows it is closer on 7 (all but `d2086`, +17 %). Sign tests do not resolve either reading (p = 0.18 and 0.45). The 27B windows lean to the other side (17 of 27 farther, median 1.11); here 7 of 9 lean closer. Together: no consistent sign, and a single high-gain row can outweigh everything else, which is what #297 found for the paths of the 27B. The data does not show the peel moving either model closer to its reference. Limits: nine windows, all on the CPU oracle (an f32-kernel run of the same engine, not an independent implementation), one corpus.

## 8. What I would do, and what is open

* The recipe-only peel is correct for the exposed lengths and wasteful for the rest: 112 of its 120 changed lengths per 4096 were not exposed. Choose between (a) a peel whose period is the busy chunk (`peel_tail` takes the period from the recipe; touches only p mod 4096 in 2050..2057 on the 35B; for the 27B the period 256 is unchanged; code in `scheduler.rs`, `recipe.rs`, `model.rs`, `plan.rs`, with a bit-equality gate against main at every other length) and (b) `busy_chunk = 4096` (section 5; no numerics change at all; costs the neighbour stall). (a) has not been tried and its gate (every non-exposed length bit-equal to main) is the one that fails here.
* If the branch is merged as it is, the coordinator accepts a bits change on 2.9 % of lengths for a fix of 0.2 %, and the `|peel{n}` key of the prefix store changes for the 35B on Metal.
* Not established: the stall of option (b); a 35B reference other than the CPU oracle (vLLM's golden is top-20 with an fp8 KV and not at these lengths); `--max-batch` above 2; the CUDA and HIP 35B recipes (not run, not changed); why Qwen3-8B is row-invariant at M <= 8.
