# DSv4.1 TP=2 decode: fold and group the dense helpers (lane dsv41/group, 2026-11-02)

Branch `dsv41/group` from origin/main `7d659df4e`. Spark dir `~/intarsia-dsv41-group`. Raw: `2026-11-02-dsv41-group/w1/`. Analysis script: `scripts/dsv41-tp2/densecrit.py`.

## Result

**No gain; stopped at step 1 of the brief. Nothing was built and no engine code changed (the branch holds only the analysis script, this note and a LOG row).** On a current-main nsys trace the dense work that D1 wanted to remove is mostly gone already: the big EXL3 shapes run at or under the byte floor, and what is left of D1 is the `rot_in` and `split_finish` helpers, a ceiling of about 1.0 ms a pass (0.4 ms a token) with zero-cost fusion, and about 0.3 to 0.4 ms a pass (0.13 to 0.17 ms a token, under 0.5 tok/s) after the 40 % that earlier bench savings reached the real pass. The gap to the gate is about 1 ms a token (2.3 ms a pass). The brief's stop rule ("under about 1 ms a pass") applies.

## 2. What is left to gain, and why I stopped

- Ceiling: removing every `rot_in` and `split_finish` launch for free is at most their crit share, 0.43 + 0.60 = **1.0 ms a pass** (an over-count: instants shared by parallel lanes are charged to one of them, and tracing inflates the helpers).
- A tally on one attention layer of `w1/pass-timeline-r0.txt` (a 5-row pass, from `hc_sub_finish` to the next): the helpers on the dense chain are the `wq_a`/`wkv` lane (`rot_in` 2 us, `split_finish` 2.4 us), `rot_in` before `wq_b` (3 to 6 us), the `wo_a` lanes (`rot_in` 2 to 4, `split_finish` 3 to 5), transpose and `rot_in` before `wo_b` (1.6 + 2.4): about 18 us a layer, **about 0.7 ms a pass** at 40 attention layers.
- Real value: fusing `split_finish` into the last-arriving block needs a per-call arrival counter that resets itself under graph replay, and the reducing block adds its own latency; folding `rot_in` into a producer's epilogue spans different ops (rmsnorm, hc, attention output), each a new kernel variant. The earlier bench-to-pass ratio (the cp A/B reached 40 % of its bench) puts 0.7 to 1.0 ms at **0.3 to 0.4 ms a pass, 0.13 to 0.17 ms a token**, about 0.4 tok/s of the 2 tok/s the gate needs. Even the zero-cost ceiling is 0.43 ms a token.
- Grouping several matrices in one launch (TF's `glinear`) has nothing big left to take: the groups it would form (`wq_a` + `wkv` + compressor, the four `wo_a` slices) already run side by side on lanes, and their main kernels are within 0.3 ms of the floor in sum.
- So step 2 (microbench) and step 3 (switch, A/B) were not started.

## 3. Observations for whoever picks the gap up

- The f16 gate (`dense_f16w_vec_kernel<..., 96>`, 40 calls, 24 us median against a 14 us floor) is 0.4 ms of excess a pass and is not an EXL3 kernel; outside this lane's hypothesis, not tested.
- MoE (19.1 ms) and NCCL (4.8) dominate crit; the dense class is 11.7.
- A node-level trace splits a replayed pass over many stream ids, so "pass stream versus lane stream" cannot be read from stream numbers; `densecrit.py` does not try.
