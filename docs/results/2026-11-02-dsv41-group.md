# DSv4.1 TP=2 decode: fold and group the dense helpers (lane dsv41/group, 2026-11-02)

Branch `dsv41/group` from origin/main `7d659df4e`. Spark dir `~/intarsia-dsv41-group`. Raw: `2026-11-02-dsv41-group/w1/`. Analysis script: `scripts/dsv41-tp2/densecrit.py`.

## Result

**No gain; stopped before building (step 1 sits at the line, step 2's rule stops it by arithmetic). Nothing was built and no engine code changed (the branch holds only the analysis script, this note and a LOG row).** On a current-main nsys trace the dense work D1 wanted to remove is mostly gone already: the big EXL3 shapes run at or under the byte floor, and what is left is the `rot_in` and `split_finish` helpers (crit ceiling 1.0 ms a pass, 0.43 ms a token, with zero-cost fusion) and the small-shape excess the lanes already overlap. The most any microbench could save is about 2.3 ms a pass; at the 40 % that bench savings reached the real pass that is under 1 ms a pass, and every term of it over-counts. The gap to the gate is about 1 ms a token (2.3 ms a pass).

The lesson is the one of the PDL lane: D1's estimate was read off TensorFold's structure, which has no side streams and no L2 prefetch. Here lanes and prefetch had already taken what grouping and folding would buy.

## 2. What is left to gain, and why I stopped

Step 1 ("if under about 1 ms a pass, stop"):

- Ceiling: removing every dense `rot_in` and `split_finish` launch for free is at most their crit share, 0.43 + 0.60 = **1.0 ms a pass**, which is at the line, not clearly under it. It over-counts: instants shared by parallel lanes are charged to one of them.
- A tally on one attention layer of `w1/pass-timeline-r0.txt` (a 5-row pass, from `hc_sub_finish` to the next): the helpers on the dense chain are the `wq_a`/`wkv` lane (`rot_in` 2 us, `split_finish` 2.4 us), `rot_in` before `wq_b` (3 to 6 us), the `wo_a` lanes (`rot_in` 2 to 4, `split_finish` 3 to 5), transpose and `rot_in` before `wo_b` (1.6 + 2.4): about 18 us a layer, **about 0.7 ms a pass** at 40 attention layers. That is under 1 ms.

Step 2 ("scale the bench by 40 %; if it can't deliver 1 ms a pass, stop"), settled without a bench because no bench can save more than the kernel time of the things it replaces:

- the helpers' whole kernel time: `rot_in` 0.85 + `split_finish` 0.87 = 1.72 ms (sum, all streams),
- the grouping excess over the floor: `wo_a` +0.26, `wq_a` + `wkv` +0.31 (sum; the lanes already overlap it),
- total about 2.3 ms a pass, times 0.4 = **about 0.9 ms a pass at the very most**, and every term over-counts: a fused finish is not free (it needs a self-resetting arrival counter under graph replay, and the reducing block adds latency), a folded `rot_in` spans different producers (rmsnorm, hc, attention output) so each is a new kernel variant, and the lanes hide part of the rest. Realistically 0.3 to 0.4 ms a pass, 0.13 to 0.17 ms a token, under 0.5 tok/s of the 2 tok/s the gate needs.
- Grouping several matrices in one launch (TF's `glinear`) has nothing big left to take: the groups it would form (`wq_a` + `wkv` + compressor, the four `wo_a` slices) already run side by side on lanes, and their main kernels are within 0.3 ms of the floor in sum.

So steps 2 (microbench) and 3 (switch, A/B) were not started.

## 3. Observations for whoever picks the gap up

- The f16 gate (`dense_f16w_vec_kernel<..., 96>`, 40 calls, 24 us median against a 14 us floor) is 0.4 ms of excess a pass and is not an EXL3 kernel; outside this lane's hypothesis, not tested.
- MoE (19.1 ms) and NCCL (4.8) dominate crit; the dense class is 11.7.
- A node-level trace splits a replayed pass over many stream ids, so "pass stream versus lane stream" cannot be read from stream numbers; `densecrit.py` does not try.
