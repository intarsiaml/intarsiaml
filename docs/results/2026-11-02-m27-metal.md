# M27: Qwen3.8-27B on Metal (endor, Apple M5 Max): it already loaded, prefill ran at 40 tok/s, now 800+

Lane `m27`, branch `lane-m27/metal`, base `origin/main` `4794bbc05`, 2026-11-02 (EDT). Box endor, 128 GB, `endor-kev` and `endor-tensorfold` untouched.
Checkpoint `amd/Qwen3.8-27B-Quark-AWQ-MXFP4` (snapshot `5233554c`), already on endor at `~/models/Qwen3.8-27B-Quark-AWQ-MXFP4` (19.8 GB file, 18.03 GB resident
weights); nothing was copied or downloaded. Quant: MXFP4 weights, bf16 activations (W4A16, the recipe's default; the checkpoint declares W4A4). Every GPU or timing
run held a fleetq slot (owner `claude-m27`, `--quiet gpu,cpu` for timing); the job ids are in the sections below. Raw logs: `2026-11-02-m27-metal/raw/`.

## 0. Result

* **Task 1: there is no load failure on main.** `intarsia generate --backend metal` on `4794bbc05` loads the checkpoint in 0.4 s and says "Paris." for
  "The capital of France is" (fleetq #7853, `raw/repro/generate-main-4794bbc.log`); `serve` loads it in 21 s and `kernels test` admits `matmul_mxfp4_gemv` on Metal
  (48 kernels, 941 cases, 0 FAIL on base, `raw/kernels/base-full.log`). The scoreboard line "27B ckpt does not load" (section 1c, source M6) is **stale**; the Metal MXFP4
  GEMV is commit `927947011` and the f32 `A_log` fix came with the NMD lane (docs/results/2026-10-27-nmd-new-model-drill-metal.md). Where the phrase came from is an inference, not a checked fact: the other 27B on
  endor, `~/models/qwen38-27b`, is the MLX affine 4-bit checkpoint, which the MLX reader (NAB, #205) dequantises to bf16 (54 GB, load peak about 2x that, NAB friction item 8),
  which cannot fit the 60 GB free next to the standing services. It was not loaded to confirm (endor swaps rather than OOM-kills).
* **The real blocker was prefill speed.** MXFP4 on Metal had a GEMV only, and its 8-row form re-reads the weights every 8 rows: **40.9 tok/s at 1k (24.3 s for 994
  tokens) and 39.5 tok/s at 4k (103.8 s)** on main (`raw/prefill/base-1k.err`, `base-4k.err`, plain `generate`, fleetq #7861). A 16k prompt would take about 7 minutes
  (projected from the 4k rate, not measured: the localeval sweep on main was stopped after 11 minutes, `raw/base-speed/`), and MMLU n=1425 would have taken many hours.
* **Fix: `matmul_mxfp4_nax`** (kernels/metal/mxfp4.metal `mxfp4_w_to_bf16`, crates/intarsia-backend-metal/src/mxfp4.rs): for M > 8 the activations are QDQ'd as in the GEMV,
  the weights are decoded to bf16 into the plan scratch (e2m1 x 2^e, exact in bf16) and the existing fp8 NAX GEMM (`gemm_nax_fp8.metal`, scale pair 1/0) runs on the neural
  accelerators. It is the `matmul_nvfp4_nax` structure with a different decoder; no new GEMM. Plus two small tunings (chunk 2048, GEMV loads), section 4.

Served numbers are from the build before the GEMV load change of section 4 (`speed` = fleetq #7878 and #7879); that change adds about +3 to 4 % decode (MTP off) and +8 % (MTP k2)
in the `generate` A/B and was not re-measured through localeval (the queue behind another lane's 2-hour GPU job; the final-build jobs were cancelled, #7901).

## 1. What was measured how

### Task 1, reproduction and fit

* fleetq #7853 (`--quiet gpu,cpu --mem 25`): `intarsia generate --model ... --backend metal -p "The capital of France is" --max-tokens 16`, prefill chunk 256, W4A16, 18.03 GB
  weights, "Paris. The capital of Germany is Berlin." Decode 19.6 tok/s in a 16-token run.
* Fit: weights 18.03 GB + KV (context 20000: the 16 full-attention layers only) + scratch (the NAX scratch is the largest projection decoded to bf16, 178 MB, plus the
  activations) under the 60 GB free next to kev and tensorfold; fleetq `--mem 30` was accepted for every serve run and the services were never touched. Peak resident
  size was not measured.

### Task 2, kernels and tests

* New: `mxfp4_w_to_bf16` (one thread per 32-weight group, also writes the NAX GEMM's scale pair), `matmul_mxfp4_nax` (three dispatches: `mxfp4_xprep_bf16`, decode,
  `gemm_nax_fp8_*`; registered at priority 5 for M > 8, K % 32 == 0, N*K <= 128M elements, W4A8 excluded, hidden without the metal4.0 library; `INTARSIA_METAL_MXFP4_NAX=0` turns it off).
* `intarsia kernels test --backend metal`: **49 kernels, 972 cases, 0 FAIL** (base: 48 / 941 / 0); the only change is the new row `matmul_mxfp4_nax 31 cases 0 fail max_abs 1.961e-3
  rel_l2 4.118e-5 ADMITTED`, and the `matmul_mxfp4_gemv` row is identical to base to every printed digit (3.401e-3 / 2.883e-5; fleetq #7870 and the final run,
  `raw/kernels/`). The shared `matmul_mxfp4` cases already cover M = 9, 40 and 300, act dynamic and none, bf16 and f32 out, so no case was added to `intarsia-core`
  (shared with CUDA and HIP); the new kernel is covered by a Metal test file instead:
* New tests `crates/intarsia-backend-metal/tests/mxfp4_nax.rs` (4 tests, pass, `raw/kernels/cargo-test-mxfp4_nax.log`): (1) W4A16 output is **bitwise** `matmul_bf16_nax` on the
  decoded weights (6 shapes up to 5120 x 5120 and 257 rows; f32 out rounds to the same bf16); (2) a row's bits do not depend on the call's M, its position, the tile or the
  output offset, for act 0 and 1; (3) against the GEMV the relative RMS difference is below 1e-5 (only the K accumulation order differs); (4) registration: M <= 8 and W4A8 stay off,
  M > 8 on, priority above the GEMV. The scratch is deliberately bound at a 4-byte-aligned offset in the tests.
* `cargo test --workspace --release`: **1199 passed, 0 failed, 29 ignored** (`--no-fail-fast`, own target dir, fleetq #7918; the first run, #7902, had 1 failure: `recipe_27b_chunk` pinned the old Metal chunk, updated here)

## 2. Prefill chunk (plain `generate`, fleetq #7875, 2 reps, `raw/prefill/chunk-sweep.txt`)

| chunk | 4k tok/s | 16k tok/s |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 256 (builtin) | 648 | 540 |
| 512 | 710 | 668 |
| 1024 | 716 | 688 |
| **2048** | **807** | **758** |
| 4096 | 782 | not run (stopped) |

Each chunk decodes every projection's weights to bf16 again, so a bigger chunk amortises that; 2048 is the best row and matches HIP's 27B setting. `recipes/qwen3_5_vl.toml`
gets `[tuning.metal] prefill_chunk = 2048` and `busy_chunk = 256` (a prompt arriving beside decoding streams would otherwise stall them for a 2048-row step, 2.5 s at this rate;
the busy path was **not** run: `serve --max-batch > 1` for the 27B on Metal was not tested, there is no row-exact MXFP4 kernel). `--prefill-chunk N` overrides. The 8k/16k
chunk-sensitivity of the prefix-reuse gate was not run.

## 3. MTP

`--spec mtp --spec-k 2 --mtp-draft-vocab 65536` works on Metal for the 27B out of the box (the head is in the checkpoint). Greedy text, 160 tokens, two prompts, `--spec off` vs k2 vs k3:
**byte-identical in all four pairs** (`raw/mtp/`, #7876, on the build before the GEMV load change; not repeated on the final build). Acceptance 54 % (story) and 77 % (code) at k2, tokens per pass 2.08 / 2.55; k3 accepts less per
draft and is not faster (22.5 -> 20.4 and 27.5 -> 26.7 tok/s on the first build), so k2. It is **not made the default** here: the recipe has no Metal MTP key, a default flip
is a decision (HIP's was the user's), and the gain is 1.17x at the served sweep. A one-line `[tuning.metal] mtp_default_k = 2` would do it if wanted.

## 4. Tuning tried (all A/B in one slot, interleaved, decode-only unless stated)

## 6. What did not work or was not done

* The first baseline sweep (localeval on main) was stopped after 11 minutes: at 40 tok/s the 16k rows alone would take about 40 minutes. Main's prefill is from plain `generate` at 1k and 4k only.
* The golden was regenerated, not copied (tatooine is off limits for this lane), so the HIP comparison is of the same recipe, not the same bytes.
* GEMV rows-per-simdgroup (section 4) lost. The MXFP4 decode GEMV still reaches only about 365 GB/s.
* Not run: MMLU with MTP on, the prefix-reuse / chunk-sensitivity gate at chunk 2048 (golden prompts are at most 700 tokens, so `accuracy` cannot see it), batched serving (`--max-batch > 1`),
  W4A4 (`--mxfp4-act dynamic`) accuracy on Metal, 32k context, peak resident memory, a 16k baseline on main, GSM8K.
* The final build (GEMV load change) did not get its own accuracy run, MMLU, MTP-ids check or localeval sweep: they were queued behind another lane's long GPU job (#7899, estimated to 18:16) and cancelled (#7901). Evidence for "bitwise" is the `kernels test` rows identical to every printed digit, identical greedy text in 3 A/B rounds (MTP off) and the unchanged accumulation order in the source.
* No fleetq slot is held.
