# Metal 35B decode: folding the byte-free launches (lane mfold, Mac roadmap idea 9)

Lane `mfold`, 2026-11-16, endor (Apple M5 Max). Base: main `7ff031d44` (#410, KV-once default-on); the first profile and
pilot were on `c8367d5a7`. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4, Metal, 4k prompt. Scripts: `scripts/mfold/`. Raw:
[2026-11-16-mfold/raw/](2026-11-16-mfold/raw/) (per-op tables and run logs; the per-dispatch `.jsonl` traces, 100 MB,
are not committed).

**Verdict: stop rule hit, findings only. No engine code is in this PR.** The top-ranked fold (the shared expert in two
launches instead of five, 121 of 764 launches removed, bitwise) does not gain 1 %: -0.8 % plain and -1.6 % MTP default in a
quiet 8-pair ABBA-BAAB. A launch that moves no bytes costs about 1 us on the serial encoder, not the 3 to 4 us the
"400 launches = 1.3 to 1.7 ms" reading of roadmap section 1.3 implies; that time is the latency of the small kernels
themselves, and folding them into a neighbour does not remove it. The kernels and their bitwise test are kept on branch
`mfold-kernels` (commit `44cc9378e`), not on main.

Two corrections to how section 1.3 reads:

* The `matmul_nvfp4_gemv` row (121 launches, 5.8 us each) is the lm_head (about 0.49 ms, one launch) plus 120 shared-expert
  GEMVs of about 1.8 us each. The shared expert is nearly free already.
* The launches that move no bytes sum to about 1.65 ms, but that is kernel time, not launch overhead: `silu_glu` on 2048
  elements is a whole launch for 1.1 us, and phase-b2b measured 0.9 us for a dependent dispatch on the serial encoder. The
  expensive ones are single-threadgroup kernels with fixed-order reductions (`rmsnorm_add` 5.4, top-k 8.1, delta rule 10.5 us).
  A fold saves at most about 1 us per launch it removes: all 400 would be about 0.4 ms (5 %), not 1.3 to 1.7 ms.

Launch order of a GatedDeltaNet layer (19 launches; an attention layer has 21): `rmsnorm_add`, fp8 qkv, fp8 z, pair b / a,
conv, delta rule, `rmsnorm` (32 rows), `silu_glu`, fp8 out, `rmsnorm_add`, pair router, top-k, experts gate-up, experts
down, nvfp4 shared gate, nvfp4 shared up, `silu_glu`, nvfp4 shared down, combine.

## 4. Why it does not pay, and what that means for the rest of the list

1. The launch is not the cost. About 1 us per dependent dispatch; 121 removed launches are at best 0.12 ms (1.4 %), and
   the fused kernels probably give some of it back (not isolated): twice the threadgroups in the gate + up launch, and a combine epilogue that
   now runs on lane 0 of each simdgroup of a GEMV instead of one thread per element.
2. The byte-free time is in three kernels: `rmsnorm_add` (0.43 ms), top-k (0.32 ms), the delta rule (0.31 ms). Candidates
   2 and 3 put them in one threadgroup with their neighbours, which keeps their latency and saves about 1 us per removed
   launch: 0.06 to 0.09 ms each, below what this fold already failed to deliver with more launches removed.
3. What could still move the plain step is making those three kernels faster on their own (a wider or tree reduction
   changes the sum order: opt-in, not bitwise), and the fp8 GEMV bandwidth (373 against 560 GB/s, 0.6 ms, mdec's item 3).
   Neither is a fold.

Idea 9 should come off the roadmap's list as measured: with the two related attempts (mdl's norm folds -3.5 %, mdec's `_xd` prep
-2.0 %) this is the third that loses (-0.8 % / -1.6 %).
