### 1.4 Top three gaps to TensorFold, per token

1. **The hyper-connection chain at the start of a layer (a>b, +1.6 ms a pass, 0.77 ms a token).** Main runs, one after another on the critical path, `hc_sub_partial` (the mixing dots over K = 28672, 20 us, 29 us when the post-MoE L2 prefetch overlaps it) and `hc_sub_finish` (norm and the 20-iteration Sinkhorn, 9 us, 14 us under the prefetch). TensorFold puts the dots and Sinkhorn of the next sublayer on a side stream beside the sublayer's own kernels (`TF_DS_HC_SIDE`).
2. **The dense projections' launch chain (+0.9 d>e, +0.6 b>c; 0.7 ms a token).** 1343 dense launches a pass against 274. wo_b runs at 262 GB/s (DRAM rate, 49 us with or without prefetch overlap), so its bytes are not hidden: the L2 prefetch covers wo_a and part of wo_b only (16 MiB a launch).
3. **The router chain (f>g, +1.0 ms a pass, 0.5 ms a token)** and the draft round (Markov loop 1.34 ms against a 1.04 ms floor for four reads of the 66 MB head; the draft block's 3 layers 3.6 ms against 3.8 for TensorFold).

The nsys gaps between chunks (1.66 ms a round on main, 0.12 on TensorFold) are not in the engine's own accounting (period minus draft minus pass is 0.23 ms), so they are mostly trace artefact of the node-level graph tracing, not host time. Not used.

L2-prefetch contention (main, `bisect` of the 3-row passes in the ab0 trace): kernels overlapped by a prefetch launch take longer: `hc_sub_partial` 29.0 against 20.2 us, `hc_sub_finish` 13.6 against 9.0, `ik_rdma_collect` 16.0 against 10.0, `rot_in` 3.8 against 1.4. The shipped prefetch wins net (-0.56 ms a pass in the graphs lane, +2 % in decode2).

### 3.1 A/B rule, written before the data

Same binary, `INTARSIA_DSV41_HC_SIDE` unset (A) against `1` (B), order A B A B, each arm a fresh start of both ranks, `STEPS=lcat` (categories-rcp + chat-rcp, 32 requests x 256 tokens, localeval), `INTARSIA_DSV41_SPEC_TRACE=1` in both. Primary: all-kinds decode tok/s of `catspeed.py` per arm; the gain of a pair is B over its A. Secondary: the untraced pass ms by rows (`roundcost.py`), which is deterministic to about 0.1 ms.
- **Default on** if both pairs gain at least 3.0 %, no kind loses more than 1 %, and the golden ids (10 prompts x 64) are identical to main spec on and spec off.
- **Opt-in, default off, reported as a decision** if the mean gain is 1.0 to 3.0 % (the output is bitwise, so the coordinator may lower the bar).
- **Dead** below 1.0 % or if either pair is negative; the code stays on the branch, documented.
- Any golden difference ends it whatever the speed.

## 4. Step 2 result: the remaining gap, by lever

The round is 45.5 ms against TensorFold's 36.3 ms under nsys (+9.2 ms). It is spread over many items: the layer `rest` (+4.6 ms a pass: hyper-connection chain 1.6, router chain 1.0, wo_a/wo_b 0.9, wq_a/wkv/wq_b 0.6, others 0.5), the pass prologue (+2.0, the previous round's Markov loop and the first launch), nsys-only gaps. Every single item is under the 0.52 ms a token (1.25 ms a round) that 3 % needs, except the HC chain at 1.6 ms of which the build recovered 0.6. Fusing the hyper-connection sublayer into the neighbouring kernels, or a TensorFold-style one-kernel attention front, would be new kernels (not bitwise to the current reduction orders without care) and none of them is above 3 % on its own measurement. **Nothing above 3 % remains as one bitwise lever.** What would still add up, if the user wants it: HC_SIDE on (+1.4 %), the router chain in one launch (about 0.5 ms a pass, +1 %), and a quantised Markov head (about 0.8 ms a round, +1.9 %, drafts change, so opt-in).
