## 1. Verdict

Measurement lane, no engine change, nothing default-on. All numbers are from **spark2 with a GPU that did not boost** (section 9): about 450 MHz SM clock and 12 W while serving, a cold 16K prompt takes 11 s instead of about 3 s. Ratios between arms are
what the cells measure; absolute seconds are about 3.6x flipev's and are not comparable to its tables.

* **herd at `--max-batch 16` (cell 1): wins, no loss found.** 16 requests on a shared 16448-token prefix: TTFT sum 1498 s -> 247 s (-83 %), last TTFT 175 s -> 20 s (-89 %), wall 177 s -> 28 s. 96 / 96 herd-on replies equal their solo reference (96 / 96 off).
* **herd + sjf together (cell 2): they compose; the short prompt gets a slot at `--max-batch 16` and is served in 2.3 s** (neither 87 s, herd alone 14 s, sjf alone 2.3 s). The price of sjf on the herd group is about 1.2 s on every herd member
  (last TTFT 15.1 -> 16.3 s, sum +9 %): the short prompt's 1K prefill pauses the leader. 216 / 216 replies equal solo in all four arms.
* **sjf `INTARSIA_PREFILL_AGE` 4 / 16 vs 8 (cell 3): no difference** (long TTFT 35.48 / 35.47 / 35.44 s, decode tokens in the window 185 / 183 / 183, short TTFTs equal to 0.03 s): with two shorts the aging bound is never reached. Cell 3b (a burst of 12 shorts, section 5.2): age 8 and 32 identical, age 2 delays the shorts' tail by 1.5 s (max 7.9 -> 9.4 s) for +0.05 s on the long prompt; nothing argues for changing the default 8. sjf vs fifo with that burst: shorts 34 to 39 s -> 3 to 8 s, the 40K prompt's first token +8.2 s (+24 %), the decoders' worst window gap 0.8 -> 1.8 s.
* **The "one unrelated request slower" in flipev's mixed cell is arrival-order noise, not herd (cell 4).** With a fixed arrival order (20 ms apart, 6 cells per arm in each of two orders) the two unrelated 8K prompts are unchanged to
  +0.00 to +0.03 s (sd 0.01 to 0.09 s); in flipev's order the slower of the pair improves by 21.6 s because it no longer waits behind four prefills. One small effect remains: in the order "unrelated pair first" the herd leader is +0.30 s (23.69 -> 23.99 s, sd 0.2 to 0.3, n = 6).
  With the arrival order left to thread scheduling (8 cells per arm) the same prompts swap TTFTs between cells (4.96 / 10.30 s in one arm, 10.30 / 4.95 s in the other): that is the 0.35 s flipev saw.
* **3 and 4 decoders (cell 5, `--max-batch 8`): sjf does not make the tail worse** (max gap 781 to 787 ms vs 785 to 787 ms fifo, p99 equal), shorts get a first token in 1.6 to 2.0 s instead of 31 to 33 s, the long prompt's TTFT is 1.6 to 1.7 s (5 %) later,
  and the per-stream decode rate in the window is unchanged (1.8 to 1.9 tok/s/stream both arms); at 2 decoders and `--max-batch 4` (cell 3) it is 13 % lower (2.6 vs 3.0).
* **ids: every reply of every cell equals its solo reference** (section 8).

## 2. Method

Spark2 (GB10, CUDA, vLLM `retro` 38 GB resident, earlyoom active), `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4`, default MTP recipe (k2; batched MTP N=2 at `--max-batch > 1`), binary built from origin/main `6196faa46` (fleetq #10023, `--load cpu --mem 55`,
`CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=3 MAX_JOBS=3 FLASHINFER_NVCC_THREADS=1`, a free-memory watchdog inside `build.sh`; lowest MemAvailable during the build 74 GB). One server per visit, one fleetq job per phase (`--quiet gpu,cpu --mem 62`),
a free-memory watchdog (`memwatch.sh`, kills the server below 15 GB available) on every serve; the lowest MemAvailable in any serve visit was 35.5 GB, nothing was killed. Prompts are token ids (random ids from a seed,
`/v1/completions`, temperature 0, `ignore_eos`, 48 tokens, as flipev); TTFT is the client's time from each request's own send to its first streamed token. Every concurrent cell is followed by the same prompts one at a time (the id reference).
Scripts `scripts/flipev2/`: `run.sh` (phases build, hs), `run2.sh` (diag, mixd), `run3.sh` (age, dec), `run4.sh` (ageburst), `herd_bench.py`, `q4.py`, `table.py`, `build.sh`, `memwatch.sh`; raw data in `raw/`.

Changes against flipev's scripts (so numbers are comparable only in direction, not absolute):

* clients run **on spark2** (`ssh spark2 python3 ...`, `127.0.0.1`), flipev's ran from wherever `fleetq run` was launched;
* `herd_bench.py`: mode `hs` (shared prefix plus one unrelated 1000-token prompt released at `--short-at`), `--arrival` (explicit arrival order, `--stagger` apart);
* `q4.py`: `--visit`, `--dec-tokens`, and `dec_alive_through_window` (every decoder still streaming at the long prompt's first token; runs where it is false are dropped as void);
* cell 4 runs at `--max-batch 16 --max-seq 24576` (flipev: `--max-batch 8 --max-seq 49152`); 6 requests never bind slots either way;
* the `age` phase adds a fifo arm (flipev's sjf-off) so every number has a same-session reference;
* **spark2 prefill is much slower than flipev's spark1 numbers**: a cold 16448-token prompt takes about 11 s here (about 1.5K tokens/s, 0.84 s per 2048-row chunk, constant over all visits) against about 3 s in flipev (spark1) for the same prompt; the GPU
  showed 96 % utilisation at 12 W and 440 MHz SM clock while serving. Not diagnosed beyond the `diag` phase (section 9); it scales every absolute prefill time (FIFO waits of 11 s per queued 16K prompt) but the same binary and box ran every arm.

`serve --max-seq 24576 --max-batch 16 --prefix-store on` (visits N H S B B S H N, cell 1 only in the N and H visits), 3 seeds per visit, so **6 cells per arm** (visit order N, H, H, N). 16 requests, 16248 shared + 200 own tokens each (16448 + 200 = 16648 tokens), released together.

| herd | cells | TTFT sum s | TTFT p50 s | TTFT last s | wall s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 6 | 1497.87 (1489.58 to 1516.10) | 93.72 | 175.43 (174.55 to 176.68) | 176.52 |
| on | 6 | 247.15 (246.87 to 247.51) | 15.34 | 20.08 (20.04 to 20.10) | 27.91 |
| change | | -83.5 % | -83.6 % | -88.5 % | -84.2 % |

Per request (sorted TTFT, mean over the 6 cells): off 11.2, 22.1, 33.8 ... 175.4 s (one 16K prefill, about 11 s, per request: FIFO); on 11.1, 11.7, 12.2 ... 20.1 s (the leader prefills once, each follower restores and prefills only its 200 own tokens, about 0.6 s apiece).
Prefill tokens of the whole visit: 1.44 M off, 0.30 M on. cached tokens on herd-on: 16384 for 15 of 16 requests. Seeds 121 / 122 / 123 agree to 1 % (sum 1490 / 1509 / 1495 off, 247.1 / 247.2 / 247.2 on). No follower is slower with herd on; the leader is equal (11.2 vs 11.1 s).

## 4. Cell 2: herd + sjf together, 8 x 16K shared + one unrelated 1K prompt at +2 s, `--max-batch 16`

Four server visits per arm pair, ABBA over the arms (N H S B B S H N), 3 seeds each: **6 cells per arm**. Slots: 9 requests in 16 slots (hsjf's mflip cell had 9 in 8, which is why the short prompt waited there). Herd-8 = the eight shared-prefix requests; the short is request 9.

| arm | herd-8 TTFT sum s | herd-8 TTFT last s | **short TTFT s** | short last token (abs) s | wall s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| neither | 398.97 (397.4 to 400.3) | 88.21 | 86.96 (86.6 to 87.4) | 90.62 | 90.62 |
| herd | 104.88 (104.4 to 105.6) | 15.11 | 13.98 (13.9 to 14.1) | 21.30 | 21.30 |
| sjf | 413.29 (409.6 to 420.1) | 89.92 | **2.34 (2.32 to 2.37)** | 20.53 | 91.11 |
| herd + sjf | 114.19 (113.7 to 115.3) | 16.29 | **2.34 (2.31 to 2.39)** | 18.07 | 21.60 |

* Herd is the big effect on the group (-74 % sum, last 88 -> 15 s); sjf is the big effect on the short prompt (87 / 14 s -> 2.3 s). Together the short gets 2.3 s and the group still finishes at 16.3 s.
* Price of sjf on the herd group: the short prompt arrives at +2 s, pauses the leader's prefill for its own 1K prefill and first token, so the leader's first token moves 11.1 -> 12.3 s and every follower by the same 1.2 s: group sum +9 % (104.9 -> 114.2 s), last +7.8 % (15.1 -> 16.3 s),
  wall +1.4 %. Without herd the same 1.2 s shift is +3.6 % of the 399 s sum.
* hsjf's mechanism holds: the short prompt is an `Active` from t = 2 s (a slot was free) and ranks first; its TTFT is 2.3 s = the in-flight 2048-row chunk (about 0.8 s) + its own prefill + first token. It does not wait for the herd leader.
* The short prompt also finishes earlier in absolute time (last token at 18.1 s with both on, 21.3 s with herd alone), while the whole run still ends at 21.6 s vs 21.3 s (the herd group is what ends it).

## 5. Cell 3: `INTARSIA_PREFILL_AGE` 4 / 8 / 16 (flipev's Q4 setup)

`serve --max-seq 49152 --max-batch 4`, `INTARSIA_CB_NO_REUSE=1`, `INTARSIA_PREFILL_ORDER=shortest INTARSIA_PREFILL_AGE=<n>`; arms F (fifo, the same-session reference), 4, 8, 16 in the visit order F 4 8 16 16 8 4 F, 3 reps per visit: **6 runs per arm**. 2 decoders
(100-token prompts, **800** tokens each, flipev: 1000) at t = 0, a 40K-token prompt at +2 s, two 1000-token shorts at long +1.5 s and +4 s. Window = long submit to long first token. No run was void (every decoder was still streaming at the long prompt's first token).

| arm | runs | long TTFT s | short TTFT s (s1 / s2) | decode tokens in window (2 decoders) | decode tok/s/stream | window gaps mean ms | p99 ms | max ms | gaps > 200 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fifo | 6 | 33.97 (33.91 to 34.09) | 33.25 / 32.16 | 206 | 3.0 | 326.6 | 793.8 | 813.3 | 701 |
| sjf age 4 | 6 | 35.48 (35.42 to 35.58) | 2.03 / 7.61 | 185 | 2.6 | 381.1 | 808.7 | 817.2 | 752 |
| sjf age 8 (default) | 6 | 35.44 (35.36 to 35.51) | 2.03 / 7.62 | 183 | 2.6 | 383.8 | 808.5 | 813.6 | 749 |
| sjf age 16 | 6 | 35.47 (35.39 to 35.57) | 2.01 / 7.60 | 183 | 2.6 | 385.0 | 806.5 | 816.7 | 752 |

* **AGE 4, 8 and 16 are indistinguishable** in this cell: every metric agrees within run-to-run spread (long TTFT sd 0.06 to 0.08 s, decode tokens sd 3 to 3.5). The aging bound (a prompt passed over `age` decisions in a row goes first) only matters when more than `age` shorter prompts are ahead of the long one;
  here at most one short is ahead at a time. So this cell cannot show a sensitivity; section 5.2 (cell 3b) gives the long prompt twelve shorts ahead of it.
* sjf vs fifo repeats flipev's finding at larger scale: long TTFT +1.5 s (+4.3 %), decode tokens in the window -10 to -11 % (206 -> 183 to 185, because the shorts' own prefill and decode run inside the window), p99 gap 794 -> 809 ms and max 813 -> 817 ms (the tail is one 2048-row chunk, about 0.8 s, either way).
  Shorts: s1 33.25 -> 2.03 s.
* s2's 7.6 s is **not** a scheduling delay: at `--max-batch 4` the slots are 2 decoders + long + s1, so s2 waits for s1 (16 tokens at about 0.5 s per step during the window) to free a slot. It is the same in all three sjf arms; in flipev's faster window s1 was done in time (0.5 s).
* Baseline decode (before the long prompt) is the same in every arm: mean gap 22.6 to 22.7 ms, p99 69 ms.

### 5.2 Cell 3b: AGE with a burst of 12 shorts (added: cell 3 cannot bind the aging bound)

`serve --max-seq 49152 --max-batch 16` (so all 15 requests have a slot), arms F (fifo), 2, 8 (default), 32 in the visit order F 2 8 32 32 8 2 F, 2 reps per visit: **4 runs per arm**. 2 decoders (800 tokens), the 40K prompt at +2 s, **twelve 1000-token shorts released together at long +1.5 s**.
With packed prefill on (CUDA, store off) the shorts run two to a pass (their TTFTs come in equal pairs), so the burst is about 6 decisions; an `age` below that lets the long prompt take a chunk in between.

| arm | runs | long TTFT s | shorts TTFT mean / max s (12 shorts) | decode tokens in window | tok/s/stream | window gaps mean ms | p99 ms | max ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fifo | 4 | 33.98 (33.88 to 34.08) | 36.67 / 39.14 | 206 | 3.0 | 330 | 793 | 816 |
| sjf age 2 | 4 | 42.19 (42.10 to 42.26) | 6.16 / 9.35 | 199 | 2.4 | 422 | 1773 | 1793 |
| sjf age 8 (default) | 4 | 42.14 (42.05 to 42.19) | 5.41 / 7.86 | 202 | 2.4 | 417 | 1779 | 1789 |
| sjf age 32 | 4 | 42.20 (42.04 to 42.36) | 5.42 / 7.88 | 202 | 2.4 | 418 | 1775 | 1802 |

* **AGE binds only below the burst's decision count.** Age 8 and 32 are identical in every column (the long prompt is never passed over 8 decisions in a row by 6 passes); age 2 lets the long prompt run a chunk after every second decision, which moves the shorts' tail
  later (mean 5.41 -> 6.16 s, max 7.86 -> 9.35 s, about 1.5 s) and buys the long prompt nothing measurable (42.14 -> 42.19 s, +0.05 s): the total work is the same, aging only reorders it. In this cell a small age costs the short tail and gives nothing back; the default 8 and anything above it are equivalent.
  Age 4 would sit in between and was not run.
* **sjf vs fifo with a burst is a bigger trade than with two shorts:** the 40K prompt's first token comes 8.2 s (24 %) later (42.14 vs 33.98 s) because 12K tokens of shorts run first; the shorts get a first token in 3.0 to 7.9 s instead of 34 to 39 s;
  the decoders' tokens in the window are -2 % (202 vs 206) and their per-stream rate 2.4 vs 3.0 tok/s; **the decoders' worst gap doubles, 1.79 s vs 0.82 s** (p99 1.78 vs 0.79 s). Caveat on that last number: the window ends at the long prompt's first token, and under fifo the shorts' prefill runs after it, outside the
  window, so fifo's stall for the burst is not in this table; the cell shows that sjf concentrates the burst's cost into the window, not that fifo is free of it. The 1.8 s gap is two prefill decisions between decode steps; not diagnosed further.
* ids: 120 / 120 contended replies equal their own server's solo reply (30 per arm); each prompt has one id sequence over all runs and arms.

## 6. Cell 4: is the unrelated request really slower with herd?

Two measurements, both `--max-batch 16 --max-seq 24576 --prefix-store on` (flipev: `--max-batch 8`; 6 requests never bind slots in either).

**4a. Thread-order arrival (`hs` phase, flipev's method): not informative.** 4 requests on a 16448-token prefix + 2 unrelated 8192-token prompts, released from a barrier, 4 seeds x 2 visits = 8 cells per arm. The server's FIFO order is whatever order the client threads reach it, which changes cell to cell:

| arm | cells | U1 s | U2 s | pair sum s | slower of two s | faster of two s | shared four mean s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| herd off | 8 | 23.64 +- 21.77 | 24.02 +- 15.96 | 47.66 +- 21.92 | 36.74 +- 15.03 | 10.92 +- 11.04 | 37.68 +- 3.44 |
| herd on | 8 | 13.31 +- 6.76 | 16.74 +- 9.78 | 30.05 +- 10.72 | 20.44 +- 6.30 | 9.60 +- 6.43 | 20.90 +- 4.11 |

The same prompts take turns: seed 131 gives U1 / U2 = 4.96 / 10.30 s in the off visit and 10.30 / 4.95 s in the on visit (same two numbers, swapped), i.e. the 4.95 s one was simply first in the queue. The sd of one unrelated request's TTFT (7 to 22 s) is 20 to 60 times the 0.35 s flipev reported, so that
number cannot be told from order noise. Full per-cell table in `raw/tables.md`.

**4b. Fixed arrival order (`mixd` phase): 20 ms between releases, visits N H H N, 3 seeds per visit and order, so 6 cells per arm and order.** Order A is flipev's (S0 U1 S2 S3 U4 S5), order B puts the two unrelated prompts first (U1 U4 S0 S2 S3 S5). TTFT mean, off -> on, sd in parentheses:

| request | order A: off s | on s | on - off | order B: off s | on s | on - off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 (leader) | 11.13 (0.02) | 11.13 (0.02) | -0.00 | 23.69 (0.28) | 23.99 (0.20) | **+0.30** |
| U1 | 16.79 (0.05) | 16.82 (0.09) | **+0.03** | 4.95 (0.01) | 4.95 (0.01) | +0.00 |
| S2 | 28.65 | 17.34 | -11.31 | 35.87 | 24.54 | -11.33 |
| S3 | 39.55 | 17.87 | -21.68 | 47.98 | 25.10 | -22.88 |
| U4 | 45.33 (0.19) | 23.73 (0.08) | **-21.60** | 10.27 (0.02) | 10.28 (0.02) | +0.01 |

| U pair sum | 62.12 | 40.54 | -21.57 | 15.22 | 15.23 | +0.01 |

* **Not real as a cost on unrelated traffic.** The unrelated prompts are unchanged to +0.00 to +0.03 s in every position where herd cannot reorder them (U1 in A; both in B), and the visits agree (per-visit U pair sum 62.18 / 62.06 s off, 40.66 / 40.43 s on in A; 15.21 / 15.23 off, 15.23 / 15.23 on in B). In A the second unrelated
  prompt improves by 21.6 s because the followers it queued behind are now restored instead of prefilled.
* The unrelated request is never slower than the off arm by more than 0.09 s (U1 in A, sd 0.05 / 0.09, within noise). flipev's 0.35 s was thread order.
* **One small real-looking effect:** in order B the herd leader (arrives third, behind the two unrelated prompts) is 0.30 s (1.3 %) later with herd on (23.69 -> 23.99 s; sd 0.20 to 0.28, n = 6 per arm, about 2 sigma of the difference of means, the per-visit means agree: 23.68 / 23.70 s off, 23.95 / 24.03 s on).
  Order A's leader is +0.00. Not chased further; it is a 1 % effect on one request in one order.

## 9. The spark2 GPU did not boost (why every absolute number is 3.6x slow)

* Under load spark2 showed **96 % GPU utilisation at 12 W and a 442 to 448 MHz SM clock, 44 C**; spark1, running another lane's job at the same moment: 56 W, 2444 MHz, 67 C (`raw/nvidia-smi-under-load.txt`; spark2's idle clock also reads 442 MHz, spark1's 2398 MHz).
  Max clock 3003 MHz, application clock 2418 MHz on both; no throttle reason active (`SW Power Cap` counter about 2 s). nvidia-smi lists no process on the GPU other than vLLM (idle, 0 requests) and our server.
* Effect: a cold 16448-token prompt takes 10.5 to 11.1 s in every config of the `diag` phase (3 prompts released together, FIFO prefill, TTFT steps between prompts; `raw/diag.jsonl`):

| config (`--max-seq 24576`) | first prompt s | next prompts (step s) |
|---|---|---|
| `--max-batch 16 --prefix-store on` | 10.94 / 10.88 | 12.4 to 12.9, 12.6 to 13.2 |
| `--max-batch 8 --prefix-store on` | 10.94 / 10.89 | 12.5 to 12.8, 12.9 to 13.5 |
| `--max-batch 16` (store off) | 10.58 / 10.53 | 11.8 to 11.9 |
| single-sequence `serve` (no `--max-batch`) | 9.91 / 9.83 | 10.7 to 11.1 |

  so it is the box, not `--max-batch` or the store (the store costs +3 % on the first prompt and +4 to 11 % on later ones here; at `--max-batch 16` vs single-sequence +7 % first, +8 to 10 % later). flipev's spark1 and the earlier herd lane's spark2 run needed about 3 s for the same prompt
  (16K, 8 requests off: last TTFT 24 s there, 88 s here). The prefill chunk call is 0.84 s per 2048-row chunk in every visit here (flipev spark1: 0.23 s).
* Not diagnosed and not touched (changing GPU clocks is a system setting, outside this lane): worth a look by whoever owns spark2 (`nvidia-smi -q -d CLOCK,PERFORMANCE`, a power cycle, the clock policy). Until then results from this box need the factor of about 3.6 on prefill-bound times.
* What this does and does not threaten: all arms of a cell ran on the same box, binary and clocks, interleaved ABBA, and the arms agree to 0.1 to 1 % between visits, so the ratios are well measured. What can change on a healthy GPU is the *balance*: GPU kernel time shrank 3.6x on spark1 while CPU
  and scheduler overhead did not, so effects that depend on the chunk time (the decode-token deficit in the window, the 1.2 s leader delay in cell 2, the 1.8 s decode gap in cell 3b) will be smaller in seconds there. flipev's spark1 numbers for the same shapes agree in sign (sjf: long TTFT +0.46 s / +5 %,
  window decode rate -17 %, tail gaps unchanged).

## 10. Flip recommendations (the decision is the user's)

**herd (`INTARSIA_PREFIX_HERD=1`): the evidence now supports it for `serve --prefix-store on`, at `--max-batch 16` as well as 8, alone and together with sjf.**
* For: 16 requests on a shared 16K prefix, TTFT sum -83 %, last -89 %, wall -84 % (cell 1); never worse for any follower or the leader; with sjf also on, the herd group finishes 8 to 9 % later than herd alone but the short prompt gets 2.3 s (cell 2); unrelated prompts unchanged to
  0.03 s in two fixed arrival orders (flipev's 0.35 s was thread order); 1008 / 1008 replies equal.
* Against / unknown: herd does nothing without `--prefix-store on`, so the real default question is the store (flip herd and leave the store off = no effect). The store costs 12 to 14 % on a burst of unrelated 8K prompts (flipev) and +3 to 11 % per cold 16K prompt here (section 9), and its greedy
  ids differ from store-off for most prompts whose last chunk differs (storeids: documented, not a bug). One small residual: in a burst where two unrelated prompts arrive first, the herd leader is +0.30 s (1.3 %). Not measured: prefixes just under the store grid or under `min_tokens`, `--max-batch` above 16, non-CUDA backends,
  real chat prompts with a shared system prompt.
* Revert: unset the variable (or one-line default).

**sjf (`INTARSIA_PREFILL_ORDER=shortest`, `INTARSIA_PREFILL_AGE`): still a policy choice, now with the trade-off sized in four shapes.**
* What it buys: a short prompt that arrives while long prompts prefill gets its first token in 1.6 to 2.3 s (cells 2 and 5, cell 3's first short) or 3.0 to 7.9 s (12 at once, cell 3b) instead of 14 to 87 s (cell 2), 31 to 33 s (cells 3, 5) or 34 to 39 s (3b).
* What it costs: every request that is overtaken waits for the shorts' prefill, so the overtaken prompt's first token comes later by the shorts' work: +1.2 s on every herd member (cell 2, +9 % of the group sum, with herd on), +1.5 to +1.7 s on a 40K prompt (+4.4 to 5 %; cells 3, 5), +8.2 s (+24 %) when 12 shorts overtake it (3b);
  the ongoing decodes lose 10 to 11 % of their window tokens at 2 decoders / `--max-batch 4`, none at 3 to 4 decoders / `--max-batch 8`, and see their worst gap double (0.8 -> 1.8 s) when a burst of 12 shorts goes first (window-limited, see 5.2). With nothing to overtake it is free (cell 5 long-only rows equal to 0.1 s).
  With 2 to 4 decoders and one or two shorts the decoders' p99 and max gaps do not change.
* `INTARSIA_PREFILL_AGE`: 4, 8 and 16 are indistinguishable with two shorts (cell 3); with a 12-short burst 8 and 32 are identical and 2 only delays the shorts' tail by 1.5 s for no gain to the long prompt (3b). Nothing here argues for changing the default 8.
* Flip if short-prompt latency matters more than long-prompt latency for the default workload; with herd on, a short prompt's slot is the other condition (`--max-batch` above the herd size, hsjf). Revert: unset the variable.

## 11. Not verified

* Random-id prompts only (flat logits); no chat prompts; one model (35B NVFP4), one GPU, one chunk size.
* Cell 5: 4 decoders + 2 shorts fifo has 3 runs (one lost to a 5-minute ssh/fleetq timeout); decoders generate 800 tokens (flipev: 1000); the first fleetq client of the `ageburst` job died on the same fleetq outage and was resubmitted (`raw/fleetq-ageburst-first-attempt.out`).
