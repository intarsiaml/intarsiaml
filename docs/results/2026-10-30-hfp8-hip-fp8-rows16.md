# HFP8: HIP fp8 dense projections at 9-16 rows in one pass (R9700, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4)

Lane hfp8, 2026-10-30. Branch `lane-hfp8/rows16`, rebased on main `05451fe66` (HGV merged). Target: bottleneck #1 of the HPROF profile
(`2026-10-30-hprof-r9700-16stream-profile.md`, sections 5 and 8): the batched dense fp8 projections (`matmul_fp8_gemv_rows`).

## Summary

* **16 streams, aggregate decode: TP=1 400.3 -> 465.3 tok/s (+16.2 %), TP=2 585.2 -> 652.2 tok/s (+11.4 %)** (medians of 3 interleaved
  rounds, `--max-batch 16`, 1k prompts, gen 512, spec off). 8 streams +11.3 % / +5.9 %, 4 streams +3.9 % / +3.3 %, one stream unchanged
  (164.5 -> 164.4 tok/s TP=1, 192.7 -> 192.7 TP=2). The step at 16 rows goes from 40.0 to 34.4 ms (TP=1) and 27.3 to 24.5 ms (TP=2).
* **Bitwise identical to main.** Every row of every M = 1..16 equals the one-row kernel's output (416 shape/M/dtype checks x 3 tile settings
  x W8A8 and W8A16 in a standalone harness, the repo's `rows_exact` and the other fp8 row tests), 16 concurrent greedy streams give
  byte-identical text on main and on this branch at TP=1 and TP=2, and the real-35B `real_35b_batched_decode_is_bitwise_the_solo_step`
  (N = 2..16, 130 steps, f32 logits) passes at TP=1 and TP=2. No reduction order changed, so no KL gate is needed.
* **The profile's diagnosis was incomplete.** The dense fp8 kernel is not bound by streaming the weights two or three times: main's time
  per launch is linear in M (about 7 us per row at N=8192), so merging the groups alone (one pass, same loop structure) gains 6 %
  (section 2). The cost is per-row work around the weights: the activation staging every block repeats, LDS bank conflicts on the
  staged activations, the warp-sum epilogue (5 shuffles per accumulator) and low occupancy. Fixing those, not the pass count, gives the gain.
* The new kernel has no scratch (VGPR 214 at M = 16; main's `fp8_gemv_kernel<12>` spills 276-284 B/lane).

## 1. What changed

`kernels/hip/fp8.hip` only (plus bench/driver scripts in `scripts/hfp8/`): a new kernel `fp8_gemv_rows_kernel<M, R=2, OutT>` for
M = 1..16. `launch_rows` sends M >= 3 (K <= 2048) and M >= 5 (longer K) to it; M = 1, 2 (and 3, 4 at K > 2048) keep their old kernels
(`INTARSIA_HIP_FP8_ONEPASS_MIN=N` overrides the threshold, `=99` is the old path). The arithmetic per output element is unchanged: chunk
`u = lane + 32 i`, 8 `v_dot2_f32_f16` (W8A8) or 16 `fmaf` (W8A16) per chunk, one chain per (weight row, activation row), the warp sum
over the lanes. What is different:

1. **Loop order.** The m and r loops are swapped: one activation row's staged chunk is read from LDS, used for both weight rows, dropped.
   Main holds all `xv[M][2]` (M x 32 bytes) at once: 245 VGPR at M = 8, 256 and scratch from M = 10. Each `acc[r][m]` still sees its
   chunks in the same order, so its bits are the same.
2. **Staged activations in segments of 2048 values** (M x 4 KiB, 64 KiB at M = 16, needs no attribute on gfx12). A segment ends on a
   multiple of 32 chunks, so each lane continues its own chain across segments; one launch serves any K (main split M = 16 at K = 4096
   into three groups of at most 6 rows).
3. **LDS layout.** A staged 16-value chunk is stored as two 16-byte halves `kSegU` apart, so a wave reads 32 consecutive 16-byte words
   per `ds_read_b128` instead of a 32-byte stride (2-way bank conflict).
4. **Scattered warp sum.** The R x M lane sums go through the very tree of `ik_warp_sum` (xor 16, 8, 4, 2, 1; each level adds two lanes'
   values), but at each level a lane hands the half of its values its partner finishes and keeps the other half, so one value per lane
   is left after the last level: about R x M shuffles instead of 5 R x M. Every value sees the same pairwise adds (`a + b == b + a`), so
   the bits are those of `ik_warp_sum`.
5. **Tiles per block.** When K <= 2048 (one segment) a block works through `tiles = clamp(ntile / 64, 1, 8)` consecutive tiles of 16
   weight rows, staging the activations once and loading the next tile's weights while it computes the current one. 64 blocks is what
   the 64 KiB of staged activations let a 32-WGP R9700 keep resident. (`INTARSIA_HIP_FP8_TILES` overrides.)

## 2. Where the time went (microbench, DRAM-cold weights)

`scripts/hfp8/bench_fp8.hip` includes `fp8.hip` and times `ik_matmul_fp8_gemv_rows` with the weights rotated over >= 400 MB of copies
(defeats the 64 MB infinity cache), best of 3-5 reps, tatooine card 0. Raw: `raw/mb*/`.

First version (one pass, loops swapped, no other change; `raw/mb1`, N=8192 K=2048): M=16 129 -> 121 us, M=8 72 -> 63 us. So the second
pass over the weights was not what cost time. Ablating that first version (`raw/mb2`, N=8192 K=2048, us):

## 3. Correctness

* **Standalone bitwise check** (`scripts/hfp8/bench_fp8.hip`, `raw/mb5-aq1`, `raw/mb5-aq0`): for M = 1..16, row m of the M-row call equals
  `ik_matmul_fp8_gemv_v2` on row m alone, bit for bit, bf16 and f32 out, on 13 shapes (8192x2048, 4096x2048, 2048x4096, 512x2048,
  2048x2048, 256x2048, 300x2048, 33x2064 (ragged chunk tail, two segments), 9x16, 8x1040, 64x6144, 16x16400, 1x4096); W8A8 (actq=1) and W8A16
  (actq=0), default tiles, tiles=3 (ragged last block) and tiles=8: 6 x 416 = 2496 checks, 0 mismatches. (A first version had the W8A16
  path reading the wrong LDS half; the actq=0 run is what pins that down.)
* **Repo tests** (the registered kernel `matmul_fp8_gemv_rows` goes through `launch_rows`): `rows_exact` 13 passed (its
  `fp8_rows_are_bitwise_the_one_row_gemv` covers every M = 1..16, 6 scale modes, the shapes above plus the tp2 shard shapes, rows in
  reversed order; it already had M = 13..16, so no case was added), `fp8_f8` 5, `rows_registry` 2, `frag_layout` 5, `quick_coverage` 1,
  all ok on the rebased tree (`raw/w-tests3`); the same on the pre-rebase tree (`raw/w-tests1`).
* **Real 35B, batched == solo bit for bit** (`batch_cuda_real real_35b_batched_decode_is_bitwise_the_solo_step`: N = 2..16, 130
  consecutive steps each, f32 logits and argmax): TP=1 `raw/w-tests3`, TP=2 `raw/w-tests2` (both on the rebased tree), passed.
* **Served greedy identity** (`scripts/hfp8/greedy.py`, `raw/w-greedy2`): 16 concurrent different ~1.1k-token prompts, temperature 0,
  192 tokens each (3072 tokens): main `05451fe66` and the branch produce byte-identical texts at TP=1 and TP=2 (`cmp` of the two
  JSONL files). There is no main-against-main control run: the two arms are identical, so there was no mismatch to attribute.
* KL / top-1 gate: not run, because no reduction order changed (items 1-5 reorder work between threads, not the adds of any output
  element) and the above is a bitwise comparison with main.

## 5. What is left

* K = 4096 shapes (GDN `out_proj`, `o_proj`, 40 calls at TP=1) still run in two segments with the activations staged in every block:
  54 us at M = 16 against 17 us at M = 1. Staging the activations as e4m3 bytes (they are exactly e4m3) would halve the LDS and let the
  tile loop serve K = 4096 too, at the price of a convert per use.
* At small N (512, 256) the launch is latency bound (15-19 us at M = 16); fusing the k/v pair or the GDN qkv/z pairs into one launch
  would be the next step there.
* M = 16 at N = 8192 still costs 1.6 x the one-row call; the remaining part is the dot2 work (16 rows x 8 `v_dot2` per weight chunk).
