## Headline

**Small round. The final defaults add +0.4 to +0.6 % 35B decode at 128 to 4096 tokens. 35B prefill and the 8B are flat
(W9, interleaved x3 vs base `ab887732`). Every default is bitwise identical to base.**

* **Where the decode gain comes from:** page-locked staging of small uploads and downloads (lever 1). It takes the host
  waits out of the per-step token-id upload and argmax download. It is HIP-only.
* **Round 8's "3.4 ms per chunk boundary" was mislabelled.** It is a HIP upload sync plus the decode-graph capture that

* **The ~275 us stall is real without a profiler,** as about 256 us after each dispatch of 1 ms or more. Round 8's split
  is a real gain. The new split trigger (lever 2) only stops cutting launches that would not stall. It measured equal to
  round 8's rule at 1024, 2048 and 4096-token chunks.
* **Split-KV stage 1 at 8 waves per SIMD (lever 3) is opt-in** (`INTARSIA_HIP_SPLITKV_OCC=1`):
  * +1.5 % decode at ~17.6k tokens;
  * -0.6 % at 128 to 2048 tokens, from a 28 B/lane register spill (W8);
  * user decision below.

## The ~275 us stall without a profiler

**About 256 us per dispatch of 1 ms or more, unprofiled, in the engine.** rocprofv3 moves the stall but does not create it:
unprofiled it opens one dispatch later than the profiler shows it. That is why a standalone reproducer that only times the
gap right after a long kernel sees ~16 us.

### Method (W1, stage `pfstamp`; `raw/stamps/`, `raw/prof/stall-*.txt`)

* `INTARSIA_HIP_STAMP=<file>` (new, diagnostics only, off by default) launches a 1-thread kernel that stores the GPU's
  constant-rate wall clock (`wall_clock64`, 100 MHz) before and after every long prefill dispatch: MoE gate_up and down (and
  each piece), the GDN delta rule (and each piece), and flash attention. It uses no events, no completion signals and no
  profiler. The stamps are dumped when the backend is dropped.
* The same 4408-token prefill ran unprofiled and under `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace`, with the split on (default) and off
  (`INTARSIA_HIP_SPLIT_US=0`). The profiled trace gives each bracketed dispatch's duration and shows the stamp kernels.
* `stampgap.py` pairs every bracket with its dispatch. `stampseg.py` sums the unstamped stretches between ops.
* Stamps do not move the total: stamped prefill 513 / 519 ms, unstamped on the same build 515 / 511 / 519 / 514 ms.

### The cleanest case: gate_up<64> -> down

gate_up<64> is one 2.1 to 2.2 ms dispatch in every run and is never split. Its end stamp is followed directly by down's
begin stamp, so nothing runs between those two 1-thread kernels (`stall-delayed-gateup-to-down.txt`). Per layer, in us:

| run | gate_up end stamp -> down begin stamp (40 layers) |
|---|---|
| unprofiled, split on | 3 on layer 0; 250 to 275 (median 260) on the other 39 |
| unprofiled, split off | 5 on layer 0; 250 to 270 on the other 39 |
| rocprofv3, split on | 4 to 5 on all 40 |
| rocprofv3, split off | 4 to 6 on all 40 |

* **Under rocprofv3**, the ~277 us sits between the long dispatch and the next one: the bracket around gate_up grows from
  ~2.19 to ~2.45 ms. This is round 8's picture, and the reproducer's.
* **Without the profiler**, the dispatch right after the long one starts promptly. The dead time inside the brackets of
  >= 1 ms dispatches is a median of 6 to 35 us (`stall-stampgap-split-on.txt`). The ~256 us gap opens **before the
  second dispatch after the long one**.
* Layer 0's gate_up has no gap in any of the four runs. With the split off, an unsplit 3.1 ms GDN launch runs just before
  it in the same layer. I did not find out why.
* Every other long dispatch shows the same. With the split off, unprofiled, the stretch after each unsplit GDN delta rule
  (3.1 ms) and each MoE down (1.1 ms) grows by 236 to 256 us (`stall-stampseg-unprofiled.txt`):
  * +7.7 ms over 30 GDN launches;
  * +7.1 + 2.3 ms over 40 down launches.
* So the engine loses this time with or without the profiler, and round 8's split gain is real. Unprofiled, split on vs off:
  513 vs 526 ms prefill; stamped span 495.1 vs 506.7 ms.
* At 1024-token chunks (W2, `stall-stampgap-chunk1024.txt`), 584 of 600 bracketed dispatches stay under 1 ms (GDN ~0.77 ms,
  gate_up<64> ~0.88 ms), and under rocprofv3 none of them shows the gap. The 16 that reach 1 ms (12 flash launches in the
  later chunks, 3 gate_up, 1 GDN) show the ~277 us under rocprofv3.

**For the ROCm issue:** a reproducer needs three dispatches (long, short, short), timing the gap before the third. Timing
only the gap after the long kernel reads ~16 us. The cause (CP, firmware or driver) is still open.

**What is left of it in the default prefill:** gate_up<64> is the only dispatch of >= 1 ms that is still not split, plus
flash attention at 4096 tokens (3.0 ms, 10 launches). The stamped runs put the gate_up -> down stretch at 10.1 ms per
4408-token prompt (39 x ~256 us, about 2 %). That figure has one caveat: in the stamped runs the dispatch after gate_up is
a 1.4 us stamp kernel. In the unstamped engine it is down's first piece (~550 us), and I did not measure whether the gap
still falls before down's second piece. Round 8 split gate_up and dropped it (the pieces' load-imbalance tails cost 28 ms
against ~11 ms of gaps); I did not retry it.

## Round 8's "3.4 ms per chunk boundary" was mislabelled

Round 8 listed about 3.4 ms of host time at every prefill chunk boundary (3 per 16k prompt), in the shared core prefill loop.
The traces say otherwise (`embgaps.py` on round 8's 4408-token and ~17.6k traces, `raw/prof/chunk-gaps-r8traces.txt`):

* **A chunk boundary costs ~0.5 ms.** That is the recurrent-state snapshot (31 `copyBuffer`, ~345 us of device-to-device
  copies) plus 105 to 190 us of idle GPU before the next chunk's embedding. At 17.6k the three boundaries cost 450 to 480 us
  each.
* **The 3.5 ms gap comes once per prompt, before the last prompt token.** A recurrent model prefills all but the last token,
  snapshots, and then runs the last token on its own (`engine.rs` `prefill_step`). That step goes through the decode graph,
  and `generate` captures the graph right there: the cache is per `SequenceState::id`, which is new per request in
  `generate`. Round 8's API trace shows the token-id upload, its `hipStreamSynchronize`, then `hipStreamBeginCapture` ...
  `hipStreamEndCapture` inside the gap.
* **The serve path does not pay the capture per request.** `LocalEngine` keeps one state for the process, so the graph is
  captured once: every row of every serve bench JSON this round has `"captures": 0`.
* **Why the GPU idles at both:**
  * every `Backend::upload` was a pageable `hipMemcpyAsync` followed by `hipStreamSynchronize` (`ik_rt_memcpy_h2d`);
  * the chunk's token-id upload, and the id / position uploads before the last token's step, therefore drain the queue;
  * the GPU then waits for the host to wake up and launch again, and in `generate` also to capture.

So the lever is HIP-only: it changes how the HIP backend uploads, and the shared prefill loop is untouched (lever 1 below).

### 1. Page-locked staging of small copies (`device.rs` `Staging`, `INTARSIA_HIP_STAGE`; HIP backend only)

*Headroom estimate before building:*
* `generate` / a fresh sequence: ~3.5 ms per prompt (the capture then runs while the GPU still works on the last chunk),
  plus ~0.1 to 0.2 ms per chunk boundary.
* Serve: ~0.1 to 0.2 ms per chunk boundary and before the last token. TTFT +0.2 % at 128 tokens, less above.
* Decode: the step boundary is ~90 to 115 us of idle GPU (argmax end -> next embedding, three runtime copies in between).
  Two of its three host waits go, so ~30 to 60 us per step, +0.5 to +0.9 % decode.

*What it does:*
* An upload of at most 64 KiB is copied into the next of 8 page-locked 64 KiB slots and queued from there with
  `hipMemcpyAsync`, without a wait.
  * An event recorded right after the copy guards the slot. The slot is rewritten only after its event has completed.
  * In practice that is long before reuse: the ring holds 8 copies, and every decode step and every prefill ends in a
    download.
* A download of at most 64 KiB goes through one more page-locked slot. It waits for the stream either way.
* These keep the old blocking path:
  * larger copies (weights, `zero`'s chunks);
  * copies during a graph capture;
  * `INTARSIA_HIP_TRACE` runs;
  * everything under `INTARSIA_HIP_STAGE=0` (read at every copy). `up` stages uploads only.
* The device gets the same bytes in the same stream order, so the bits are identical by construction. The trait already
  allows this: `Backend::upload` "May be asynchronous on `stream`".

*Where the time came from.* The cost was the explicit `hipStreamSynchronize` after each upload, not pageable memory as
such. Mutant `r9-st-pageable` shows it: it queues the copy straight from the caller's pageable bytes without a wait, and it
survives. On ROCm 7.2 the runtime copies a small pageable source before `hipMemcpyAsync` returns. HIP does not promise that,
which is why `ik_rt_memcpy_h2d` synchronised (its comment says so) and why the ring exists: with it, the no-wait upload
relies only on the documented contract for page-locked memory.

*Measured* (per-lever table above): decode +0.6 % at 128 / 1024 / 4096 in serve; `generate` -5.5 ms per 4408-token prompt.

### 2. Split trigger at the stall line (`ik_split_pieces`, `INTARSIA_HIP_SPLIT_MIN_US`, default 950 us)

*Headroom estimate before building:* about zero.
* Round 8's W8 already measured split vs no split at 1024 tokens as noise.
* The trigger only changes launches estimated at 600 to 950 us: GDN at ~780 to ~1230 tokens, MoE down<64> at ~2100 to
  ~3400 tokens.
* Those launches never stall, so their pieces were pure overhead, ~3 % of those kernels' time. That is at most ~1 ms per
  1024-token chunk.

*What it does:*
* `ik_split_pieces` keeps one launch unless the estimate exceeds both the piece budget (`INTARSIA_HIP_SPLIT_US`, 600) and
  the trigger (950).
* Above the trigger, pieces are still sized by the budget. Every size in round 8's tables gets the same pieces (4096-token
  GDN: 6, down<64>: 2).
* `INTARSIA_HIP_SPLIT_MIN_US=0` restores round 8's rule.
* Pieces are bitwise either way. Round 8's tests (`gdn_v2`, `moe_pairs`) now force pieces with the trigger at 0.
* `ik_hip_split_pieces` host seam, pinned by `tests/split_trigger.rs`.

*Measured* at the three chunk sizes asked for (4408-token `generate`, trigger vs `INTARSIA_HIP_SPLIT_MIN_US=0`,
interleaved x2):
* 1024 (W2): -0.75 ms;
* 2048 (W9): -0.5 ms;
* 4096 (W9): equal.

All of them are within noise. It is a cleanup with no measurable speed effect: launches that would not stall are no longer
cut. Round 8's estimate of the overhead (pieces of sub-1 ms launches, ~3 % of those kernels' time) was too high to show
here. I kept it because it removes pieces that buy nothing, and its kill switch restores round 8's rule.

### 3. Split-KV attention stage 1 at 8 waves per SIMD (`splitkv_kernel_o8`, `INTARSIA_HIP_SPLITKV_OCC=1`, opt-in)

*Finding* (CPU-slot ISA resource usage, `isa.sh`): `splitkv_kernel<8, 32, 2>` (the 35B's GQA group of 8) takes 212 VGPRs,
so at most 7 waves per SIMD.
* A long-context decode step launches 128 blocks of 8 waves per layer: 64 splits x 2 kv heads. From 4096 keys on, the
  split count is at its 64 cap (and the decode graph always launches the full grid; surplus blocks exit at once).
* At 7 waves per SIMD, 112 of the 128 blocks are resident and 16 run as a second round.
* At <= 192 VGPRs, 8 waves fit, and all 128 do.

*Headroom estimate before building:* stage 1 is 903 us per step at ~17.6k (13.9 % of decode, 64 % of bandwidth). Removing
the second round could bring a launch from ~90 us toward ~65 us, about 250 us per step (+3 % decode at 17.6k). The 128-token
decode would be unchanged, because there only a few splits do work. (That last part was wrong, see W8 below.)

*What it does:*
* The kernel body is now `splitkv_body`. `splitkv_kernel` (the old kernel) and `splitkv_kernel_o8` both call it.
* `splitkv_kernel_o8` adds `__attribute__((amdgpu_waves_per_eu(8)))`: 192 VGPRs, 28 bytes per lane of scratch spill,
  8 waves per SIMD.
* The instructions and their order are the same, only register-allocated differently, so the bits are the same.
* `INTARSIA_HIP_SPLITKV_OCC=1` selects the 8-wave kernel (read at every launch); unset or anything else runs base's
  7-wave kernel. `ik_hip_splitkv_occ` host seam. (`62ab34fd` made it the default; `5bf91f0f` made it opt-in after W8.)
* `tests/splitkv_occ.rs` checks it bitwise against the 7-wave kernel over:
  * decode and the short-prefill path, groups 1 to 8, head dims 32 to 256;
  * key counts from 1 to past the 64-split cap, peaky and flat softmax;
  * and it pins the selection (off by default, `1` on). The test sets `OCC=1` explicitly for the 8-wave side.

*Measured* (W5, interleaved x2): 89.8 / 90.1 -> 78.6 / 79.1 us per launch (-12 %), 898 / 901 -> 786 / 791 us per step.
Decode after ~17.6k tokens goes 145.7 -> 148.1 / 147.9 tok/s, unprofiled. That is less than the estimate: the kernel is at
~73 % of bandwidth now, not at the floor.

*Measured at short context* (W8, interleaved x2, `raw/logs/w8.log`), which is why it is opt-in:
* 160-token decode after a 24-token prompt (rocprofv3): stage 1 is **16.1 us per launch with OCC=1 vs 12.4 us** with the
  7-wave kernel (160.6 vs 124.1 us per step; step kernel time 5.657 vs 5.624 ms). The loss is inside the kernel: graph gaps
  are the same (0.839 vs 0.838 ms per step). Likely the 28 bytes per lane of spill: at short context few splits run, so
  occupancy buys nothing and the spill's scratch round trips add latency.
* 35B serve decode at 128 / 1024 / 2048: 164.3, 164.4 / 163.8, 164.2 / 163.1, 163.4 tok/s with OCC=1 vs 165.4, 165.4 /
  165.1, 165.1 / 164.2, 164.1 without: **-0.6 % at every size**, no range overlap. OCC=1 ran first in each pair, so drift
  would have favoured it. Prefill is unchanged (it does not use stage 1 at these sizes).
* The 8B (GQA group 4, also through `splitkv_kernel_o8` when on) showed no difference vs base in W8's A/B with OCC=1 on;
  its decode is weight-bandwidth-bound at ~38 tok/s, so 3 % of a 0.5 % kernel does not show.
* So OCC=1 pays only at long context: -0.6 % at 128 to 2048, about break-even at 4096 (W7: +0.5 % vs base, roughly what
  staging alone gives), +1.5 % at ~17.6k.
  The decode graph is captured once with a fixed grid, so picking the kernel by context length is not a host-side switch.
* Next-round lead, not built: keep V packed in `splitkv_body` and unpack each element where it is used (8 fewer live VGPRs),
  to remove the spill at 8 waves. A sketch is in `raw/lr-splitkv.patch` (not built, no ISA check).

## Caveats and env vars

New env vars, all HIP-only. `SPLIT_MIN_US` and `SPLITKV_OCC` are in `ENV_KNOBS` (the admission fingerprint). `STAGE`
changes only host copies, and `STAMP` is diagnostics.

| variable | default | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `INTARSIA_HIP_STAGE` | staged | `0`: every copy blocking, as before; `up`: stage uploads only |
| `INTARSIA_HIP_SPLIT_MIN_US` | 950 | split a launch only above this estimate; `0` = round 8's rule |
| `INTARSIA_HIP_SPLITKV_OCC` | off | `1`: split-KV stage 1 as the 8-wave `splitkv_kernel_o8` (+1.5 % decode at ~17.6k, -0.6 % at 128 to 2048) |
| `INTARSIA_HIP_STAMP=<file>` | unset | diagnostics: wall-clock stamps around the long prefill dispatches, written at exit |

Caveats:
* `r9-st-pageable` survives (Mutations). On ROCm 7.2 it is equivalent; on a runtime that does not copy a small pageable
  source before `hipMemcpyAsync` returns, the ring is what keeps uploads correct.
* The stall's cause is still open, as is why layer 0's gate_up shows none. The ~10 ms left behind gate_up<64> per
  4408-token prompt is an estimate from the stamped runs (see the stall section's caveat).
* The 8-wave split-KV kernel (opt-in) spills 28 bytes per lane. That spill makes it 30 % slower per launch at short
  context (W8) while it is 12 % faster at ~17.6k (W5). A variant without the spill is the next-round lead.
* `generate`'s gain from staging (-5.5 ms per prompt) mostly comes from the decode-graph capture before the last prompt
  token overlapping the GPU. Serve captures once per process, so serve sees only the per-boundary and per-step part.

## Decisions for the user

1. **Lever 3 default** (`INTARSIA_HIP_SPLITKV_OCC`). It ships opt-in. The trade:
   * `=1` gives +1.5 % 35B decode at ~17.6k tokens and about break-even at 4096;
   * it costs -0.6 % at 128 to 2048 (W8);
   * the bits are identical either way.

   If long-context serving dominates radiance's traffic, set `=1`. The decode graph uses one fixed grid, so the kernel
   cannot be picked per context length on the host. The next-round fix is to remove the 28 B/lane spill (sketch in
   `raw/lr-splitkv.patch`). A second, independent sign of the long-context gain: 35B decode at 16384 vs base was +1.38 %
   with it on (W7) and -0.38 % with it off (W9).
2. **35B prefill at 4096 tokens:** -0.30 % (W9) and -0.22 % (W7). The ranges overlap, and lever 2 is equal at 4096 in
   `generate`. Accept it as noise, or ask for a dedicated per-lever serve A/B at 4096 next round (lever 1 off, lever 2 off).
3. **Prefix-reuse at `--prefill-chunk 1024`:** base FAILs it with the same numbers (see Accuracy). This is pre-existing,
   not new; it is a note for whoever owns that gate.
4. **`r9-st-pageable` survives** (see Mutations). It is equivalent on ROCm 7.2 only because `hipMemcpyAsync` from small
   pageable memory copies before it returns. Keep the page-locked ring, which relies only on documented behaviour.

No summation-order changes this round, so there is no opt-in KL decision.
