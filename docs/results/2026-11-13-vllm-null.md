# vllm-null: how much does vLLM's thinking length differ from a second vLLM run? (2026-11-13)

Lane `vllm-null`. Question from `docs/results/2026-11-13-think-len.md`: intarsiaml (soak arm Bc) thought 15 % longer than vLLM (V1) on 85 GSM8K questions (ratio of means 1.148, bootstrap 95 % CI 1.02 to 1.29), but vLLM does not reproduce itself (about 20 of 85 first-96-token prefixes agree). This lane reruns the vLLM arm twice (V2, V3) to get the vLLM-vs-vLLM spread, the null.

## Verdict

**The 15 % does not stand out from vLLM's own spread, but it is not zero either.**

- vLLM against itself: ratio of means 1.064 (V2/V1), 1.043 (V3/V1), 0.980 (V3/V2). The three run means are 1858, 1977 and 1937 tokens (6.4 % apart). All three null CIs include 1, so the run-to-run spread of the mean is roughly plus or minus 6 % at n=85.
- intarsiaml (Bc, mean 2133) against the three vLLM runs: 1.148, 1.079, 1.101; against the per-question mean of all three vLLM runs (mean 1924): **1.109 (bootstrap 95 % CI 0.992 to 1.232)**. Against V2 and V3 each, the CI includes 1 and the sign test is non-significant (46 longer of 85, p 0.52). Only the original V1 pair is significant (p 0.029), and V1 is the shortest of the three vLLM draws.
- So the original +15 % was inflated by V1 being a low draw. Against the vLLM mean the excess is about +11 % with a CI that just reaches 1; the median per-question picture is weaker still (geometric-mean ratio 1.033, longer on 45 and shorter on 40). Bc's mean is above all three vLLM runs, but only about 1.7 times the largest vLLM-vs-vLLM gap (1.064) and well inside the CIs. With four draws (three vLLM, one intarsiaml), the chance that the intarsiaml draw is the longest by luck alone is 25 %.
- The excess sits in the tail, as think-len found. Without the five V1 outlier questions, Bc / Vmean is 1.024. Four of the five V1 outliers remain in the top five against the vLLM mean (gsm8k-00075, -00045, -00002, -00035), and Bc is longer than V2, V3 and Vmean on all five. These questions are long for Bc (3570 to 6318 tokens) and mixed for vLLM (837 to 4492, varying 1.5 to 3 times between runs), so a tail effect on a few questions is not excluded, and n=85 cannot settle it. Tail counts: more than 2500 tokens, vLLM 17 / 21 / 16 against Bc 26; more than 4000, vLLM 2 / 5 / 4 against Bc 6.
- Conclusion for the soak: "+15 %" should be read as "about +8 to +15 % on these 85 questions, within about 1.7 times vLLM's own run-to-run spread, with a possibly heavier tail". It is not evidence of a numerical defect. More vLLM draws or a larger question set (a few hundred) would resolve it; the tail on the five questions is the thing to look at.

## Accuracy

Flexible accuracy: V1 0.965, V2 0.976, V3 0.988, Bc 0.988. All paired differences are within one question (McNemar exact p 0.5 to 1.0 for every pair). gsm8k-00012 is wrong on all four runs; V1 also misses 00037 and 00045, V2 misses 00045. No run truncated at 8192, all 85 finish with `stop`. Final-answer text identical to V1: V2 62, V3 61, Bc 53 of 85; bare-number style (localeval flag) V1 71, V2 65, V3 72, Bc 61, so vLLM's own bare-number count moves by 7 between runs while Bc's 61 sits 4 below the lowest vLLM run (the style difference in the soak doc is partly this noise and partly not; not analysed further here).

## What was run, and deviations

- V2 and V3 repeat the soak's V-think call: `localeval quality --thinking on --structured prompt --pin-gsm8k --max-tokens 8192` against spark2 production vLLM (`retro`, recipe identical to V-think), temperature 0, sequential (localeval sends one request at a time, as in the original arm). Script `scripts/vllm-null/run.sh` (sources `scripts/soak/lib.sh`), analysis `scripts/vllm-null/null.py`, output `raw/null-analysis.txt`.
- **n=85, not 100**: `--limit 85` gives the same first 85 ids that Bc ran (V1 is cut to its first 85 for the comparison). Wall per run 36.6 and 35.9 min instead of 40.8.
- Slot: one fleetq job, **#9256** (owner `claude-vllm-null`, `--host spark2 --load gpu --expect 90`), both runs inside it, exit 0, released. The original arm used `--quiet gpu,cpu` (#9246); this lane used `--load gpu` as instructed, so the box was shared. vLLM's request counters (`raw/vllm-foreign-traffic.txt`): V3 moved by exactly 85 requests; **V2 moved by 88**, so 3 requests that are not ours reached production vLLM during V2 (or localeval's idle probes; not distinguished). That adds a little queueing to V2 but does not change greedy token counts of ours beyond vLLM's usual batching noise; it is a caveat for V2's timing only. vLLM was not taken down.
- Both vLLM reruns are greedy, but vLLM is not deterministic across runs (batch composition changes numerics), which is exactly what the null measures. Concurrency 1 for ours, with other clients' requests possibly interleaved on the server (`--max-num-seqs 2`).
- Three vLLM draws give only three null ratios; the CIs, not the three point values, carry the weight. The intarsiaml side is a single deterministic draw (Bc), so the comparison cannot separate "engine effect" from "that one deterministic path happens to be long".
