# cpupin: is the post-chain MTP slowdown a CPU core-placement effect? (CUDA, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4, spark1)

Lane `cpupin`, branch `cpupin` = PR #436 (`mcdiag`, head `09894b5ce`) merged with origin/main (`a76bb040d`). Measured on spark1 (DGX Spark GB10) with vLLM up beside it
(fleetq `--quiet gpu,cpu` for every timing run, `--load cpu` for the two builds; CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=3, free-memory watchdog `scripts/cpupin/wd.sh` on every build and serve:
it never fired, available memory was 56-58 GB at every server start). Raw output: `docs/results/2026-11-18-cpupin/raw/`; scripts: `scripts/cpupin/`.
Nothing default-on, no engine code changed by this lane (the PR carries #436's opt-in measurement hooks, this lane's scripts, and the gates below).

**Result: core placement does not explain it. No knob was implemented.** Three answers:

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| (c) does the engine's launch thread migrate to a little core after the first chain? | **No.** In all 16 servers the `intarsia-engine` thread (the only busy thread, 46-48 s of CPU per server) ran on one X925 core for the whole process, before and after the first chain: 100 % of its ticks on X925 cores, 0 X925-to-A725 moves, free or pinned. The free scheduler never put it on a little core. |
| (b) is a pinned base faster than a free base (a general GB10 decode win)? | **No.** `baseP` (taskset to the X925 cores) / `base` (free) = 0.9972 in tok/s (158.78 vs 159.23, mean of 4 round medians), +0.057 ms per graph step. Free base already runs on an X925 core. mcdiag's "A725 pin is 0.15 ms slower" is the price of forcing a thread onto little cores, which the scheduler does not do by itself. |
| (a) does pinning to the X925 cores remove the post-chain gap? | **Not shown.** The point estimate is smaller pinned, but the gap is still there and the difference is inside the noise of 4 rounds (details below). Since the thread never moves, an engine-thread-only pin could not change anything (it already sits on an X925 core). |

Per the brief (pinning does not explain it) the chain-length sweep mcdiag named was run instead; its result is below, with one correction of the probe's own range.

## Topology (checked on spark1, not taken from mcdiag)

`lscpu`, `/sys/devices/system/cpu/cpuN/cpu_capacity`, MIDR: **X925 (MIDR `0x410fd851`, max 3900 MHz) = cpu 5-9 (capacity 997) and 15-19 (1017, cpu19 1024); A725
(`0x410fd871`, max 2808 MHz) = cpu 0-4 (718) and 10-14 (731).** mcdiag's pin to "5-9" used half of the X925 cores; the pin here is `5-9,15-19`.

## Test 1: core placement, real n20 chains, ABBA (`raw/pin1/`, `scripts/cpupin/ab.sh`)

Served `--max-batch 1 --spec mtp --spec-k 2`, code-gen 800 tokens, 8 reps per server after 2 warm-ups, `INTARSIA_SPEC_TRACE=1`, one server per arm and round, 4 rounds,
arm order reversed on even rounds. Arms: `base` (free), `baseP` (`taskset -c 5-9,15-19`), `n20` (`INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY=20`, free), `n20P` (n20 pinned). Greedy replies equal in
every arm (same sha per request, `raw/pin1/*.jsonl`). `scripts/cpupin/tsample.py` sampled every thread of the server every 100 ms (cpu, cpu ticks), on A725 cpu 11 (not the server's cores).
The n20 arms ran 35 chains each.

Decode tok/s, median of the 8 reps per round (`raw/pin1-analysis.txt`):

| arm | r1 | r2 | r3 | r4 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 160.2 | 160.0 | 158.4 | 158.4 | 159.23 |
| baseP | 159.1 | 159.0 | 158.0 | 159.1 | 158.78 |
| n20 | 157.1 | 155.5 | 157.8 | 156.2 | 156.63 |
| n20P | 157.8 | 159.9 | 156.8 | 157.3 | 157.94 |

Ratios per round and mean: **n20 / base 0.9808 0.9715 0.9965 0.9860, mean 0.9837** (mcdiag: 0.9873; flip bar 0.995); **n20P / baseP 0.9921 1.0057 0.9921 0.9889, mean 0.9947**;
baseP / base 0.9932 0.9932 0.9980 1.0044, mean 0.9972; n20P / base (free) 0.9919.

Graph-step offset of the n20 arm to the base arm of the same round, same request, same 50-position bin (`scripts/mcdiag/real_an.py`, `raw/pin1-vs-free-base.txt`), ms per step:

| arm (vs free base) | chain not yet seen | chain seen | seen minus not seen, per round r1..r4 | mean (SE) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| n20 | -0.062 (81 bins) | +0.233 (367) | +0.159 +0.311 +0.150 +0.235 | +0.214 (0.037) |
| n20P | -0.055 (81) | +0.093 (367) | +0.165 +0.056 +0.131 +0.180 | +0.133 (0.027) |
| baseP (no chain, all bins) | +0.060 (448) | | | |

Against the pinned base (`raw/pin1-pinned-vs-pinned-base.txt`): n20P not seen -0.035, seen +0.015, i.e. +0.05. So the gap is +0.21 free and +0.13 to +0.05 pinned depending on the
reference. The free-minus-pinned difference of the within-server contrast is 0.08 with a standard error of about 0.05 (1.8 SE); per round the pinned arm is positive in every round
(+0.17, +0.06, +0.13, +0.18). That is **a possible partial reduction, not a removal and not significant at 4 rounds**; the n20P / baseP ratio of 0.995 rests on one round (1.006).
Server-to-server variation is as large as the effect: round drift of the base arm is 17.46-17.69 ms per step, and which X925 core the thread landed on has no visible pattern
(cpu 5, 8, 9, 15, 16, 19 all give 17.46-17.69 ms).

### (c) thread residency (`raw/pin1-residency.txt`, `scripts/cpupin/tsan.py`)

Per server, the busiest thread (always `intarsia-engine`), its cpu ticks, and the share on X925 cores before / after the server's first `spec: copy pass`:

- all 8 free servers (4 base, 4 n20): 100 % on X925 before and after, 0.00 big-to-little moves per 10 s of busy time; each server kept ONE core for the whole run
  (cpu 15, 15, 15, 8 for base; 5, 5, 8, 15 for n20), the n20 servers with 1000 to 1600 ticks before their first chain and 3100 to 3800 after it;
- pinned servers: same, one core each (5, 19, 9, 16 / 15; n20P 16, 5, 19, 9);
- every other thread (http, bridge, `cuda-EvtHandlr`, watchd) used under 1 s of CPU per server and ran on whatever core (including A725); none shows a step at the first chain
  (`intarsia-http` in n20: 6-17 ticks before the first chain and 20-32 after, only because the later requests keep arriving).

The 100 ms sampling would miss an excursion shorter than 100 ms that returns to the same core; a persistent migration (what the hypothesis needs) would show as a changed cpu column.

## Test 2: chain-length sweep (`raw/sw1/`, `raw/sw2/`, `scripts/cpupin/serve2.sh`, analysis `serve2_an.py`)

One event per server: 2 warm-ups, 6 requests of 300 tokens (pre), one 800-token request in which a bogus chain (token id 1 repeated L times, so accepted 0, rolled back) is verified once
at position 450 (`INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY=256 INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY_PROBE=450,<L> INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY_PROBE_ONCE=1`), 10 requests of 300 tokens (post); 4 rounds, arms interleaved ABBA.
Carry-over = (post minus pre) of the arm's offset to the base arm of the same round, ms per graph step, mean over rounds (SE over rounds):

| probe | rows in the verify pass | per round r1..r4 | carry-over mean (SE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L3 | 4 | +0.012 -0.011 +0.120 -0.060 | +0.015 (0.038) |
| L5 | 6 | +0.086 -0.020 +0.082 -0.010 | +0.034 (0.029) |
| L7 (sw2, same pass size as a real n20 chain: 8 rows, 891 eager kernels) | 8 | -0.041 +0.016 (r3 void, see below) -0.018 | **-0.014 (0.017, 3 rounds)** |
| L8 (sw1) | 9 | | **void**: see below |

- **A single bogus chain leaves no measurable carry-over at any length, including the 8-row pass** (L7 -0.014 +-0.017 ms against +0.17 to +0.29 ms for real n20 chains in the same server
  process). So "a chain pass ran" is not by itself the trigger; something that a real chain does and a rejected bogus one does not (accepted rows kept, the head catch-up after
  accepted rows, several chains in a request) is. This narrows the trigger; it does not identify it.
- L7 round 3 is void: the L7 server's pre-chain requests already ran at 31 ms per step (normal 17.5) from the first request on, before any chain, and it recovered to 17.5 mid-post (the
  base server of that round, 2 minutes earlier, was normal). The cause is not identified; it looks like an outside load on the box (vLLM is up beside the runs), not the probe. It is kept in `raw/sw2/` and `raw/sw2-analysis.txt`; the table drops it.
- The L5 and L3 arms ran in sw1 (4 rounds); the L8 arm there is not a chain measurement. **Probe kind 8 asks for 9 rows and the CUDA verify path does not take 9 rows: the request
  ends early (452 of 800 tokens, a truncated reply, no `spec: copy pass` line, different sha to base in each round).** That is a limit of the measurement hook, not of the default
  path (presumably the 8-row limit of the row-exact verify kernels, not checked here; the copy knob's own chain is at most `len` 7 + 1 = 8 rows by default). The probe's `<what>` count should be capped at 7; the doc of
  `INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY_PROBE` in `docs/tuning.md` says "a bogus chain of that many tokens, default the knob's `len`", with no cap. Not changed here (the hook is #436's).
- Resolution: SE 0.02 to 0.04 per arm; L3, L5 and L7 are all within 2 SE of zero and not ordered by length (the L8 "+0.071" printed in `raw/sw1-analysis.txt` is the truncated request, ignore it).

## What is not known

- Why real chains leave +0.2 ms and a rejected bogus 8-row chain leaves none. Candidates not tested: accepted rows in the chain pass (state advance / catch-up after acceptance), the effect of
  several chains in one request, or the real chains' drafts being non-bogus tokens (different expert routing, so different MoE kernel work afterwards; the plan and graph are unchanged).
  A forcing probe that makes the pass accept (drafts taken from a base-greedy continuation) would separate acceptance from the pass itself.
- Whether a process-wide mask (taskset, which also confines the driver and helper threads) shrinks the gap by 0.08 ms or not; at 4 rounds it is 1.8 SE. It cannot be the engine
  thread's core (it never moves), so it would be a helper thread or the driver, and the helper threads use under 1 s of CPU. More rounds of `n20` vs `n20P` would settle the number; it would not
  give a fix, since n20P / baseP is still about 0.995 at best.
- The 100 ms sampler cannot see sub-100 ms excursions.
