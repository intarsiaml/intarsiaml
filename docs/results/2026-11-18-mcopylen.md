# mcopylen: adaptive copy-chain length (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4, Metal, endor)

Lane `mcopylen`, branch `mcopylen` from origin/mrows16 (PR #438 head), merged with origin/main `835796344` before the push. Opt-in only: `INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY=<n>,<len>,a` beside
`--spec mtp --spec-k 2`; off by default, nothing new is default-on. Measured on endor (Apple M5 Max, macOS 27.0), `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4`, tp 1, every run in a `fleetq` slot
(`--quiet gpu,cpu`). Raw output `docs/results/2026-11-18-mcopylen/raw/`, scripts `scripts/mcopylen/` (mrows16's, with the lane's paths).

## Verdict

- **The first design meets the win condition to within about 1 %; no second design was needed.** `20,15,a` against the fixed arms (4 ABBA rounds x 3 reps, medians of the per-round ratios):
  copy3k **0.990x of n20,15** (0.989 to 0.993 per round; 1.067x of n20,7), json-xform **0.990x of n20,7** (1.037x of n20,15), agent-edit 0.999x of n20,7, code-gen 1.003x of n20,7.
  So it keeps about 88 % of the len-15 gain on copy3k (+6.7 % over len 7, against +7.6 % for len 15) and none of the json-xform loss (len 15 is -4.3 % there, adaptive -1.0 %).
  The remaining gaps are small but they repeat with the same sign in rounds 2 to 4 (they are not larger than the round-to-round spread of the other cells, 0.5 to 1 %): I call them "within noise" only in that sense.
- **Round 1 is not a clean round for n20,7** (four of its cells ran at thermal level 2 -> 2, its copy3k read 319 tok/s against 358 to 366 in rounds 2 to 4); it is kept in every table and the per-round values show it. The medians are of the four per-round ratios, so it moves them little.
- Greedy ids equal `--spec off` in all 10 cells of the ids gate; knob unset: `plan show --json` byte-identical to origin/main; unit tests and the docs drift test pass. Default stays off; whether to recommend `20,15,a` for copy-heavy workloads is the user's call.

## The rule

`INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY=<n>,<len>,a` (third field `a` or `adapt`; no new env var, so cpu engine tests reach it through `EngineMeta::spec_copy`). Per request (`spec::CopyRun`, `Seq::copy_run`):

- A chain is at most `min(len, 7)` tokens (an 8-row verify pass, one launch on Metal) until the request has accepted **128 copied tokens in a row in whole chains** (`COPY_ADAPT_RUN`); then up to `len` (a 16-row pass at `len` 15).
- A chain that accepted fewer drafts than it proposed, or a step that proposed no chain (the copy ended, or the back-off window), restarts the count: the next chain is short again and the 128 tokens must be earned anew.
- Never above `len`, so the verify rows and the recurrent save buffers (sized for `len`) are unchanged; the existing consecutive-miss back-off (`CopyBackoff`) is untouched and counts misses the same way in both modes.
- Without the third field the cap is `len` and nothing changes (trace line, plan and behaviour as before). With it, the `INTARSIA_SPEC_TRACE` line of a chain gets a ` cap N` field after `emits N` (before `drafts`, so `ids.sh`'s parse still works).

Why this rule, from the data rather than from the brief's "cap at accepted + 1":
1. **Cost is close to linear in rows on Metal.** copy3k accepts about 99 % at both lengths: 16 tokens per pass at len 15 (381 tok/s -> about 42 ms per pass) against 8 per pass at len 7 (355 tok/s -> about 22.5 ms): a 16-row pass costs about 1.9x an 8-row one, so the extra 8 rows gain about 7 % per accepted token (the fixed per-pass cost) and a chain that misses early wastes 8 more rows than a short one (about 90 % of a short pass).
2. **json-xform's misses are isolated and periodic, and a miss is usually followed by a full chain** (mrows16's trace at len 15: `... 15/15 15/15 2/15 15/15 15/15 ...`, a miss every 70 to 110 tokens, the longest run of whole chains 75 tokens). A reactive "shrink to accepted + 1 after a miss" would shrink exactly the chains that would have been full and pay several passes to grow back, while the miss itself already cost 16 rows before the rule could react. The only thing that separates it from copy3k (miss 1 chain in 180, a run of 1665 tokens) is the length of the clean run, so the rule gates the long length on a long clean run.
3. **No intermediate lengths:** 9 to 14-token chains pay the second launch (`row_groups`, 2 launches from 9 rows) for less gain; the rule jumps between 7 and `len`.
4. The threshold 128 (8 whole 16-token chains, about 14 whole 8-token chains) is above json-xform's longest run (75) and below copy3k's typical one; **it is tuned to json-xform's record period** and a workload with misses every 130 tokens would pay the len-15 loss. A smaller value (64) was not tried (it would sit inside json-xform's runs).

The simple per-request design was kept as it met the criterion; the plan (single design) is also cheaper than a learned one. The one thing it loses is the first 128 tokens of every copy at the short length (copy3k: 38 short chains of 7 out of 199, in two stretches: tokens 20 to 172 at the start, and tokens 1117 to 1269 after the one mid-run miss at token 1084, a chain that accepted 8 of 15; the run ended at token 2901 with a chain that accepted 2).

## Gate (c): speed, `serve --max-batch 1 --max-seq 8192 --spec mtp --spec-k 2`

Method as mrows16 (`scripts/mcopylen/ab.sh`, `run-ab.sh`): one fresh server per arm and round, 4 rounds each in its own fleetq slot, the 4 arms in reverse order on even rounds (ABBA across rounds: base / n20L7 / n20L15 / n20L15a, then the reverse), 3 reps per cell, greedy streaming, per-round nonce, decode tok/s = (completion tokens - 1) / time after the first token, a warm-up request per server, cooling to thermal level 0 (at most 120 s) before every server, `notifyutil` level before / after every cell in `raw/ab-run-r*.log`. Arms: `base` (knob unset), `n20L7` (`INTARSIA_SPEC_COPY=20,7`), `n20L15` (`20,15`), `n20L15a` (`20,15,a`). Cell value = median tok/s over all reps of all rounds [min-max]; ratio = median over rounds of the round's median ratio (per-round values for rounds 1 to 4 in brackets). Replies have the same sha in every arm, round and rep (`tables.py` prints no `IDS DIFFER`).
Round 2 and later waited about 2.5 hours in the fleetq queue behind another lane's job; rounds 1 and 2 are 2.5 hours apart, which does not matter to the within-round ratios.

## Not done / unverified

- Only the three copy-heavy shapes the cells cover: copy3k (one long copy), json-xform (records), agent-edit (308-token reply, few chains). A workload with a miss every 100 to 160 tokens would sit just above the 128-token threshold and could pay the len-15 loss on part of its chains; 128 is tuned to json-xform and was not swept (no second design was needed).
- The cpu engine test can only reach `len` <= 7 (the tiny test model's verify pass takes at most 8 rows), where the adaptive cap is `len` itself; the switch between 7 and `len` is covered by the `CopyRun` unit test and, end to end, by the Metal ids gate (cap 7 and cap 15 chains in copy3k), not by a cpu engine test.
- CUDA and the Sparks were not run (the 9..=16-row verify exists on CUDA already; the cost model above is Metal's, and CUDA's gain at len 15 was +18 % with different misses cost, so the threshold is not claimed for it). Sampled requests, thinking mode, `--max-batch` above 1, tp > 1, 16k-context speed, real agent transcripts and `len` above 15 are as in mrows16: not covered.
- The A/B and the first ids gate ran before the merge with origin/main; the merge brought in the mcdiag probe (which reads the chain length the rule sets) and unrelated lanes. After the merge: tests, plan gate and a repeat of the ids gate (5 of 5, `raw/ids-gate-merged.txt`) were re-run; the speed A/B was not.
- The A/B ran with endor's standing services up (kev, tensorfold) and other lanes' CPU jobs beside the unit-test job, not beside an A/B or ids run (`--quiet gpu,cpu`).
- Commits are signed `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5` as the lane rules ask.
