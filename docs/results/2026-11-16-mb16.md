## 1. Decode vs prefill: the mechanism (existing, reused)

No new mechanism. The registry already separates the two kinds of call:

* A batched decode step (and a speculative verify pass, and the MTP head catch-up) runs with `Model::exact_rows` set, so every
  call it makes carries `OpCall::exact_rows` (`Model::exec_call_one`). Prefill never sets it.
* `Registry::select` (crates/intarsia-core/src/registry.rs): when an `exact_rows` call's op has at least one `rows_exact`
  registration, the call may take only `rows_exact` kernels; an ordinary call never takes one. fp8 / nvfp4 / bf16 / MoE already
  have their `_rows` twins this way.
* `plan::check_batch_ops` accepts a batched step's kernel if it is `rows_exact` (or the solo step's own kernel).

MXFP4 had no `rows_exact` registration, so an `exact_rows` MatmulMxfp4 call fell back to the ordinary choice: the GEMV at
M <= 8 (the solo step's own kernel, whose M-row shaders are bitwise the `_m1` shader per row by design, tests/mxfp4_gemv.rs) and `matmul_mxfp4_nax`
(priority 5) at M > 8, which the plan check refuses ("a 9-row batched step runs matmul_mxfp4 on matmul_mxfp4_nax, which is not a
row-exact kernel ...").

Change: `matmul_mxfp4_gemv_rows` (mxfp4.rs, kernels.rs, lib.rs), `rows_exact`, M = 2..=16 (`mxfp4::rows_ok`: the GEMV's
predicate, no W4A8), priority -1, the GEMV's scratch. It runs the same launcher as `matmul_mxfp4_gemv` (same M-row shaders
`mr_auto` picks), except that it never takes the MMA shaders (`INTARSIA_METAL_MXFP4_MR=8`, bitwise only on some archs: forced to
auto), and refuses M > 8 per launch. Added to `row_chunks`, so 9..=16 rows run as two launches of `ceil(m/2)` / `floor(m/2)` rows.
Prefill (no `exact_rows`) is untouched: `matmul_mxfp4_nax` / naxd as before.

Every `exact_rows` MatmulMxfp4 call now takes the twin. Who issues them on the 27B:

| pass | M | before | after | bits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| batched decode, `--max-batch` N | 2..=N | GEMV (N <= 8); N > 8 refused at load | `matmul_mxfp4_gemv_rows` | same at 2..=8 (same shaders); 9..=16 new |
| MTP verify (default engine, k2) | 2 / 3 | GEMV | `_rows` (same launcher and shaders) | same (gate: `mtp` ids) |
| MTP head catch-up (`head_rows_exact`, off by default) | 2..=8 | GEMV | `_rows` | same |
| DFlash2 / DSpine verify (`--spec dflash`) | up to 16 | GEMV <= 8, NAX 9..=16 | `_rows` | 9..=16 now bitwise the solo GEMV (was NAX); speed not measured (no 27B draft on endor) |
| batched verify (lane mx6, opt-in) | totals up to 16 | as above | `_rows`; totals > 16 already had no fp8 head twin | - |

`Model::VERIFY_MAX_ROWS` = 16 and every verify / batch pass has at least 2 rows, so the 2..=16 predicate leaves no `NoKernel`.

(6816 / 7801 / 10731 bytes differ at M = 9 / 12 / 16). The rows test's f32-output cases are its sharp ones.

### 3.1 Why: the 27B's MXFP4 GEMV is compute-bound from 4 rows

A batched step's decode iteration is 42 ms at 4 rows, 83 ms at 8 and 162 ms at 16: the cost per row is flat from M = 4 up, so
the 16-row step (two 8-row chunks) is two 8-row steps, and the only gain left is the scheduler's (fewer prefill interruptions,
no queue). `timing_mxfp4_rows` (#9807, `raw/timing-mxfp4-rows.log`; one 27B layer's six W4A16 projections, one call each):

| M | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 (5+4) | 10 | 11 | 12 (6+6) | 13 | 14 | 15 | 16 (8+8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| us | 362 | 385 | 406 | 477 | 877 | 1019 | 1161 | 1046 | 1313 | 1706 | 1850 | 1991 | 2136 | 2283 | 2164 | 2058 |
| us / row | 362 | 192 | 135 | **119** | 175 | 170 | 166 | 131 | 146 | 171 | 168 | 166 | 164 | 163 | 144 | 129 |

* M = 4 is the cheapest row (the P2 M-row shader); M = 5..7 run the `_m8` shader (`mr_auto` keeps it there) at 166-175 us per row.
  `row_chunks` halves 9..=16 rows, so 10..14 rows land on 5..7-row chunks: that is the s9 / s12 regression.
* Even at the best chunking (4-row launches, 119 us per row) a 16-row step costs as much per row as main's 8 (131 us, or 119
  if 8 were also split 4 + 4), so no chunk plan gets 16 streams past +5 %.

## 5. Verdict

**Findings only; no engine change ships, no default changed.** A row-exact MXFP4 twin makes `serve --max-batch 16` load and run
bitwise (every stream of every cell equals its solo `generate`), but 16 streams gain +2.4 % aggregate over main's `--max-batch 8`
(stop bar 5 %), and 9 / 12 streams lose 5.7 % / 20 %. The reason is structural: from 4 rows up the 27B's MXFP4 GEMV costs the same
per row (119-131 us per layer at its best chunks), so a 16-row step is two 8-row steps.

The kernel, its tests and the timing test are on branch `mb16-kernel` (pushed, not in the PR): `d80df13d0` (kernel + tests),
`b8787a1bb` (`timing_mxfp4_rows`). The PR carries this doc, the raw data, `scripts/mb16/` and the LOG row. If it ever ships, note:
it is unconditional (every `exact_rows` MXFP4 call takes it), so DFlash2 / DSpine verify passes of 9..=16 rows on the 27B move
from NAX to chunked GEMVs (bitwise the solo decode then, speed unmeasured: no 27B draft on endor), and MTP verify (M = 2 / 3)
changes kernel name only (same shaders); that `mtp` ids gate (`scripts/mb16/gates.sh mtp`) was not run.

Defaults: none touched. Batching stays opt-in (`--max-batch` unset = 1, the 27B's MTP engine); there is no 27B deployment on
endor with a `--max-batch` setting; the recipe's `max_batch = 32` and Metal's `batch_row_cap` 16 are unchanged.

## 6. Follow-ups

1. **MXFP4 chunk planner** (bitwise, any chunking is): split row-exact MXFP4 calls into 4-row launches (plus a 2 / 3 tail) instead of
   halves; from the timing table, M = 6 / 7 / 8 would cost 862 / 883 / 954 us instead of 1019 / 1161 / 1046 (-15 / -24 / -9 %), which
   helps main's own `--max-batch 8` steps of 5..8 rows and removes the 9..15-row regression here. It does not lift 16 streams past 5 %.
2. **A cheaper row-exact multi-row kernel at M >= 4** is the only route to a real gain at 16 streams (the M-row shaders are
   compute-bound there; the MMA form `INTARSIA_METAL_MXFP4_MR=8` is bitwise on the M5 Max but 11-18x slower).
3. The 16-stream TTFT result (p50 24.0 s -> 1.06 s at equal aggregate) is a latency argument for `--max-batch 16` under heavy load
   that the throughput stop rule does not weigh: the coordinator's call.
4. Lesson (section 0.3): `cargo test --workspace` rebuilds `target/release/intarsia` without `--features metal`; benches should run
   a copied binary.
